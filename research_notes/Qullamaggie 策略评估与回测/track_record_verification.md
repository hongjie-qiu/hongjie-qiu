### Inferences
- **The percentage series cannot be compounded into the dollar series.** Chaining the journal's annual returns from the $43,000 January-2014 balance (×16.36 ×2.10 ×3.87 ×11.04 ×1.96) would give roughly $124M by end-2018, whereas the claimed account was about $1.4M in 2018 and $3.5M in January 2020, and cumulative taxed profit for 2014–2018 was only about $3.8M. The percentages therefore describe returns on a small, constantly withdrawn account base (or on some other denominator). A reader should treat "+1,004% in 2017" as a statement about a small account, not about wealth creation. (My arithmetic on third-party-compiled figures.)
- **The dollar series and the tax series do reconcile.** Taxed profit 2014–2019 sums to about $4.97M, close to the "about $5.5M by the end of 2019" in one account analysis if that figure is cumulative profit; after 30% tax it is about $3.5M, matching the claimed $3.5M account in January 2020 (living costs and unrealized gains ignored). The 2020 equity change ($3.5M → $36M, +$32.5M) matches the 2020 taxed profit of about $33M. (My arithmetic.)
- **Currency cross-check of the press figures.** SEK 305M at roughly 9.2 SEK/USD (2020 average) is about $33M, and SEK 360M at roughly 8.6 SEK/USD (2021 average) is about $42M — the same as the Deep Dive site's dollar figures. (Exchange rates from memory, not sourced in this session.) $80M × 30% = $24M also matches the site's "total tax paid".
- **Concentration in time:** about $75M of the roughly $80M taxed in 2014–2021, or about 94%, was earned in 2020–2021.
- **The $105M peak is mark-to-market, pre-tax and partly leveraged.** Cumulative realized pre-tax profit through 2021 was about $80M and after-tax about $56M, so a $105M equity print in November 2021 necessarily included large unrealized gains and an unpaid 2021 tax liability (about $12–13M at 30%).

## 3. Verification: what hard evidence exists, and how strong is it?

### Takeaway
The strongest outside evidence is Swedish tax data: he was named by a news site as Sweden's 15th-highest income earner for 2021 (SEK 360M, "day trader"), and tax-derived figures put his realized profit at about $33M in 2020 and $42M in 2021 with a $26M loss in 2022. ## 4. 2022–2026: the bear market and after

### Takeaway

## 7. Context for judging a single spectacular track record

### Takeaway
Almost all of his documented dollar profit came from two years (2020–2021) of concentrated, margined, predominantly long momentum trading in the 2020–2021 bull market, and the same exposure was followed by a fall of about 57% in account equity over the next eight months. His own published view is that large drawdowns are a built-in feature, and nothing in the record shows the method earning comparable returns in a different regime.

### Inferences
- About 94% of the realized profit taxed in 2014–2021 was earned in 2020–2021 (my arithmetic on the tax series). Before 2020 he was a consistently profitable small-account trader making roughly $0.3M–$1.5M a year; that pre-2020 record, not the $100M headline, is the better guide to what the method yields in ordinary conditions with a few million dollars.
- Gross long exposure of $150M on $105M of equity is about 1.43× leverage, in what his setups select for (fast-moving momentum stocks); a 40% decline on that exposure is a $60M loss, i.e. 57% of equity, which is the size of the fall he reports (illustrative arithmetic; the actual path involved many trades, plus possibly withdrawals). The tight per-trade stops limited single-trade risk but not portfolio risk when many correlated positions gapped or were re-entered repeatedly.
- The record is one path through one extraordinary regime (2020–2021), selected after the fact from the thousands of people who traded leveraged momentum then. The tax data show the money was real; they cannot show how much was skill, and the 2022–2024 results (a large loss, then "moderate" years) are what a strong regime dependence would look like.
- For a newcomer the replicable parts are the process elements (small fixed risk per trade, selling into strength, a market filter); the non-replicable parts are the 2020–2021 opportunity set, the willingness to run 10%–25% positions on margin, and the years of losses he absorbed first.
