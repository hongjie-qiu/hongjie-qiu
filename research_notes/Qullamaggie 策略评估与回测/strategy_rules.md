## 1. BREAKOUT setup — prior move, consolidation, entry, stop, partial, trail

### Takeaway
A stock in the top 1–2% of 1/3/6-month gainers makes a 30–100%+ leg in days-to-weeks, then consolidates 2 weeks–2 months in an orderly, tightening range with higher lows while "surfing" the rising 10/20-day (sometimes 50-day) SMA; he buys the range break at the opening-range high (first 1-, 5- or "60-minute" = 09:30–10:00 candle) with the stop at the low of the day, provided the stop is no wider than 1× ADR/ATR (he prefers ⅓–½ ATR). He sells ⅓–½ after 3–5 days, moves the stop to break-even and exits the rest on the first *close* below the 10-day (fast stocks/beginners) or 20-day (slower, larger stocks) moving average.

### Cited Findings

**Pattern definition (his words)**
- [A-blog] "If you study thousands of the biggest winning stocks over the past 100 years they tend to move in stair steps. Meaning they will make a 20-50%+ move, pull back and go sideways for a while, then make another move." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-blog] Three steps, verbatim: "A big move higher sometime in the past 1-3 months. This move can be anywhere from 30-100%+ and usually lasts a few days to a few weeks." / "An orderly pullback and consolidation with higher lows and tightening range in the consolidation phase." / "A range expansion (breakout) out of that consolidation. The consolidation phase is usually 2 weeks to 2 months. During the consolidation the stock price “surfs” the rising 10- and 20-day. and sometimes toe [sic: the] 50-day moving average." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-stream] Leadership requirement: "it doesn't matter if this is a one month move or a three month move or a six month move ... but it has to be in the top one or two percent of the strongest stocks in that time frame." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] Which average: "you have a moving average ... the simple 10 SMA or the 20 SMA or the 50. The best ones are the 10 and 20 ... then it starts getting tighter and tighter, goes sideways, starts building higher lows ... and then they break out of this tight range." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] MA hierarchy: "the strongest ones find support on the 10-day, the strong ones the 20-day and the ... slower ones on the 50-day generally"; "these are all the only technical indicators you need: the 10-day, the 20-day and the 50-day moving averages." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM)
- [A-stream] SMA vs EMA: "on the daily chart I use the simple moving averages; on the 60 minute chart I use exponential moving averages. Why? Because they work." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI). The 60-minute averages he names are the 10 and 20 EMA ([Stream 2020-12-22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wLYBzP4qRjY)) and the 65 EMA ("it's just surfing the 65 EMA on the 60 minute chart; on the daily chart it's [the] 10-day", [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)).
- [A-stream] Price must be *near* the averages, not extended: "it needs to be on the 10 or 20 or the 50 day. This is like way above all of this; it's 20 percent above the 10 day, it's 33 above the 20 day ... it's up in the stratosphere right now." — [Stream 2020-08-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdXxuBIFTZ0)
- [A-stream] A "five-star" example in his words: "it had a big move off the lows, went up sixty percent, pulled back, found support on these 10 and 20 day moving averages and now it's been going sideways for the past two weeks and building higher lows ... and now it had a range break here and it's a hot sector." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM)
- [A-stream] Relative strength: "the best ones are the ones that show relative strength, like the ones that ... can't go lower even if ... the indices overall are showing weakness." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] "Hot sector" = "when several stocks in a sector ... have gone up 500 to 1000 plus percent in the past three, four, five months ... you want to be in the sectors where a lot of stocks are making big moves and breaking higher." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM)
- [A-stream] Long-term trend filters are loose: "I rarely buy stocks below the 50 day, or I mean, depends." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI). Asked why he does not disqualify stocks below the 200-day MA (unlike O'Neil/Minervini): "doesn't matter much" (author reply, 2021-04-17) — [Blog comments, "3 TIMELESS setups"](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-blog] Pattern family: "this setups also works on the weekly chart and the intraday (1- and 5-minute) charts. On the intraday timeframe this setup is called Holy Grail by some on the daily it can be called a hight tight flags sometimes. It’s pretty much flat channels, symmertrical and descending triangles." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

**Entry trigger**
- [A-blog] "Enter on the opening range highs. The opening range highs can be the highs of the first 1-minute candle, the 5-minute candle or the 60-minute candle (on the 60-minute timeframe the first candle is always only 30 minutes, 9.30-10 AM). You can use whatever timeframe or a combination of them. You don’t even have to use any intraday chart, just look at the daily chart and enter when the stock is starting to break out." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-blog] "This method can also be traded by anticipating the breakout but I don’t find it as effective and more skill and experience is needed." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-stream] Trade-off between timeframes: "you usually want to get in as early as possible so you want to use the one and five, but sometimes the fail rate on the one and five minute candles are a bit higher than on the 60 minutes, so sometimes you may get stopped out, then you have to re-buy higher." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] Do-not-chase filter (intraday extension): "I usually don't buy the stock if it's up more on the day than its average true range; preferably [I] want to get in when the stock is only up ... a third or half or two thirds of its average true range." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM); restated: "if the price change on the day is more than the average true range I'm not gonna do the trade." — [Stream 2020-11-25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6UxqgsEMquQ)
- [A-stream] Gap-ups are unwanted for breakouts: "if you want to go long you do not generally want gap ups unless it's an episodic pivot, then the gap up is the criteria for the setup." — [Stream 2021-02-08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fz3OIpJ8XrU)
- [A-stream] Close-based alternative: "it doesn't even have to take out the highs of the day; if it closes really strong ... just a few pennies below the highs of the day, that could also be an entry, and then you use the [lows] of the day as your stop." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] No scaling in: "you don't need to scale in; you can buy everything at once ... but you need to scale out." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI)
- [A-blog] Missed the breakout by a day? "Sometimes I buy the retrace" (author reply 2021-02-24). Orders: "I use market orders mostly. Limit orders are for people who want to miss a good breakout" (author reply 2021-02-09). — [Blog comments, "3 TIMELESS setups"](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-stream] "95 percent of my trades [are] market; usually when I sell partials I use a limit order, but when I buy a position or get stopped out I always use market orders." — [Stream 2020-07-10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XFjjt4Cm16I); "I use buy stops all the time." — [Stream 2020-10-08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zodqKgRJaRQ)
- [A-stream] Earnings proximity: "I only hold through earnings if I have a profit cushion." — [Stream 2020-08-06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2f1nX_TGelQ); "if you just bought a stock and it hasn't gone yet and there's an earnings date coming, you shouldn't hold it over earnings; it's just pure gambling." — [Stream 2020-09-16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhSrnZXH8s4)
- [A-stream] Breakout-day volume is not a hard pre-condition: "most of the time you can't ... determine early in the day that it's going to have higher volume ... liquid mid and large cap stocks, they always have volume, you don't need to worry about it." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI)

**Initial stop**
- [A-blog] "Stop is always lows of the day and stop should not be wider than the ATR or ADR of the stock, to prevent the risk/reward mechanics getting out of whack. So if the ADR of the stock is 5%, your stop shouldn’t be wider than 5%. If the ATR if [sic] 50c, then stop should not be wider than 50c." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-stream] "the stop on all of them, this specific method, is at the lows of the day, always at the lows of the day." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] Frequency and exceptions: "about 95% of the time I use the [lows] of the day as my stop when I buy a long swing, and if I short it's usually the [highs] of the day"; exception that day: "I'm using the lows of this previous 60 minute candle instead because the stop would be too wide otherwise." — [Stream 2020-09-16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IhSrnZXH8s4). Another exception (gap-down open): "usually I use the low of the day as my stop, but since this thing ... gapped down below previous day's lows I used ... red to green ... that's my stop." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM)
- [A-stream] Width cap: "one of my rules is that the stop shouldn't be higher [wider] than the average true range." — [Stream 2020-08-10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=438_cyYQ888)
- [A-stream] Typical width: "my stops are usually ... around half of average true range; they are rarely a full average true range. I always feel like I'm chasing when ... the stock is closing in on the full average true range." — [Stream 2020-08-06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2f1nX_TGelQ); "I prefer when my stops are ... a third or half of the average true range" and an almost-full-ATR stop "is acceptable." — [Stream 2021-01-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=46Dw3UTmKlA)
- [A-stream] Why tight: "my stop ... is less than one quarter of the stock's average range ... it's all about tight stops; this is how you get 20, 30, 50 risk to reward on trades." — [Stream 2020-11-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sgTXnAr2UXs)
- [A-stream] No fixed-percent stops: "you can't set the fixed three percent; you have to look at the chart, where is the logical exit; I don't believe in these fixed percentage stops ... different stocks have different volatility." — [Stream 2021-01-13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0udk9grGeqE)
- [A-blog] Order type: "Do you use mental or hard stops? Both. When a stock is close to my stop I use hard stops so I don’t have to watch it constantly. I always use market stops, never limit stops." Overrides: "Very rarely, but sometimes yes. If you made less than a few million in the markets you should never do that." — [FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq/)
- [A-stream] Gap through the stop: "if it gaps down below my stop you gotta get out; preferably you get out in after-hours/pre-market, but if it's too thin ... you've got to get out at the open." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI)
- [A-stream] Losers resolve fast: "usually I get stopped out the same day or the next few days and that's the best way to get stopped out as a swing and position trader." — [Stream 2020-11-19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Yz7MMNqxNgY)

**Partial sale, break-even, trailing exit**
- [A-blog] "You should sell 1/3 to 1/2 of the position after 3-5 days, and then move the stop to break even. The rest of the position should be trailed with the 10- or the 20-day moving average. Depends on how fast the stock is. If a beginner stick to the 10-day. You wait for the first CLOSE below the 10- day." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-stream] "after three to five days you sell half, move your stop to break even and just keep trail on the 10-day moving average"; "if it closes below the 10-day you sell it." — [Stream 2020-08-03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X9vP9hJY6qM)
- [A-stream] Day counting and variants (stock bought Wednesday): "you sell a third on day three, which means on Friday you would sell a third, and then you sell a third on day five, like on Tuesday ... and then you trail the rest with the 10-day moving average; or ... you sell half on day three, four or five and then you ... trail the second half. You can play around with it." (So entry day = day 1.) — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI)
- [A-stream] Fraction is loose: "you sell half or a third or two thirds or whatever you feel like, but I would say around half after the first three to five days, move your stop to break even and then you just trail on the 10-day moving average. ### Inferences
- A faithful mechanical baseline (all parameters traceable to the quotes above): universe = top 1–2% (his own scans: top ~7%) by 1/3/6-month gain with ADR and dollar-volume floors (section 4); prior leg ≥30% within ≤ ~3 months; consolidation 10–40 trading days with rising lows and contracting range, price within a few % of rising 10/20 SMA; trigger = break of the consolidation high via 1-/5-/30-minute opening-range high; reject if (entry − LOD) > 1× ATR(20) or day's gain already > 1× ATR; stop = LOD (market stop); sell ⅓–½ on day 3–5; stop → entry price; exit remainder on first daily close < SMA10 (ADR ≳ 5–7%) or SMA20 (slower names).
- Because the stop is the day's low and must be ≤ 1× ADR (typically ⅓–½), realised risk per share is small relative to price (often 1–4%); this is what makes 10–50R outcomes arithmetically possible and why the position-size cap (10–25% of equity), not the risk %, frequently binds (see section 5).
- With daily bars only, the entry can be approximated by "buy when price exceeds the consolidation high" (he explicitly allows this), but the LOD stop then needs intraday data (the low *before* entry vs. the final daily low differ); a daily-bar backtest that uses the full-day low as the stop will under-count same-day stop-outs.
- Two exit schemes are documented in his own words and both deserve a test: (A) the taught rule — time-based partial (⅓–½ on day 3–5), break-even stop, all-out on the first close below one average (10- or 20-day); (B) his Oct-2019 personal rule — sell 15–25% into a +10–20% move, then ¼–⅓ of the remainder on the first close below the 10-day, another ¼–⅓ below the 20-day, the rest below the 50-day. (B) keeps a tail position much longer and will change the R-multiple distribution.
- The "3–5 days" partial is time-based, not target-based; he gives no rule for a position that is flat or slightly negative on day 3–5 (in his examples such trades have already stopped out or are simply held to the stop).

### Gaps
- No numeric definition of "orderly", "tight", "higher lows", "linear" or of the breakout level itself (range high vs. trendline); he says this is learned from studying thousands of charts (section 10).
- No stated minimum volume multiple on the breakout day; no explicit rule on market-cap/price floors for this setup (section 4).
- Whether the 3–5-day partial is taken regardless of profit is not stated.
- I could not open the "mandatory" breakout video linked from the blog ([youtube.com/watch?v=xx8GvtAxilk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xx8GvtAxilk), hosted on a moderator's channel; he elsewhere mentions "a whole video four hours long where I talk about breakout setups", [Stream 2021-02-24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E1ah3mwoV40)) or the on-screen "instructions" he keeps referring to; the rules above come from the blog and the "Swing Trading School" streams of Aug 2020.

## 2. EPISODIC PIVOT (EP) setup — gap, volume, catalyst, prior chart, entry, stop, holding; difference from Bonde

### Takeaway
His EP = a gap up of ≥10% on surprising news (mostly earnings/guidance with large growth and a big beat) with massive early volume (ideally a full average day's volume in the first 15–30 minutes), preferably in a stock that has gone sideways/down for 3–6+ months; buy the opening-range high (1-min, add on 5-min, or 5/60-min if late), stop at the low of the day (≤1×, max 1.5× ADR/ATR), then manage like a breakout (trail 10/20-day; blog also allows 20/50-day). He credits Pradeep Bonde for the setup but his version is stricter on gap size and timing: Bonde's own scans are 4–8% close-to-close moves on 3×+ volume, entered pre-market/at the open with fixed-target exits.

### Cited Findings

**Definition and criteria (his words)**
- [A-blog] "When unexpected good news hits a stock, especially if that stock is neglected, that can trigger multi-month, and multi-year moves." Types listed: "Earnings and earnings guidance; Government regulations, political stuff; Biotech related like drug trial results or FDA decisions; Macro and political related …and many more! The main ones I focus on are earnings and earnings guidance." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-blog] "An EP needs to be a gap of 10%+ and big volume should be there, if not in pre-market then the first 5-10 minutes after the open." — [Blog "3 TIMELESS setups", 2021-01-08](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)
- [A-blog] Step list, verbatim: "Gap up 10%+" / "Big volume. If the volume is not there in premarket, it needs to come in at the open. Many times the best ones have traded their average daily volume in the first 15-30 minutes after the open." / "If an earnings or earnings guidance related EP, there has to be big growth numbers, preferably mid/high or even triple digit EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat to analyst expectations. Many times the smaller stocks don’t have analyst coverage, you just have to trust the numbers and volume." / "It’s best if the stock has not rallied over the past 3-6 months. If the stock has already made a big, multi month move into the gap-up, is it really a surprise to the market? ### Inferences
- Differences between the two EPs (from the quotes above): (1) trigger size — Kullamägi a ≥10% *opening gap*; Bonde a ≥4–8% *close-to-close* move; (2) volume test — Kullamägi intraday (ADV traded in the first 15–30 min, or ≥20–25% of ADV in the first minutes); Bonde a daily multiple (3×–10× average); (3) entry — Kullamägi after the open on the ORH break with LOD stop; Bonde pre-market/at the open with a multi-day-low or EP-day-low stop; (4) exit — Kullamägi partial after 3–5 days then MA trail (no target); Bonde four tranches with 20%+ targets. Shared core: surprise catalyst, growth, "neglect".
- For coding: `gap_pct = open/prev_close − 1 ≥ 0.10`; catalyst flag (earnings date ±1 day is the only catalyst that can be coded cheaply); first-N-minutes volume vs. 20-day ADV; neglect proxy = 3–6-month return below some threshold or price inside a multi-month range; entry = 1- or 5-min ORH; stop = LOD with `(entry − LOD) ≤ 1–1.5× ADR`; exits as in section 1. The fundamentals screen (YoY EPS/sales growth "mid/high double to triple digit", size of beat, guidance raise) needs point-in-time estimates data.
- His scan floor (7.5% up, above the prior high) is a candidate list, not the definition; the definition he writes down is the 10% gap. A backtest should treat 7–10% as a tunable parameter.

### Gaps
- No numeric thresholds for "big beat" or "big guidance", for how "neglected" the prior 3–6 months must be, or for minimum pre-market volume.
- "Delayed / secondary EP" entries: I found no rule in his own writing beyond "No need to be first in" / "add through the day" and passing recap remarks (e.g. a recent EP "could have a secondary leg higher", [recap 2021-10-13](https://fewmoredays.io/387-uranium-names-breaking-out-yesterdays-stream-oct-13-2021/)). The Stockbee taxonomy ("delayed reaction EP" etc.) was not retrievable in this session.
- Bonde's posts were read only through the UCT dossier's quotations (stockbee.blogspot.com not reachable here); verify before publishing.
- The 2023 stream statements come from a third-party reconstruction; the underlying videos were not accessible.

## 3. PARABOLIC SHORT (and parabolic long) — qualifying move, entry triggers, stop, targets

### Takeaway
Short only after a vertical, multi-day run (larger caps +50–100%+ in days/weeks; small caps +300–1000%+; ideally 3–5+ consecutive up days), and only after intraday weakness appears: break of the 1-/5-minute opening-range low, the first red 5-minute candle if it runs from the open, or a failed bounce into VWAP; stop at the high of the day (or a VWAP reclaim), cover toward the 10/20-day moving averages. It is his highest-win-rate but lowest-payoff setup (≈5–10R), learned from Tim Sykes's "supernova", and he repeatedly tells followers not to trade it. The mirror "parabolic long" buys the first green 5-minute candle/ORH after a 50–60%+ multi-day collapse.

### Inferences
- Codable core: N-day return ≥ X (X by cap bucket), ≥3 consecutive higher closes, price ≥ ~30% above SMA10 / ≥40–50% above SMA20 (his own descriptive numbers for one example, not a stated rule); short on break of the 1- or 5-minute opening-range low or on the first red 5-minute bar after an opening push, or on a VWAP rejection; stop = HOD; cover in pieces into the 10/20-day SMA zone. Needs 1-/5-minute data, VWAP, borrow availability/cost and halts — the last two are the main reasons a naive backtest will overstate results.
- He gives two different size thresholds (blog 50–100%/300–1000% vs. stream "two, three hundred percent or ... a thousand percent plus"); treat as cap-dependent ranges rather than a single number.

### Gaps
- No stated position-sizing rule specific to shorts ("starter" then add is how he describes it on streams), no rule for overnight holds through squeezes, and no numeric market-cap boundary between "larger" and "smaller" cap.
- Borrow/locate costs and short-sale-restriction effects are mentioned but never quantified.

## 4. STOCK UNIVERSE & SCANS — formulas, thresholds, watchlists, ranking

### Takeaway
His universe is "momentum leaders": liquid, high-ADR US stocks (and ETFs) ranking at the top of 1-, 3- and 6-month price gain, where gain is measured from the lowest low of the window (TC2000 `C / MINL22`, `…65–67`, `…126`). The published definition is "top 1–2%"; the TC2000 scans he actually showed used rank ≥ 93 (top ~7%) plus an ADR floor (2.4%→3.5–4% for himself; ≥5–6% recommended for small accounts) and a dollar-volume floor that rose with his account ($15–20M in 2019 → $60M → $80M in 2020), tuned so each scan returns ≤ 50–60 names. Final selection among candidates is manual.

### Inferences
- Backtest universe spec faithful to him: US-listed common stocks + ETFs, no OTC; `ADR20 = 100*(mean(H/L,20) − 1)`; `DollarVol = C × V` averaged (he does not state the window; TC2000's default is used); momentum = `C / min(L, n)` for n ∈ {22, 65–67, 126}; cross-sectional rank ≥ 93–99; thresholds as a sensitivity grid: ADR ≥ {3.5, 5, 7}%, dollar volume ≥ {$10M, $20M, $60M, $80M}.
- "Top 1–2%" (definition) vs "rank ≥ 93" (his scans) is not a contradiction in practice: the scans are a wide net, capped at ~50–60 names per timeframe, from which he hand-picks leaders. A mechanical test should report results for both 98–99 and 93+ percentiles.
- His liquidity floor is a function of account size (≈ position ≤ 1% of ADV; scan floor ≈ 20–50× account for small accounts), so "his" universe drifted toward mid/large caps as equity grew from ~$4M (2019) to $80M+ (2021); small-account replications that use ADR ≥ 5% and $10–20M dollar volume are trading a different, more volatile universe than he did in 2020–21.

## 5. RISK & SIZING — risk per trade, position size, concurrency, margin, overnight/gap handling

### Takeaway
Risk per trade fell as the account grew: ~1–2% (sub-$100K years) → 0.5–1.5% (below a few million) → mostly 0.2–0.5%, "rarely more than 1%" (2020–21). Positions are typically 10–20% of equity (FAQ: 5–25%, most 10–15%), hard ceiling 20–25% (streams) or 30% (blog) overnight; size is also capped at ~1% of average daily volume. He ran roughly 20–30 positions on margin in the 2020–21 bull (typically 120–140% long, "never" 200% one way) and far fewer in bad markets, scales risk in proportion to equity, uses market stops, and exits gap-throughs immediately.

### Inferences
- Size formula implied by his statements: `shares = equity × risk% / (entry − LOD)`, then cap at `min(20–25% of equity, 1% of ADV)`. With a 0.25–0.5% risk budget and LOD stops of ~1–3% of price, the uncapped size is 8–50% of equity, so the position cap binds often; effective risk per trade is therefore frequently *below* the stated budget.
- "1% on most trades" (Coyle, 2026 book) conflicts with his own 2020–21 statements (0.2–0.5% typical, ≤1% max); the chapter text was not available to resolve whether Coyle is rounding, describing an earlier/later period, or quoting him. List both.
- 20–30 simultaneous positions at ~5–20% each is consistent with the 120–140% long exposure he reports; a backtest that limits itself to 4–5 positions (his advice to small accounts) will have much lumpier results than his own book did.

### Gaps
- No stated portfolio-level heat limit (sum of open risk) or sector cap; he manages total exposure by feel ("I'm just too scared to take on more exposure").
- No explicit sizing rule for shorts; no stated rule for cutting size after losses beyond "decrease size until you get confidence" (reply 2021-02-03).

## 6. MARKET REGIME — when to trade breakouts and when to sit out

### Takeaway
One filter, stated many times: on the Nasdaq, breakouts are traded when the 10-day SMA is above the 20-day SMA and both are rising (looser versions: at least one rising); when the 10-day is below the 20-day and both slope down, "don't buy breakouts". He calls it imperfect — it "keeps you out of most of the bad markets and ... in most of the good markets" (later quantified, per secondary sources, as ~80%/80%) — admits he often violates it, and says the money comes from 3–5 good months a year.

### Cited Findings
- [A-stream] Canonical statement: "a momentum market is when the 10 and the 20 day moving averages — like I use the Nasdaq; for me the Nasdaq is the market ... — so when the 10 day is rising and the 20 day [is] rising and when [the] 10 day is above the 20 day, that's the momentum market ... if you get any breakouts when the 10 day moving average is sloping down, when [the] 20 day [is] sloping down and [the] 10 day [is] below the 20 day, there's a high fail rate; you ... probably shouldn't ... swing trade on the long side." — [Stream 2020-08-04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WfpvTnScSE8)
- [A-stream] Looser wording next day: "generally what you want to see is the 10-day sloping higher and the 20-day sloping higher, or at least one of them needs to be sloping higher generally, and the 10 day needs to be above the 20 day; it's fine if it undercuts a little bit for a while ... that's a general rule." — [Stream 2020-08-05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYFwzNZkRDI)
- [A-stream] Hard version: "when the 10-day [and] the 20-day moving average ... start sloping lower and the 10-day ... [is] lower than the 20-day ... you don't trade the breakouts; even if you see a good setup you shouldn't trade [it]." — [Stream 2020-08-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZdXxuBIFTZ0)
- [A-stream] Graded version: "an easy market filter is just use the 10 and the 20 day: if the 10 day ... is above the 20 day and ... both are [trending] higher, that's a very good market; if the 10 day gets below the 20 day ... you should be a bit cautious; if the 10 day start[s] sloping down you should be more cautious; if the 10 day slopes down, the 20 day slopes down and the 10 day [is] below the 20 day ... you should probably not ... buy any breakouts at all ... very simple, very robust market filter; I wish I followed it, but yesterday I didn't ... ### Inferences
- Codable regime flag (strict): `SMA10(IXIC) > SMA20(IXIC) and SMA10 rising and SMA20 rising`; lenient: `SMA10 > SMA20 and (SMA10 rising or SMA20 rising)`. "Rising" is undefined (1-day vs n-day slope) — a parameter to test. He says "the Nasdaq" without naming a symbol for the filter (on streams he refers at various times to the Nasdaq/COMPQ, "the Qs"/QQQ and TQQQ); third parties usually implement it on QQQ. IWM/SPX are discussed descriptively on streams, and in May 2021 he explicitly preferred the Nasdaq averages over SPY's.
- The filter applies to *breakouts*. EPs and parabolic trades are discussed as tradable outside it (earnings season; "EP off the lows"), though he says even EPs failed in the 2022 bear market (third-party report). A backtest should apply the regime switch per setup.
- Since he states he repeatedly broke the rule, his realised track record is not the track record of the filtered system.

### Gaps
- No written back-test or statistics from him for the filter; the "80%/80%" figure is his estimate as relayed by secondary sources.
- The 100-day-slope addition (2023) and anything he said about regime in 2024–2026 could not be verified from primary material.
- No explicit statement on how many months per calendar year breakouts "work" beyond "3–6 good months / 3–6 hard months".

Day-trading alerts and repeated blow-ups (2011–12) → first profitable year and migration to swing trading via Stockbee (2013) → large percentage years with 35–50% drawdowns from shorting (2014) → codified three setups, financial independence and public teaching (2017–2019) → the 2020–21 bull, when equity went from ~$4M to $82M+ and nearly all rules above were stated → written rules frozen since Nov 2021. ## 10. DISCRETION — what he says is "feel" and cannot be mechanised

### Takeaway
He teaches a mechanical skeleton to beginners but says plainly that he does not trade it mechanically: setup quality ("5-star"), tightness/linearity, which of many candidates to take (he passes on ~80% of good-looking setups), how much to sell into strength, when to override the MA trail or a stop, and how much exposure to carry are judgment built from thousands of hours of chart study. He doubts the method can be automated as well as it can be traded by hand.

### Inferences
- **Codable with little loss:** momentum-rank/ADR/liquidity universe; prior-move and consolidation-length windows; ORH entry; LOD stop with ADR/ATR cap; equity-risk sizing with position and ADV caps; 3–5-day partial; break-even; close-below-SMA trail; Nasdaq 10/20 regime flag; EP gap/volume/earnings-date screen; parabolic N-day run + ORL/first-red-bar/VWAP-fail trigger.

- **Not codable as stated:** star rating and the decision to skip ~80% of candidates; choosing among simultaneous triggers; anticipation entries; discretionary early exits (extended above the 10-day, "doesn't act right", opportunity cost); selling "every 3–5%"; overriding stops; how far to lean into margin when "deserved"; reading news quality for non-earnings EPs; borrow/halts on shorts.
