## 7. Historical valuation at similar growth rates, and what would be needed for the multiple to expand

### Takeaway
Reliable historical forward-multiple data for periods of comparable growth was not found; what is available shows Disney on a trailing P/E of about 14x in late 2010 rising to about 21x by 2013–14, a forward P/E peak of 39x in January 2022 and a low of 13x in July 2026. **Price implied by a given multiple (derived)**

| Multiple | on FY2027 EPS $7.52 | on FY2028 EPS $8.46 |
|---|---|---|
| 14x | $105 | $118 |
| 16x | $120 | $135 |
| 18x (≈ S&P 500 today) | $135 | $152 |
| 20x (≈ Disney's 5-year average per Yahoo/TIKR) | $150 | $169 |

- At $122.36 the market is paying 16.3x for about 10–12% EPS growth. The $130–$144 target range corresponds to 17.3–19.1x FY2027 EPS, or 15.4–17.0x FY2028 EPS.
- In 2010–2014, when Disney was also compounding earnings at a double-digit rate, the trailing multiple expanded from the mid-teens to about 21x; today's 17.4–17.9x trailing adjusted multiple sits in the middle of that range. This is a loose analogy (trailing GAAP then, adjusted now; a growing cable-network business then, a declining one now).
- The 2021–22 multiples (39x forward) reflected trough earnings and streaming-subscriber enthusiasm and are not a realistic reference point — the Yahoo author makes the same point.
- Conditions that the sources imply would support 18–20x: (1) streaming margins continuing to rise toward Netflix's despite the loss of disclosure — which makes the proof harder to see; (2) Experiences growth resuming in the second half of FY2027 and Asian parks stabilising; (3) the $3 bn of savings reaching earnings rather than being fully reinvested; (4) upward revisions to FY2027–28 EPS (so far only +0.3% to +1.2%); (5) free cash flow covering buybacks and dividends (currently about $1.4 bn short). Conversely, the stock already recovered from 13x to 16x between July and November 2026, so part of the re-rating has happened.
- Because Netflix now trades at the same forward multiple (about 16–17x) with faster growth, a Disney re-rating above the market multiple would need the market to pay for parks and franchise durability rather than for streaming.
