## 1. Headline numbers — Q4 FY2026 and full-year FY2026, versus consensus, with and without the 53rd week; one-off items

### Takeaway
Q4 FY26 (14 weeks to Oct 3, 2026; reported Tue Nov 17, 2026) was a clean beat: revenue ~$25.95B (+16% reported, roughly +8% excluding the extra week), total segment operating income ~$5.0B (+43%), adjusted EPS $1.77 (+59%; $1.59 / +43% excluding the 53rd week) versus ~$1.68 consensus. Full-year FY26: revenue $102.34B (+8%), segment operating income $19.73B (+12%), adjusted EPS $7.02 (+18%; $6.85 / ~+16% ex-53rd week), but GAAP EPS fell 18% to $5.63 because of ~$1.46B of restructuring/impairment charges (mainly A+E impairments — there was **no gain** on the A+E sale), a $0.23 non-cash tax charge, and a prior-year tax benefit.

### Inferences
- Underlying (ex-53rd-week) Q4 growth was strong but far less dramatic than the headlines: revenue ≈ +8%, segment OI ≈ +29%, adjusted EPS +43%. The Q4 FY25 base was weak (segment OI −5%, Entertainment OI $691M on tough theatrical comps), which flatters the comparison.
- FY26 adjusted EPS ex-53rd week of ~$6.85 (≈ +16%) beat the company's own ~12% ex-53rd-week guidance (which implied ≈ $6.64 **[derived: 5.93 × 1.12]**), i.e. roughly a $0.20 beat versus guidance on a comparable basis.
- The trajectory through FY26 improved sequentially: segment OI growth went −9% → +4% → +21% → +43% (≈ +29% ex-53rd week); adjusted EPS growth −7% → +8% → +28% → +59% (+43% ex).
- GAAP earnings quality is weaker than adjusted: FY26 GAAP EPS fell 18%. Implied FY26 tax expense ≈ $3.6B (pre-tax $14.2B less consolidated net income $10.6B) ≈ 25% effective rate, versus an implied net tax *benefit* of ≈ $1.4B in FY25 (pre-tax $12.0B, consolidated net income $13.4B) **[derived]** — so the GAAP decline is mostly a tax-comparison effect plus A+E impairments, not operating deterioration.
- FY26 adjusted-to-GAAP bridge: 5.63 + 0.65 (restructuring/impairment) + 0.23 (tax charge) = 6.51; the remaining ≈ $0.51 to reach $7.02 is not itemised in what I retrieved **[derived residual; composition not found — probably acquisition-related amortisation]**. For Q4: 1.51 + 0.13 = 1.64; residual ≈ $0.13 not itemised.

## 2. Segment detail — Entertainment, Sports, Experiences; quarter, year and prior quarters; reporting changes

### Takeaway
All three segments grew operating income in Q4 FY26 even after stripping out the extra week: Entertainment $1,327M (+92%; ≈ +59% ex-wk53) with streaming (SVOD) operating income doubling to $824M at a 13.6% margin; Sports $1,393M (+53%; ≈ +44% ex); Experiences a record $2,250M (+20%; ≈ +10% ex) on domestic strength, with international parks operating income down 7% on Shanghai/Hong Kong weakness. From Q1 FY27 Disney stops disclosing SVOD operating income/margin, moves licensing-type consumer products into Entertainment and drops equity income from Entertainment — reducing transparency exactly where the profit momentum has been strongest.

### Inferences
- Streaming is the swing factor in Entertainment: SVOD OI nearly doubled to $2.57B in FY26 and margin stepped up every quarter (8.4% → 10.6% → 12.9% → 13.6%). Non-SVOD Entertainment OI (linear, content sales/licensing, equity income) was ≈ $2.88B in FY26 vs ≈ $3.37B in FY25 **[derived: 5,443 − 2,568; 4,674 − 1,302]**, i.e. down ≈ 15% — the legacy businesses are still shrinking underneath.
- Dropping the SVOD operating income disclosure at the moment management says it will raise SVOD content and marketing investment in FY27 means investors lose the cleanest margin KPI just as the margin may flatten.
- Sports profit was back-end loaded: OI was down in each of Q1–Q3 FY26 (−23%, −5%, −17%) and the full-year +7% depended on Q4 (+53%; ≈ +44% ex-53rd week), helped by NFL scheduling, the NFL transaction and an extra week. Ex-53rd week FY26 Sports OI growth ≈ +4% **[derived: (3,090 − 80) / 2,890]**.
- Experiences quality: domestic is doing the work (Q4 OI ≈ +21% ex-53rd week), while international OI fell 7% and consumer products grew 12%. After the FY27 recast, the high-margin licensing business (≈ 50% OI margin in Q4: 651 / 1,311) leaves Experiences, so reported Experiences margins and history will shift.

## 3. Fiscal 2027 guidance and Q1 FY27 guidance; headwinds; comparison with earlier 2026 statements

### Takeaway
FY27 guidance (given Nov 17, 2026) is: double-digit adjusted EPS growth and ~8% total segment operating income growth, both excluding the 53rd week and after the reporting changes; high-single-digit operating income growth at Entertainment and Experiences and double-digit at Sports (ex-53rd week); cash content spend ~$26B (+9%), capex ~$9B, free cash flow ~$11B, buybacks ~$9B, dividend $2.00.
