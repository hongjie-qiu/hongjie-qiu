## 1. Experiences (parks, resorts, cruises, consumer products)

### Takeaway
Experiences is the strongest and best-evidenced growth driver going into fiscal 2027: record fiscal Q4 and full-year 2026 results, domestic attendance +3% and per-capita spending +4% on a like-for-like basis, Walt Disney World forward bookings pacing ahead, and a guide for high-single-digit operating-income growth in fiscal 2027 — while the main local competitor (Universal Orlando) reported falling revenue and profit. The bear evidence is specific rather than broad: Shanghai and Hong Kong are deteriorating and expected to stay weak in fiscal 2027, international visits to the US are still shrinking, capex and pre-opening costs are rising, and visibility on the new Asia-based ship is limited.

### Inferences
- Like-for-like fiscal Q4 2026 growth (about +8% revenue, +10% operating income after removing the stated 53rd-week contribution) is consistent with the fiscal 2027 high-single-digit operating-income guide; the driver mix (attendance +3%, per-capita +4%, new cruise capacity) looks balanced rather than price-only.
- Epic Universe has not visibly taken share from Walt Disney World: TEA shows Disney's Orlando parks flat in calendar 2025, and in calendar Q3 2026 Universal's revenue fell while Disney's domestic attendance rose. The more plausible headwind for Disney's US parks is inbound international travel, which is still declining.
- The Asia weakness is a profit-mix issue more than a headline risk: Disney owns under half of both parks, but consolidates them, so reported international attendance/revenue can look worse than the attributable profit impact. The size of that impact is not disclosed.
- Fiscal 2027 Experiences earnings will be reported on a new basis (without consumer products) and against a 53-week comparison — two mechanical effects that will depress optical growth and should not be read as demand weakness.
- Caribbean cruise capacity growth (cited by both Royal Caribbean and Carnival) is the main external pricing risk for Disney's fleet expansion, most of which also sails the Bahamas/Caribbean from Florida.

## 2. Streaming (Disney+, Hulu) and competitive position

### Takeaway
Profitability is clearly strengthening — streaming operating income doubled to $824 million in fiscal Q4 2026 and the fiscal 2026 margin reached 11.5% — but growth is price-led, advertising is soft (ad revenue +5.6%, ad rates reported down 6%), and Disney is removing the last streaming-specific profit disclosure just as it merges Hulu into Disney+ and considers a free tier. In share of viewing, Disney (9.3%) sits between YouTube (15.8%) and Netflix (7.7%); its churn is second-best after Netflix.

### Takeaway
The US consumer backdrop is mixed: employment is firm (unemployment 4.1%, payrolls +165,000 in October 2026) and sentiment has ticked up, but sentiment is at very low absolute levels (51.0), inflation is elevated, real retail spending is weak and the Federal Reserve has a tightening bias. ### Inferences
- Disney's US parks customer skews to households able to pay $119–$229 per day, so the "higher-income households carry spending" pattern in the retail data is consistent with Disney's attendance and per-capita growth despite record-low sentiment readings; the exposure is to a broadening of weakness up the income scale or to a labour-market turn, neither visible in October 2026 data.
- A Fed with a hiking bias is a second-order risk for Disney (financing costs on a $9 billion-a-year capex programme, and consumer credit for vacations) rather than a first-order one.
- The FCC dispute is the least quantifiable risk: the owned ABC stations are a small part of profit, but licence jeopardy and content-related pressure can affect affiliate relations, advertising and — as September 2025 showed — streaming churn.
- A Midjourney ruling on summary judgment (possible in the next few months) would be a sector-wide precedent on AI training; it matters for the long-run value of Disney's IP more than for near-term earnings.
