## 1. US DOJ search monopoly case (Judge Amit Mehta, D.D.C.; appeal in D.C. Circuit Nos. 26-5023, 26-5047, 26-5049)

### Takeaway
Mehta's remedies (opinion 2 Sep 2025, final judgment 5 Dec 2025, in force 3 Feb 2026) are behavioral: no Chrome sale and no ban on default-placement payments (Apple and Samsung can still be paid on a non-exclusive basis), but exclusive contracts are barred, default deals are capped at one year, and Google must share search data and offer syndication to "qualified competitors" for six years under a Technical Committee. As of the latest reporting no rival had yet received data (status report of 6 Aug 2026), the cross-appeals are fully briefed (29 Oct 2026), and D.C. Circuit argument is reported for January 2027 with no exact day found; the government's cross-appeal focuses on reinstating a payment ban.

### Inferences
- The brief fragment plus MLex's "one legal error" wording suggest the government's cross-appeal is confined to the payment ban. If so, a court-ordered Chrome divestiture is effectively off the table on appeal, and the secondary sites calling Chrome "formally open" are probably wrong or out of date.
- The remaining swing factor is the payment ban: if the D.C. Circuit reverses on that point, Apple and Samsung default economics would be reopened. Google's own appeal is the upside case, since it attacks liability and could unwind everything.
- The 45-day notice is the next observable trigger at the district court: when plaintiffs serve it, Google will likely renew its stay motion. On the 6 Aug 2026 status report's timeline that could fall around year-end 2026.
- With argument in January 2027, a panel decision in mid-to-late 2027 is plausible; rehearing or Supreme Court petitions could push finality into 2028.

## 2. US DOJ advertising-technology case (Judge Leonie Brinkema, E.D. Va.)

### Takeaway
On 2 Sep 2026 Brinkema refused every structural remedy (no AdX divestiture, no open-sourcing of DFP's auction logic, no contingent DFP sale) and ordered behavioral remedies with a six-year term: Prebid interoperability, opening AdX to rival ad servers, publisher data export, bid-data sharing, a ban on Google Ads favoring Google's own tools, and monitoring. The parties filed a disputed 41-page proposed final judgment on 2 Oct 2026; I found no report that the final judgment has been entered or that any appeal has been filed, so the case status after early October 2026 is unknown.

### Cited Findings

#### Decision (latest found: proposed final judgment filed 2 Oct 2026)
- Liability (17 Apr 2025): the court found Google violated antitrust law and holds a monopoly in online advertising. — [Lanier Law Firm, counsel to Texas [M]](https://www.lanierlawfirm.com/business-litigation/google-lawsuit-and-multi-state-representation/)
- Remedies decision issued **2 Sep 2026** after a September 2025 remedies trial. Brinkema rejected (i) divestiture of AdX, (ii) open-sourcing DFP's final-auction logic, and (iii) a contingent divestiture of the remaining DFP business, and adopted most behavioral remedies with modifications. A two-page order disposed of the remedies phase. — [PPC Land [M]](https://ppc.land/doj-loses-adx-divestiture-bid-as-brinkema-accepts-behavioral-remedies/); [Courthouse News [M]](https://www.courthousenews.com/google-dodges-antitrust-breakup-of-ad-tech-business/); [Computerworld [M]](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4218844/judge-spares-googles-ad-tech-business-from-a-breakup.html)

- Next steps: the parties had 30 days to submit a proposed final judgment. A 41-page filing arrived on 2 Oct 2026 with disputed provisions (reported by PPC Land). Remedies take effect 60 days after Brinkema signs the final order. The latest reporting reviewed shows no final judgment entered. — [Everything-PR ≈ [S]](https://everything-pr.com/google-ad-tech-antitrust-remedies); [Digiday [M]](https://digiday.com/media-buying/google-avoided-yet-another-breakup-now-comes-the-test-of-the-ad-tech-remedies/)

#### Appeal status (latest found: statements of intent only, September to early October 2026)
- Google disagrees with the liability ruling and will appeal, per Reuters as relayed. — [Courthouse News ≈ [M]](https://www.courthousenews.com/google-dodges-antitrust-breakup-of-ad-tech-business/). - Critics: Open Markets Institute urged DOJ and the states to cross-appeal for divestiture; Public Knowledge said the remedies leave monopoly power intact; another group called the decision an "embarrassing capitulation". — [Everything-PR ≈ [S]](https://everything-pr.com/google-ad-tech-antitrust-remedies); [Public Knowledge [M]](https://publicknowledge.org/public-knowledge-warns-google-ad-tech-remedies-leave-monopoly-power-intact/); [Center for Media & Digital Governance [S]](https://cmdg.tech/publications/ad-tech-decision-constitutes-embarrassing-capitulation-to-google)

### Inferences
- Both US judges have now declined a breakup, so the structural tail risk in the US is much smaller than a year ago. What is left in ad tech is compliance cost, some share and take-rate pressure, and follow-on damages (section 4).
- Because Google intends to appeal liability to the Fourth Circuit once judgment is entered, and DOJ has not said whether it will cross-appeal, the ad-tech case will probably run through 2027.
- The 12-to-15-month implementation windows mean little product change before late 2027 even without a stay.

## 3. European Union

### Takeaway
The EU is now the most active front: the €4.1 billion Android fine became final on 2 Jul 2026 (Alphabet paid $5.2 billion including interest that month), the first DMA non-compliance decisions followed on 23 Jul 2026 (€890 million: €460 million for search self-preferencing, €430 million for Play anti-steering), and DMA specification decisions of 16 Jul 2026 force search-data sharing from January 2027 and Android AI interoperability by 1 Aug 2027. ## 7. Policy and political backdrop

### Takeaway
Washington is defending Google abroad while still litigating against it at home: the President threatened a Section 301 investigation and tariffs within a day of the EU's July 2026 DMA fine, yet DOJ kept pressing its cross-appeal for a payment ban. DOJ antitrust leadership has been in flux since Gail Slater left in February 2026, and commentators expect a tilt toward behavioral settlements. On AI, the EU delayed its high-risk rules to December 2027 and the US has no federal preemption statute, so state laws still apply; power and permitting limits on data centers are tightening in several states.

### Inferences
- US trade pressure is a partial shield for Google in Europe: it raises the political cost of an EU-ordered ad-tech breakup or large periodic penalties. It has not stopped the Commission so far, which issued the DMA fines and specification decisions in July 2026.
- Domestic risk now sits mainly with the courts, not with enforcement appetite. With DOJ leadership unsettled and a reported preference for behavioral outcomes, a negotiated end to remaining cases is more plausible than new structural demands, but DOJ's July 2026 brief shows it has not dropped the search case.
- Data-center constraints are a pace-of-growth issue, not a legal liability: state pauses are temporary and carve-outs in the federal AI order favor infrastructure, but interconnection queues are the binding limit.
