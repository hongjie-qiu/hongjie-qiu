- The S&P 500 at 7,323.63 is 6.3% below its Oct 6 record close of 7,818.93, and the Nasdaq-100 at 27,116.26 is 9.4% below its 29,928.31 window high (both computed): a pullback bordering on correction in the Nasdaq-100, with the benchmark index now below its own 200-day average. GOOG's breakdown (its own death cross came three weeks earlier, on Oct 29) led the market's deterioration rather than followed it.
- GOOG's own damage is larger than the market's and mostly idiosyncratic: about −31% from its high versus −6% for the S&P 500 and −9% for the Nasdaq-100. A market that keeps falling would press on a stock that already sits 1–3% above a critical support shelf.
- Nov 18 showed a rotation back toward tech at the index level (Nasdaq +0.42% while the Dow fell 1.11%), and GOOG rose with it (+0.58%), but one day does not change the trend of five S&P 500 losses in a row.
- A 10-year yield above 5% with the Fed still hiking is an unusual, hostile setting for long-duration mega-cap growth stocks and for companies funding very large capex; it is the macro counterpart of the stock-specific worry about spending and negative free cash flow.
- A VIX in the mid-20s indicates elevated but not panic-level fear; it has not reached the spike levels that often mark a washout low, consistent with GOOG's own option metrics (IV rank near 50).
