# Market size, liquidity, capacity, competitive structure and other venues for real-money prediction markets — state as of 26 November 2026 (viewpoint: an individual running an automated strategy with small-to-medium capital)

Scope and method. This note closes the gap left by the earlier round (headline volumes only). It builds on, and does not repeat, the figures already established there (October 2026 US volume of $120.73B; Bloomberg bot study; Akey et al.; Columbia wash-trading study; named firms; holding rewards). Web search was capped at 45 queries (all used). Page fetching worked only for github.com, raw.githubusercontent.com and the PyPI / npm registries, so most press and data pages below were **not opened**.

Labels used on every finding:
- Reading: **[RF]** = read in full in this session (source text, result files or registry metadata fetched directly); **[SS]** = search-result summary only (page not opened; wording, dates and numbers may be imprecise); **[DERIVED]** = my own computation from [RF] data, with the files named.
- Evidence class: **[V]** = verified-type evidence (academic paper, on-chain / order-book data, official or court filing, package registry); **[P]** = press or secondary data reporting; **[U]** = unverified claim (vendor or affiliate guide, company marketing, blog, X-derived).
- Volume type: "notional" = contracts × $1 payout; "cash" = dollars actually paid. Where a source does not say, I write "type not stated".
- Chinese terms encountered: 流动性 (liquidity), 深度 (depth), 价差 (bid-ask spread), 订单簿 (order book), 冷门合约 (longshot contracts), 做市商奖励计划 (market-maker reward programme), 手续费 (fees), 发币 (token launch), 空投 (airdrop), 积分 (points), 反女巫 (anti-sybil), 套利偏差 (arbitrage deviation).

Dating. Every bullet carries its sample period. Anything dated before mid-2026 (active-user counts, Robinhood's share of Kalshi, in-house desk statements from late 2025, Hyperliquid launch-month volume, the Columbia wash-trading series) may have been superseded and is marked or should be read that way.

Two primary sources were read in full and carry most of the hard microstructure numbers: (a) Dubach, "The Anatomy of a Decentralized Prediction Market" (arXiv 2604.24366, single-author preprint, not peer reviewed) and its replication package; (b) Dubach, "Measuring Trade Direction in a Prediction Market" (arXiv 2610.06412, October 2026) and its replication package of saved results. Both cover **Polymarket international only**. No comparable primary data for Kalshi or Polymarket US could be read.

## 1. Volume, open interest and active traders during 2026 (Polymarket international, Polymarket US, Kalshi), category split, and concentration in headline markets

### Takeaway
Reported sector volume rose roughly five- to seven-fold during 2026 (about $17–23B in January, $52–56B in June–July, $120.7B on US-regulated venues alone in October), but these are notional contract counts dominated by Kalshi sports and parlay-style "combo" contracts: cash paid in October was reported at about $22.7B and sector open interest is only about $1.5–2B. Kalshi holds roughly 70% of global and 81% of US-regulated notional volume, sports are about 70–85% of volume but under 40% of open interest, and on Polymarket international a few dozen headline markets (geopolitics in March 2026) carry most of the on-chain dollar volume.

### Inferences
- **Notional versus cash.** Using the earlier round's pair for October 2026 ($123.6B traded, $22.7B cash paid, 644M trades), cash is about 18% of notional and the average trade is about $192 notional or $35 cash. July 2026 (Artemis) works out to about $145 notional per trade; Polymarket US in October 2026 to about $200 notional per trade ($14.77B / 74M). DefiLlama's July figure ($19.06B) is about one third of Artemis's ($55.5B); differences in venue coverage and in notional-versus-cash accounting are the likely reasons, but I could not verify either methodology.
- **Derived October 2026 shares (DeFi Rate):** Kalshi 81.3%, Polymarket US 12.2%, Novig 2.7%, Underdog 1.6%, the other four tracked exchanges together about $2.56B (2.1%). Implied September levels: Kalshi about $60.9B, Novig about $1.45B, Underdog about $0.72B.
- **Kalshi's October notional ($98B) nearly equals its entire lifetime volume through mid-June 2026 (about $112B).** Combined with "combo volume +120%" in October, this points to parlay-style contracts and the NFL season inflating notional far faster than cash or open interest. Sports-plus-combos volume should not be read as depth available to a non-sports strategy.
- **Turnover is extremely fast.** July 2026 notional of $55.5B against peak open interest near $2B is roughly 28 times a month. Positions in sports and short-dated crypto are opened and resolved within hours; politics holds 45% of open interest on 9% of volume. From the August figures, politics open interest is about $660M and sports about $556M across 11 platforms.
- **Illustrative only, not a measured figure:** if DefiLlama's shares (Kalshi 70.1%, Polymarket combined 24.6%) held in October and are applied to DeFi Rate's Kalshi number, Polymarket combined would be about $34B notional and Polymarket international about $20B. The two trackers count differently and the share is from an earlier, post-World-Cup period, so this could be off by a factor of two in either direction.
- **Concentration.** On Polymarket international in March 2026, perhaps 40–50 markets carried most of the dollar volume, and they were geopolitical (fee-free) questions. The median "tradeable" long-tail market traded about $550 a day. A strategy's addressable flow is therefore set by a small rotating set of headline markets plus a very large number of tiny ones.

## 2. Depth and spreads: distributions for headline, mid-tail and long-tail markets; slippage for $100 / $1,000 / $10,000 / $100,000; share of markets with negligible liquidity; liquidity after the 2026 fee and rebate changes

### Takeaway
On Polymarket international, the typical quoted spread across all listed tokens was about 4 cents in February–August 2026 with a bimodal cross-section (roughly 35–55% of tokens at 2 cents or tighter depending on the month, about 15% wider than 50 cents), and displayed depth is small outside a handful of markets: by my calculation from released panel data, a $10,000 order fits inside the ten best displayed levels in about 30% of the 100 largest markets and about 2% of long-tail markets, and a $100,000 order in under 10% and 0% respectively. The only before-and-after evidence on the 2026 fee changes shows no detectable widening of quoted spreads after taker fees were extended on 30 March 2026; no comparable distributional data for Kalshi was found.

### Cited Findings

**Polymarket international — spread distribution across the whole venue**
- [RF][V] Token-day panel of the public order-book archive, 22 February–9 August 2026 (171 days, 17.0M token-days, of which 13.45M or 79% carried a live non-placeholder quote): daily median quoted spread per token has p25 = 1 cent, median = 3.8 cents, p75 = 26 cents, p95 = 85 cents, mean = 19.4 cents. The daily cross-sectional median ranged from 2 to 9 cents; the share of live tokens with a spread of 2 cents or less ranged from 28.7% to 64.0%. The authors describe the cross-section as bimodal: a tight mode at one or two ticks and a wide mode above 50 cents. — [x3_composition.json](https://github.com/philippdubach/polymarket-trade-direction-replication/blob/main/analysis/x3_composition.json); [x3_composition.py](https://github.com/philippdubach/polymarket-trade-direction-replication/blob/main/analysis/x3_composition.py)

**Polymarket international — spreads by price level and by market tier**

### Inferences
- **Practical slippage picture for Polymarket international (inference from the tables above).** $100: effectively free of impact in headline markets and usually fillable within a few levels in the long tail; cost is the half-spread (0.5–1 cent in tight markets, several cents in the wide mode). $1,000: fills at or near the touch in roughly a third of top-100 markets, needs to walk the book in most others, and exceeds ten levels of displayed depth in two-thirds of long-tail markets. $10,000: realistic as a single marketable order only in the top 30 or so markets at a time. $100,000: only in a handful (under ten) of flagship markets; elsewhere it must be worked passively over hours or days.
- **Quoted spreads look tight in headline markets; the binding constraint is depth.** Half the top-100 markets quote 1 cent wide, yet the median displayed size at the best ask is under $200 on a time-averaged basis.
- **Average realised taker cost (about 1 cent per share one way, volume-weighted) is of the same order as the taker fee at mid prices and as most documented structural edges** (1–4 cents in the earlier notes). With the fee formula recorded in the earlier notes (coefficient × p × (1 − p) per share), the fee at 50 cents is 0.75–1.75 cents for coefficients of 0.03–0.07 and 0.25 cents where the coefficient was cut to 0.01 on 20 November 2026. A taker strategy in a fee-bearing category therefore needs gross edge of roughly 2–3 cents per share or more to clear spread plus fee; this is my arithmetic, not a measured figure.
- **The fill-weighted five-minute numbers (realised spread 6.5 cents, impact −4.2 cents) suggest the typical small taker fill is followed by an adverse move.** This is consistent with the maker-wins / taker-loses evidence in the earlier notes, but the author warns against a causal reading.
- **Fee changes did not visibly damage quoted liquidity.** The 30 March fee extension shows a zero median change in spreads; the 28 April cutover shows a two-day disruption. Structural liquidity (share of tight markets) was flat from March to July and worse in August as listings surged.
- **Kalshi is probably deeper in sports game lines and thinner in economics and finance contracts** (Risk.net lede; Kalshi's sports share), but I found no measured distribution, so any Kalshi slippage number in the final report would be a guess.

### Gaps
- **No measured slippage or price-impact table for $100 / $1,000 / $10,000 / $100,000 exists in any source found.** The table above is my approximation from time-averaged displayed depth on one venue in one month.
- **Kalshi and Polymarket US: no spread or depth distributions were found.** OddsShopper articles reportedly contain August 2026 Kalshi order-book observations but could not be opened ([which Kalshi markets are liquid](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/which-kalshi-markets-are-liquid)); the Risk.net analysis is paywalled; Kalshi's own post "liquid prediction markets are finally here" was not readable ([Kalshi news](https://news.kalshi.com/p/liquid-prediction-markets-are-finally-here)).

## 3. Capacity: how much capital strategies can absorb, typical position sizes of profitable wallets, returns versus account size

### Takeaway
I found no academic or rigorous practitioner estimate of strategy capacity and no study of returns by account size; the question has to be bounded from structure. Sector open interest of about $1.5–2B, median long-tail flow of a few hundred dollars a day per market, average trade sizes of $20–$230, and displayed depth of a few hundred to a few thousand dollars in most markets imply that liquidity-taking strategies outside flagship markets absorb hundreds to low thousands of dollars per position, which suits accounts of roughly $10K–$250K and explains why large firms concentrate on the few markets where they can deploy size.

### Inferences
- **Per-position capacity for a liquidity-taking strategy (Polymarket international, from section 2):** long tail about $100–$1,000; mid-tier markets inside the top 100 about $1,000–$10,000; flagship markets $10,000–$100,000 or more. A portfolio of 30–100 concurrent long-tail positions therefore deploys roughly $5K–$50K; reaching $250K requires either mid-tier and flagship markets or patient resting orders.
- **Passive strategies are flow-limited rather than depth-limited.** A maker capturing 10% of the flow in a median long-tail market trades about $55 a day; 100 such markets give about $5.5K of daily turnover. The same share of a median top-100 market gives about $21K a day, but that is where professional makers quote.
- **Open interest bounds holding strategies.** Politics open interest of about $660M across all platforms (August 2026) is the pool for slow, text-heavy positions. A $1M book would be about 0.15% of it, and concentrated in the few markets that hold most of it.
- **Scale reading.** With $10K–$100K an individual is not capacity-constrained in the niches where an LLM helps (slow, semantic mispricings), but each trade is small. From roughly $0.5M upward the strategy is pushed into headline markets and into direct competition with Susquehanna, Jump and the in-house desks. This is an inference from depth and flow data, not a measured capacity curve.
- **Average profit per profitable bot** in the Bloomberg study ($131M across 823 accounts) is about $159K over the sample, which indicates the scale at which the documented winners operate; the distribution is certainly skewed.

- **No academic or practitioner capacity estimate per strategy was found**, and none of the search results quantify edge decay against deployed capital.
- **Typical position sizes of profitable wallets** and **returns versus account size**: no study found. The CEPR paper may contain size breakdowns but was not read.
- Kalshi position accountability levels (numbers) and their effect on retail-size accounts were not retrieved.
- Capital efficiency details (collateral netting, margin, interest on balances) are in the earlier notes and were not re-researched.

### Inferences
- **Where the professionals are.** Susquehanna and Jump quote the highest-volume products (sports game lines, major crypto, headline politics and macro) on Kalshi, Polymarket and Rothera; that is where 70–85% of notional trades. A retail-scale taker there is trading against firms with fee concessions, position headroom and equity alignment with the venue.
- **Where they are not.** Coverage that Susquehanna trades only where it can deploy size, the one-to-three-wallet tail of niche Polymarket markets, and thin economics and finance books all point to mid- and long-tail markets being quoted by small automated makers or nobody. That is the plausible habitat for an individual's automated strategy, at the cost of small size per trade.
- **In-house desks are a governance risk more than a competitive one.** Kalshi's desk claims under 6% of sports making volume and no profit; the more concrete risk is discretion: an operator that is also a counterparty has voided trades elsewhere (Novig).
- **Professional share of volume and profit is not measurable from public sources.** The defensible statement is qualitative: makers as a class earn what takers lose (earlier notes), winners are few and mostly automated (Bloomberg, Akey et al.), and the number of capable professional entrants grew during 2026 (Jump doubling, DRW and AQR desks, DraftKings and Polymarket desks).
- **Direction of travel.** Rebate tiers from 20 November 2026 (earlier notes), equity-for-liquidity deals and designated-maker privileges all concentrate advantages in the largest liquidity providers. A small maker should expect worse relative economics over time, not better.

### Gaps
- **No figure for market makers' or trading firms' share of Kalshi or Polymarket volume or profit was found**; a targeted search returned nothing quantitative.
- Kalshi's current list of designated market makers and the fee, rebate and position terms are partly in confidential appendices; the public filings were located but not read.
- Whether Polymarket's in-house desk is live in November 2026, and its scale, is unknown.
- DRW, Wintermute, AQR and Citadel activity levels after mid-2026 were not confirmed; the Finance Magnates "Citadel is circling" piece is undated in the results.
- The CEPR / Akey et al. paper and the Kalshi-hosted paper "Who calibrates markets" (October 2026) were not read ([Kalshi research PDF](https://kalshi.com/research/kalshi-research-who-calibrates-markets.pdf)).

### Inferences
- **Investable universe for an individual's automated strategy in November 2026:** Kalshi (about 81% of US notional, full API), Polymarket US (about 12%, API) and Polymarket international (roughly a quarter of global volume with Polymarket US, full API). Everything else is either closed to programmatic retail trading or too small.
- **The fastest-growing new pools are closed.** Novig, Underdog, DKeX, Rothera and Crypto.com together are perhaps 6–7% of US notional and are sports apps with affiliated or in-house market makers and no evident public API. They matter as price references and as evidence that sports flow is fragmenting away from Kalshi, not as venues for this strategy.
- **Front ends versus pools.** Coinbase is a Kalshi front end. Robinhood is now three things at once: a Kalshi front end, the owner (with Susquehanna) of Rothera, and a distributor of Crypto.com's OG contracts. Interactive Brokers routes to Kalshi and CME and owns ForecastEx. FanDuel Predicts is a CME front end. DraftKings moved from front end to own pool in June 2026.
- **API-friendly on-chain challengers are not capacity.** Limitless, Opinion, Predict.fun and Myriad have single-digit millions of dollars locked and volumes that swing with incentives. They may offer cross-venue price discrepancies, but with position sizes of tens to hundreds of dollars and real smart-contract and operational risk (the Predict.fun wallet incident).
- **Hyperliquid outcome markets are the one technically attractive newcomer** (native order book, mature API, existing crypto-trading user base) but volume evidence stops at launch month, and coverage so far is crypto price binaries where latency competition is strongest.
