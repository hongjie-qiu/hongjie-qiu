## 1. Polymarket (international): architecture, order mechanics, APIs, speed bumps, fees/rebates/rewards, funding frictions, and what changed in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
As of late November 2026 the international venue is still an off-chain-matched central limit order book settling on Polygon, but since the "CLOB V2" cut-over (reported as 28 Apr 2026) collateral is the USDC-backed wrapper **pUSD** rather than USDC.e, and the venue is no longer fee-free: a price-curved taker fee `fee = C × θ × p × (1−p)` applies in every category except geopolitics, with θ cut on 20 Nov 2026 (23:59 UTC) to 0.01 for most crypto / non-election politics / mentions / economics / culture / weather markets and 0.05 for sports, finance/tech, election and BTC 5-/15-minute markets, while taker and maker rebates now only reach accounts above $200k of 30-day *weighted* volume. Marketable orders in crypto markets face a non-cancellable taker delay that was changed four times in 2026 (500 ms → removed → 250 ms → 50 ms → 150 ms) and marketable sports orders face per-market delays of roughly 0-3 s, so the rules explicitly disadvantage latency-taking and must be re-read from the changelog before every deployment.

**Order types, tick sizes, minimum sizes**
- [OFFICIAL] Order types are GTC, GTD, FOK and FAK; limit orders are GTC or GTD, market(able) orders are FOK ("fills the entire order immediately or does not fill any of it") or FAK ("fills against the available liquidity immediately and cancels any unfilled remainder"); post-only is a flag used with GTC/GTD. — [Polymarket docs: L2 client methods](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/clients/l2); [Polymarket docs: Place Orders](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/orders/create)
- [OFFICIAL-repo/COMMUNITY] Post-only orders are rejected if they would immediately match. — [Polymarket/agent-skills order-patterns.md](https://github.com/Polymarket/agent-skills/blob/main/order-patterns.md); [community mirror](https://github.com/KJHelgason/Polymarket_Agent_skills/blob/master/trading/order-types.md) (attribution between the two not pinned)
- [SECONDARY] GTD expiry needs a minimum buffer of about now + 60 s. — [polymarkets.co.il order-type guide](https://polymarkets.co.il/en/bots/polymarket-fok-fak-gtc-orders/)
- [OFFICIAL] Tick size is per market and one of "0.1", "0.01", "0.001", "0.0001"; off-tick prices are rejected (`INVALID_ORDER_MIN_TICK_SIZE`). — [Polymarket docs: L2 client methods](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/clients/l2). Conflict: a third-party guide says the usual tick is 0.01 and additionally lists 0.005 and 0.0025 — [parlay.run](https://www.parlay.run/polymarket-api) / [cryptoskills.dev](https://cryptoskills.dev/skills/polymarket) (attribution not pinned); trust the official enumeration and read tick size from the market object.
- [OFFICIAL] Each market carries a `min_order_size` (minimum number of shares the CLOB accepts); the docs example shows 5. — [Polymarket docs: Place Orders](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/orders/create)

**APIs and rate limits**
- [SECONDARY] API surface: Gamma API (market metadata), Data API, CLOB API (REST + WebSocket); Gamma, Data and the CLOB price/order-book endpoints are public; order placement is geo-restricted (e.g., blocked from the US). — [dwellir guide](https://www.dwellir.com/guides/polymarket-api-guide); [totalis.trade](https://www.totalis.trade/blogs/prediction-markets/polymarket-api); [quantvps](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/polymarket-us-api-available)
- [OFFICIAL] WebSocket: four channels — market, user, sports, RTDS; only the user channel needs authentication (subscribes by condition IDs); endpoints `wss://ws-subscriptions-clob.polymarket.com/ws/market` and `/ws/user`, RTDS at `wss://ws-live-data.polymarket.com`. — [Polymarket docs: WebSocket overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/websocket/overview)
- [OFFICIAL] Market channel requires a client heartbeat every 10 s; on the sports socket the server pings every 5 s and the client must pong within 10 s. — [Polymarket docs: WSS market reference](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/wss/market); [WebSocket overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/websocket/overview). Heartbeat intervals for the user channel and RTDS were not confirmed from official pages (a third-party client uses 30 s for CLOB and 5 s for RTDS).
- [OFFICIAL] Rate limits are enforced by Cloudflare with burst and sustained windows; over-limit requests are throttled (delayed/queued) rather than immediately rejected. Published values: general 15,000 req/10 s; CLOB general 9,000 req/10 s; `POST /order` and `DELETE /order` 5,000 req/10 s burst and 120,000 req/10 min sustained; `POST /orders` (batch) 2,000 req/10 s burst and 21,000 req/10 min sustained; API-key endpoints 100 req/10 s. — [Polymarket docs: rate limits](https://docs.polymarket.com/quickstart/introduction/rate-limits); [rate-limits.md](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/rate-limits.md)
- [OFFICIAL] Changelog: `POST /order` burst limit set to 5,000 per 10 s (500/s) in April 2026. — [Polymarket docs: predictions changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/predictions)
- [SECONDARY] Conflicts: one guide still shows a 48,000 per 10 min sustained cap (apparently outdated); another claims per-signer token buckets on top of the published limits and that eventual 429s carry no retry headers. — [polytest.io](https://www.polytest.io/blog/polymarket-api-rate-limits); [agentbets.ai](https://agentbets.ai/guides/polymarket-rate-limits-guide/); [predictefy](https://blog.predictefy.com/polymarket-api-rate-limits) (attribution not pinned). The changelog snippet also mentions a "500 per 10 s burst / 3,000 per 10 min sustained" limit for an endpoint I could not identify — [changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog).
- [OFFICIAL] Builder programme: builder code attached to CLOB orders for attribution; a Relayer API key enables gasless wallet operations; relayer transactions are capped at 100/day (Unverified tier), 10,000/day (Verified), unlimited for the top tier, which also gets the highest API rate limits. — [Polymarket docs: builder tiers](https://docs.polymarket.com/builders/tiers); [builders overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/builders/overview.md). [COMMUNITY] Relayer API keys can be generated at polymarket.com/settings and used instead of builder keys — [rs-builder-relayer-client](https://docs.rs/crate/rs-builder-relayer-client/0.1.3).

- [SECONDARY/COMMUNITY] A 500 ms taker delay on crypto markets was removed on 18 Feb 2026, without advance notice, at the same time dynamic taker fees arrived. — [BlockBeats](https://m.theblockbeats.info/en/news/61326); [AiCoin](https://www.aicoin.com/en/article/518164); [X thread](https://x.com/KyleDeWriter/status/2024905555119145082)

- [OFFICIAL] Crypto-market taker delay cut from 250 ms to 50 ms effective 17 Aug 2026 11:00 UTC ("we'll closely monitor liquidity and market quality … and adjust as needed"). — [Polymarket Developers on X](https://x.com/PolymarketDevs/status/2089295325660172578); [changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/predictions)
- [OFFICIAL + SECONDARY] The changelog lists a further change raising the delay from 50 ms to 150 ms effective 4 Sep 2026 14:00 UTC; a third-party guide describes the current crypto delay as 150 ms. Documentation lagged: on 24 Aug 2026 the order-lifecycle page still said 250 ms. — [changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/predictions); [polybot.trading](https://polybot.trading/blog/polymarket-taker-delay-250ms-to-50ms)
- [OFFICIAL] Sports: a per-market configurable delay can apply around live play; a marketable order waits out the market's window, is pending and non-cancellable, and is rejected (not matched) if market/balance/allowance/risk checks fail when the delay expires. — [Polymarket docs: order lifecycle](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle)
- [OFFICIAL] Help centre (article roughly April 2026): sports markets carry a 3-second delay on marketable orders, a 1-second taker delay was being tested on NBA and MLB, and all outstanding limit orders are automatically cancelled at the official game start (start times can shift). — [Polymarket help: limit orders](https://help.polymarket.com/en/articles/13364444-limit-orders)

### Inferences
- Consolidated 2025-2026 change log for the international venue (dates as sourced above): Dec 2025 own-chain intent disclosed → Jan 2026 first taker fees (15-min crypto) → 18 Feb 2026 500 ms crypto taker delay removed; sports fees start (NCAAB, Serie A) → 6 Mar 2026 fees on all new crypto markets → 30 Mar 2026 fees on nearly all categories → Apr 2026 order rate-limit raise → 28 Apr 2026 CLOB V2 + pUSD → by Jun 2026 250 ms taker delay (crypto + finance up/down), 5 Jun orders locked during delay → 10 Jul 2026 sports θ 0.03→0.05 (single source) → 17 Aug 2026 delay 50 ms → 4 Sep 2026 delay 150 ms → 20 Nov 2026 θ cuts + rebate-tier overhaul. Roughly one rule change per month: any backtest older than a few weeks uses the wrong cost model.
- With `fee = C·θ·p·(1−p)`, the fee equals θ·(1−p) of the premium paid and θ·p of the maximum gross profit of a buyer. Examples per share: θ = 0.01 → 0.25¢ at 50¢, 0.09¢ at 90¢, 0.0475¢ at 95¢; θ = 0.05 → 1.25¢ at 50¢, 0.45¢ at 90¢, 0.2375¢ at 95¢. Buying a 95¢ "near-certain" share costs ~0.95% of the 5¢ upside at θ = 0.01 but ~4.75% at θ = 0.05.
- After 20 Nov 2026 the international venue is the cheapest of the three main venues for takers in θ = 0.01 categories (vs Kalshi 0.07 and Polymarket US 0.0695-0.095, sections 2-3); in sports/election/finance it is ~29% cheaper than Kalshi (0.05 vs 0.07) before rebates.
- Rebates are now irrelevant for small automated strategies: $200k of weighted volume needs 800,000 shares traded at 50¢ (≈$400k premium) or ~2.2M shares at 90¢ (≈$2.0M premium) per 30 days, all as fee-paying taker (or maker) fills.
- The taker delay is asymmetric (it delays and locks marketable orders; nothing found says maker cancels are delayed), i.e., it works like a maker-protecting speed bump in crypto and live-sports markets. A strategy whose decision latency is seconds (LLM inference) is not harmed by 150 ms but cannot win stale-quote races there either; in live sports a 1-3 s lock means the order may execute against a book that has already repriced, or be rejected.
- Published order limits (500 orders/s burst, 200/s sustained) are far above what an LLM-paced strategy needs; the binding constraints are the relayer cap (100 gasless transactions/day for unverified builders), on-chain approvals/wrapping after V2, and geo-restrictions.
- Any bot code or tutorial written before 28 Apr 2026 (V1 exchange, USDC.e collateral, old signatures) is obsolete.

## 2. Polymarket US (CFTC-regulated venue via the QCEX acquisition): how it differs

### Takeaway
Polymarket US is a separate, KYC-gated, CFTC-regulated exchange with its own documentation and API (`docs.polymarket.us`), rulebook-based central settlement through Polymarket Clearing (no UMA) and a much higher, Kalshi-style fee curve: since early November 2026 the taker coefficient is tiered from 0.095 (under $10k of 30-day taker volume) down to 0.0695 ($250k+), makers pay nothing, and a temporary 70% taker-fee rebate applies to straight API trades from 21 Nov to 31 Dec 2026. It reported $14.77B of October 2026 volume; nothing found indicates that its order books are shared with the international venue, and the separate stacks (USD-cleared DCM vs on-chain pUSD CLOB) point to separate books.

### Inferences
- The US venue's economics for a small automated taker are the worst of the three main venues outside the promo window: Θ = 0.095 means 2.375¢ per contract at 50¢ (4.75% of premium) per side. ## 3. Kalshi: fees, APIs, rate limits, incentives, limits, collateral/margin, interest, latency

### Takeaway
Kalshi's fee curve was stable through 2026 — taker `0.07 × C × P × (1−P)` (max $1.75 per 100 contracts), maker `0.0175 × C × P × (1−P)` only on roughly 160 designated series (otherwise free), no settlement fee — and it pays 3.50% APY on cash plus open positions. Access is via REST, WebSocket and FIX with token-bucket rate tiers (Basic ≈ 20 reads and 10 order operations per second), matching is sharded by category on AWS with no documented speed bump, positions remain fully collateralised (a margin regime for self-clearing institutions was filed on 22 Sep 2026 and is still pending), and several CFTC-filed liquidity programmes exist whose market-maker terms are mostly not public.

### Inferences
- Per-contract taker cost at θ = 0.07: 1.75¢ at 50¢ (3.5% of premium), 0.63¢ at 10¢ or 90¢, 0.33¢ at 95¢. A taker round trip at mid-prices costs ~3.5¢ per contract, so short-horizon taker strategies need more than 3.5 points of edge; holding to settlement saves the exit fee because there is no settlement fee. Buying 95¢ contracts gives up 6.65% of the 5¢ gross upside to the fee (θ·p).
- For an LLM-paced strategy the Basic tier (~10 order operations/s) is ample; rate tiers, FIX, PrivateLink and shard-aware routing only matter for market making and latency-sensitive taking, where there is no speed bump to protect slower participants — the opposite design choice from Polymarket's crypto/sports delays.
- 3.50% APY on cash and open positions lowers the carry cost of long-dated positions on Kalshi relative to venues that pay nothing (Polymarket international pays 4% only on a small set of designated markets).
- Maker fees on ~160 (mostly sports) series mean passive quoting is not free exactly where volume is concentrated; a maker at 50¢ pays 0.44¢ per contract on those series.
- Margin, if approved, would be institutional-only and exclude sports/culture/mention markets, so retail/API bots should assume full collateralisation.

## 4. Resolution: mechanisms, dispute windows, time to payout, frequency and examples of contested resolutions (2024-2026)

### Takeaway
Polymarket international still resolves through UMA's managed optimistic oracle (whitelisted proposers, ~2 hours if unchallenged, days if escalated to a UMA token vote with no appeal), and disputes are frequent and rising — more than 1,150 disputed markets in January-May 2026, with about half of voting power in nine wallets and a planned voting revamp postponed. Polymarket US and Kalshi resolve centrally under CFTC-filed rulebooks (Kalshi usually pays within hours; its Outcome Review Committee decides within 24 hours and there is no independent appeal), so on every venue the exact rule text and the resolver's discretion are a first-order risk for automated strategies.

- [SECONDARY, headline only] The same real-world event can produce different winners on Kalshi and Polymarket because rule wording differs. — [oddsshopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-vs-polymarket-settlement-rules)

### Inferences
- Capital lock-up after the event: Polymarket international ≥ ~2 h after a proposal (plus the time until someone proposes), up to several days when disputed; Kalshi typically hours, up to a day or more under review. "Near-certain outcome" strategies must price both the time value and a non-zero probability of an adverse or delayed resolution.
- More than 1,150 disputes in five months is roughly 230 disputed markets per month; with ~0.2% of contracts reaching a vote, the tail risk is small per market but concentrated in exactly the ambiguous, high-volume markets where apparent mispricing is largest.
- Because the dominant UMA voters are few and may hold positions, resolution risk on the international venue is partly a counterparty/governance risk rather than a purely informational one; on Kalshi and Polymarket US it is discretion risk of a regulated operator with no independent appeal.
- An LLM-driven strategy needs the full rule text, clarifications and resolution source per venue as model input; cross-venue "arbitrage" on identically titled markets is exposed to divergent settlement.
