## 1. Empirical speed of price adjustment: how fast do Polymarket / Kalshi prices incorporate news, and is there capturable post-news drift?

### Takeaway
On liquid, high-attention Polymarket markets, public breaking news is impounded in seconds to tens of seconds (first trade ~7 s, price >90% within ~31–71 s in the two best-documented November 2026 cases), and in some cases buying starts before the public alert. Evidence for exploitable post-news drift is mixed-to-negative for headline markets, while slower/incomplete adjustment is documented for thin markets (Kalshi live NBA) and most long-tail markets are simply stale and illiquid rather than "slow but tradable".

### Cited Findings

**Second-level case evidence (best direct measurements found; single events, Nov 2026)**
- **[J]** Protos reconstructed Polymarket's reaction to Nvidia's CEO-transition news second by second: the press release crossed GlobeNewswire at 8:48:12am; the first buy came at 8:48:19am (~7 s later); the price passed 90% at 8:48:43am (~31 s after release); it reached 99–100% by 8:50am. — [Protos](https://protos.com/how-quickly-did-polymarket-react-to-nvidia-news/)

- **[J/U]** On the later reinstatement news (reported 25 Nov 2026), Polymarket odds on Huang's return went from 4¢ to 90¢ within 71 seconds and traded at 99¢ 221 seconds after the first purchase. — [Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/11/25/polymarket-huang-return/); [KuCoin flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-bets-on-jensen-huang-s-return-surge-in-221-seconds); [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/market/33806955/)

**Academic microstructure evidence (2026 working papers)**
- **[P]** Akey, Grégoire, Harvie & Martineau (SSRN, posted 1 Oct 2026, 74 pp.; complete Polymarket on-chain trades plus tick-level top-of-book quotes): no trade occurs in 99% of one-minute intervals, yet those intervals account for 76% of price variation; a VAR attributes at most 4% of efficient-price innovation variance to trades, with quote updates driving several times more price discovery; spreads are tightest and books deepest near $0 and $1 and widest/thinnest around $0.50; most markets have little to no trading and the last trade's accuracy worsens with staleness. — [SSRN 7547106](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7547106)
- **[P]** Gómez-Cram, Guo, Jensen & Kung (working paper; Polymarket trades 2023–2025): roughly 3% of traders account for most price discovery; skilled traders react to news more quickly; the informed group (market makers plus skilled takers) is under 3.5% of accounts but takes over 30% of total gains, while about 67% of accounts absorb most of the losses. — [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders); [CoinDesk, 26 Apr 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/26/only-3-of-traders-drive-prediction-markets-accuracy-not-the-crowd-study-finds); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/prediction-market-showcase-widsom-of-an-informed-minority-not-the-crowd-study); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study)
- **[P]** Angelini & De Angelis (arXiv 2606.07811, 2026; live Kalshi NBA contracts, one-minute quotes merged with timestamped play-by-play): prices move in the expected direction after scoring events, lead changes and runs, but **underreact on impact** relative to a public-information benchmark; salient events are absorbed fairly quickly in liquid markets, while thin markets adjust less completely; "illiquidity … shapes the speed with which public information is incorporated into prices"; "informativeness is not instantaneous efficiency". — [arXiv 2606.07811](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.07811v1)
- **[P]** Tsang & Yang (arXiv 2603.03152 / SSRN, Feb 2026; Polymarket on-chain ledger; Biden–Trump debate, Trump assassination attempt, Biden dropout; price adjustment measured in 5-minute event time): within four weeks the same Trump-YES market produced reversal, persistence and almost no price movement despite heavy trading; trading rises after each shock, especially among incumbent traders with pre-event exposure against Trump, who are also more likely to flip positions. — [arXiv 2603.03152](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03152); [SSRN 6248859](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6248859)
- **[P] (disconfirming for drift)** Valentin, "Right Before the News" (SSRN, Aug 2026; 64 single-stock corporate events): in 28 events Polymarket was already at least 80% priced on the correct side before the news existed ("the signature we find is anticipation, not speed"); a pre-registered 18-cell macro test finds nothing significant; **the post-release convergence trade loses 72–82% of stake**; buying the stock on Polymarket's confidence lost money on 17 of 22 trades. — [SSRN 7234098](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7234098)
- **[P] (disconfirming for drift; snippet-level, authors not identified in-session)** A conference paper on public learning from decentralized prediction markets reports that skilled flow has a contemporaneous price impact "without significant subsequent continuation or reversal". — [AFA program page](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=267904)
- **[P]** Ng, Peng, Tao & Zhou, "Price Discovery and Trading in Modern Prediction Markets": coverage reports that large trades were predictive of returns, with price discovery concentrated in markets with large directional "conviction" trades (a different emphasis from Akey et al.'s quote-driven result). — [SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5331995.pdf?abstractid=5331995&mirid=1); [seminar page](https://fss.um.edu.mo/fss-deco-seminar-price-discovery-and-trading-in-modern-prediction-markets/)
- **[P] (attribution of authorship uncertain)** A cross-venue study of the 2024 election finds Betfair and William Hill each held 30–45% of price discovery in the early campaign period while Polymarket contributed 5–10%. — ["Price Discovery Across Political Prediction Markets"](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)

### Inferences
- Combining Akey et al. (price discovery through quote updates, not trades) with the Protos timings: on liquid markets news is impounded mainly by makers cancelling and re-quoting, so a taker arriving "seconds late" mostly finds a moved book rather than stale quotes to hit. The capturable window on a headline market is roughly the first 5–30 seconds, and only with a wire-speed feed.
- Buying that starts 11 s before the fastest public X aggregator posts implies the earliest movers hold either a faster licensed feed (terminal/wire) or non-public information. A "react after the public headline" strategy competes against both and cannot tell them apart in real time.
- Election nights show that the tradable "news" is granular data (vote counts), which is modelled continuously; the formal call arrives hours after the price is at 99%. "React to the race call" is not a trade. The same logic likely applies to any outcome that becomes statistically obvious before it becomes official.
- For scheduled macro decisions the outcome is typically pre-priced (Kalshi's day-before record), so reaction trades only exist on genuine surprises, where numeric parsers are the natural tool.
- Drift evidence splits by liquidity: negative/absent in headline markets (Valentin; AFA snippet), present as underreaction in thin live markets (Angelini & De Angelis). But thin-market underreaction was measured at one-minute granularity against a model benchmark, and exploitability after spreads, fees and taker delays is not established.
- Long-tail markets: Akey et al.'s "most markets have little to no trading" suggests displayed last-trade prices can be stale for long periods, but with wide spreads and thin books the opportunity is capacity-constrained and is better described as "post a smarter quote" than "snipe a stale price".

## 2. Latency competition: incumbents, data feeds, reaction times, and platform countermeasures

### Takeaway
The speed layer is occupied by professional market makers (Susquehanna, Jump, DRW, Wintermute) and a small set of bot-like wallets (≈5% of wallets ≈ 75% of Polymarket volume) using paid low-latency inputs (a millisecond Truth Social feed at up to $100,000/month, press wires, official sports data). Platforms have added taker-side speed bumps — Polymarket's 1–3 s non-cancellable delay on live sports, a 250 ms hold on some up/down markets, book wipes at game start — which protect makers and further penalise late-arriving takers.

### Inferences
- A sports taker on Polymarket faces: data-feed disadvantage versus official-feed/in-venue bots, plus a 1–3 s forced wait during which the order cannot be cancelled while makers remain free to re-quote. That is an adverse-selection machine pointed at slow takers; an LLM adds latency and no information in this niche.
- If Kalshi macro contracts halt before the release (to be verified per contract), the "react to the number" trade does not exist on Kalshi at all for those contracts; it exists only on venues that stay open through the release.
- The paid Truth Social feed formalises a two-tier latency structure for posts by key public figures: licensed millisecond delivery for institutions versus push/web for everyone else. A retail-grade LLM pipeline reading the public site or X is structurally behind for the highest-value account(s).
- Reaction times of the leaders in unscheduled text news look like seconds (7 s to first trade in the one measured press-release case), not microseconds. ## 3. LLM latency and cost budget; where LLM comprehension adds value versus where it is too slow

### Takeaway

## 4. Resolution-rule edge: "event happened but not yet resolved", tail-end / bond / 尾盘 strategies — measured returns and blow-ups

### Takeaway
Aggregate data show a small positive average edge for buying near-certain outcomes (contracts priced ≥90¢ earned about 0.83¢ per dollar on Polymarket in one 2026 preprint), but no rigorous annualized return for the tail-end (尾盘) strategy was found, and practitioner audits plus 2025–2026 dispute cases show the edge is consumed by dispute lock-ups, resolution-wording surprises and rare reversals. Disputes are rising (reported >1,150 in January–May 2026) and are decided by a concentrated, conflicted UMA voter set.

### Inferences
- At ~0.8¢ per dollar average for ≥90¢ contracts, the gross edge is thinner than a single percentage point of mis-estimated tail probability; whether it survives 2026 taker fees is unverified. The strategy is short tail risk with a payoff ratio near 1:19 to 1:99.
- Annualized figures are driven almost entirely by assumed capital turnover (for example ~1% per ~2-hour Proposed window). Dispute lock-ups and reversals attack exactly that assumption, which is why the Hubble audit and the "1800%" headline can both be "true" under different assumptions. Neither is a verified, cost-inclusive return.
- The Strategy case is the cleanest illustration of the resolution-rule trap: the event did happen inside the window, yet the market paid "No" because of how the rule was read. An LLM here is useful as a rule reader and precedent checker (flagging wording such as "announced" versus "occurred", named sources, deadlines) and as a screen for dispute-prone categories (politics and geopolitics in the Hubble data), not as a speed tool.
- Because disputes are adjudicated by voters who often hold positions, "correct reading of the rules" is necessary but not sufficient; the realised outcome is a prediction about a vote. That caps position size for any resolution-stage strategy.
- Weather-market evidence that favorites get more overpriced in the final hours warns that late-stage favorite buying is negative-EV in at least one category; the tail-end edge is category-dependent.

- **[J]** Polymarket's own AI feature reportedly fed users fabricated information. — [CJR Tow Center](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/polymarkets-ai-is-feeding-users-fabricated-information.php)

### Inferences
- The absence of audited news-bot P&L, set against abundant marketing claims and a documented campaign of staged winning bets, means public success stories carry close to zero evidential weight. The credible evidence says gains accrue to a small, fast, well-capitalised minority.
- A fast LLM news reactor is a high-value target: it converts unverified text into orders within seconds, in markets where manufacturing a 5-point move is priced at under $1M and where a planted article reliably shifts LLM forecasts. Source allow-listing, multi-source confirmation and size caps are core requirements, and each costs latency — the "smarter" pipeline is pushed further into the slow lane.
- Model monoculture (many agents on similar models reacting identically) suggests LLM-driven reaction trades will crowd on the same side at the same moment, steepening adverse selection for the slower copies.

## 6. Analogous evidence from other asset classes: LLM news trading in equities / crypto and alpha decay

### Takeaway
In equities, LLM-read news predicted next-day returns (Lopez-Lira & Tang: about 34 bps/day long-short before costs), with the effect strongest in small stocks and after negative news and declining as LLM adoption rises. Look-ahead-free 2025–2026 benchmarks find that LLM trading agents earn no alpha once headlines are replayed at publication time, with headline news priced within minutes. The implication for prediction markets is that a comprehension edge survives only in low-attention, limits-to-arbitrage corners and shrinks as adoption spreads.

### Cited Findings
- **[P]** Lopez-Lira & Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements?" (arXiv 2304.07619; first posted April 2023, v6 October 2025): a daily-rebalanced long-short strategy on GPT-4's assessment of overnight news earned an average 34 bps per day before transaction costs (an earlier version reported a pre-cost Sharpe ratio of 3.8); predictability is stronger among smaller stocks and following negative news; **strategy returns decline as LLM adoption rises**, consistent with improved price efficiency and framed as a limits-to-arbitrage effect; simpler models (GPT-1, GPT-2, BERT) could not forecast returns. — [arXiv 2304.07619v6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)
- **[P]** Anic, Barbon, Seiz & Zarattini, "ChatGPT in Systematic Investing" (2025): LLM news scores improve cross-sectional momentum on S&P 500 stocks, with the informational edge strongest in particularly news-sensitive situations. — [arXiv 2510.26228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228v1)
- **[P] (via aggregator listing; verify on arXiv)** "The Only Edge Was Memorized in Training: LLM Trading Agents Around Earnings and Bad News" (arXiv 2611.22177, Nov 2026): headline news moves prices within minutes, and a benchmark replaying headlines at publication time finds no trading alpha; any residual edge sits around known-date events such as earnings and after sharp selloffs. — [awesomepapers.io listing](https://awesomepapers.io/ai-agents/papers/2611.22177)
- **[P]** "LLM Trading Agents Earn Beta, Not Alpha: A Look-Ahead-Free Benchmark" (arXiv 2611.19530, Nov 2026): prices 2026 news events with hourly, 5-minute and 1-minute bars; the authors concede that trading at the headline's minute is optimistic because faster traders act in milliseconds; the minute-level study covers ten stocks and one earnings season; the models shown have negative alpha. — [arXiv 2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)
- **[P]** "Profit Mirage" (arXiv 2510.07920): LLM financial agents' strong backtests largely disappear one step beyond the training cutoff — the agents are recalling history rather than trading on new information. A 2026 position paper ("The Alpha Illusion") argues reported alpha from end-to-end LLM trading systems should not be treated as deployment evidence. — [arXiv 2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.07920); [alphaXiv 2605.16895](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16895v1)
- **[U]** Industry commentary (April 2026): LLMs may accelerate alpha decay by making copyable signals spread faster. — [IBKR Campus](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-quant-news/llms-and-the-shortening-shelf-life-of-copyable-alpha/)
- **[P] (intersection with settlement mechanics)** Dai, Jia & Yu, "Settlement Manipulation in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2606.31675, June 2026): after Polymarket launched five-minute Bitcoin contracts, settlement-time spot order flow spiked with post-settlement reversals; secondary summaries disagree on the dollar transfer (about $1.28M versus $7.6M retail losses), so treat amounts as unverified. — [arXiv 2606.31675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31675)

### Inferences
- The equity evidence maps onto prediction markets in three ways: (1) LLM comprehension did carry information, but at a next-day horizon and in small, neglected names — the analogue is long-tail and rule-complex markets, not headline markets; (2) the edge decays with adoption, and 2026 prediction markets already show heavy bot participation; (3) at intraday horizons with realistic timestamps, LLM agents do not beat faster traders — matching the 7–31 s repricing observed on Polymarket.
- Backtest contamination applies with full force to any "LLM would have traded this news" claim on prediction markets: models may have memorised outcomes, and replay timing is usually optimistic. Only live, timestamped, out-of-sample records count.
- Limits to arbitrage are what preserve a slow edge. In prediction markets those limits are small books, capital lock-up until resolution, fees and resolution risk — the same frictions that cap the profit. The surviving niche is real but small per market, so it needs breadth across many markets, which is what an LLM pipeline is suited for.
