## 1. Data transparency: what trader-level data is public, and how fast can an outsider see a fill?

### Takeaway
On Polymarket international, every wallet's trades, positions, activity and PnL ranking are readable without authentication, and a fill can be tied to a wallet within roughly 1–3 seconds. Polymarket US and Kalshi publish only anonymous market data, so systematic wallet-following is technically possible only on Polymarket international.

### Cited Findings

**Polymarket international**
- The official CLI lists "Public data — no wallet needed" commands for any address: `data positions`, `closed-positions`, `value`, `traded`, `trades`, `activity`; per-market `holders` and `open-interest`; `leaderboard --period month --order-by pnl`; plus `profiles get 0x…` (README read Nov 2026). [A][full] — [Polymarket/polymarket-cli](https://github.com/Polymarket/polymarket-cli)
- The official Rust SDK describes its `data` feature as "Data API client for positions, trades, leaderboards, and analytics". [A][full] — [Polymarket/rs-clob-client](https://github.com/Polymarket/rs-clob-client)
- The official real-time stream has an `activity` topic with `trades` and `orders_matched` message types that need no auth. Each `Trade` message carries `proxyWallet`, `name`, `pseudonym`, `side`, `size`, `price`, `timestamp` and `transactionHash`. The client package is now marked deprecated in favour of a unified SDK. [A][full] — [Polymarket/real-time-data-client](https://github.com/Polymarket/real-time-data-client)
- Official docs describe a Data API (v2) with positions, trades and leaderboards; the leaderboard docs note that `volume` is in shares, not USDC. [A][snip] — [Data API overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/data-api/overview.md); [leaderboard endpoint](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/boards/get-the-trader-leaderboard.md)
- An open-source tracker documents the anonymous trades query `https://data-api.polymarket.com/v2/trades`: wallet-bearing rows, 10,000-row pages, a published limit of 200 requests per 10 seconds, and a `takerOnly` switch. It states the v1 URL was retired on 2026-10-24. It polls every 5 seconds by default and says two pages cover "roughly five to seven minutes at observed rates". [C][full] — [pselamy/polymarket-insider-tracker](https://github.com/pselamy/polymarket-insider-tracker)
- Measured latency (vendor, Sep–Oct 2026): the order-book WebSocket lags the exchange by 17 ms on average. On-chain settlement on Polygon arrives a median 2.0 s after matching (p10 1.1 s, p90 3.0 s, 400 transactions). Orders and cancels are off-chain, so the mempool gives no early look. [C][full] — [OrcaLayer mechanics notes](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/05-polymarket-mechanics-facts.md)
- Taker orders are held 1 s on football markets (0 on NBA) and 250 ms on certain crypto and finance up/down markets, and cannot be cancelled while pending; resting maker orders are not delayed (checked Sep–Oct 2026). [C][full] — [OrcaLayer mechanics notes](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/05-polymarket-mechanics-facts.md)
- On-chain pitfalls: the exchange emits an `OrderFilled` event for both sides and routes one leg through ten operator wallets, so naive sums double-count volume (about 2x) and mislabel maker and taker. The Goldsky order-book subgraph stopped indexing around 28 April 2026. [C][full] — [reproduction methodology](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders/blob/main/docs/METHODOLOGY.md)
- A hobby copy bot polling on a 5-minute trigger recorded a median copy delay of 5.6 minutes (p90 9.6), which shows that realised delay depends on the copier's own polling, not on data availability. [C][full] — [BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader)

**Polymarket US**
- The official Polymarket US Python SDK lists as public (no auth) only events, markets, order book, best bid/offer, search, series and sports. Positions, activity and balances are authenticated and return the caller's own account. RFQ trade prints are described as "anonymous". There is no endpoint for another user's positions or trades and no leaderboard endpoint (README read Nov 2026). [A][full] — [Polymarket/polymarket-us-python](https://github.com/Polymarket/polymarket-us-python)

**Kalshi**
- Kalshi's public trade feed shows size, price and taker side but never who traded; accounts are KYC'd and limited to one per user. A public leaderboard is described as opt-in, and traders can post chosen positions on "Kalshi Social". These statements come from analytics vendors and bloggers, not Kalshi documentation. [C/D][snip] — [Allium](https://www.allium.so/blog/kalshi-vs-polymarket-two-roads-to-the-same-bet/); [Launchpoly](https://launchpoly.com/blog/kalshi-whale-tracking); [Polyburg](https://polyburg.com/compare/kalshi-copy-trading); [Substack](https://whirligigbear.substack.com/p/are-traders-on-kalshi-being-profiled)
- One source notes that on Polymarket's offshore venue you can see wallet IDs for executed trades but not the identity behind resting orders. [C][snip] — [Substack](https://whirligigbear.substack.com/p/are-traders-on-kalshi-being-profiled)

### Inferences
- The fastest identity-bearing signals are the public `activity/trades` stream and Polygon settlement. With a 2.0 s median settlement lag plus the copier's own order latency (and a 1 s taker hold in football), a realistic floor for an automated copier is about 1–5 seconds after the leader's fill. The price move itself is visible in tens of milliseconds, before the wallet is.
- A leader's resting limit orders are not attributable, so a copier only learns of a maker-style leader after the fill, when the price the leader got is usually gone.
- Tools that claim Kalshi "copy trading" can only be using opt-in or self-published data, not a complete trade tape.
- 2026 brought API churn (Data API v1 retired, subgraph halted, client deprecated), which is an operational risk for any self-built 跟单 pipeline.

### Gaps
- Latency of the Data API and the `activity` stream relative to matching was not found; only the book WebSocket and on-chain lag were measured.
- Kalshi's own documentation on leaderboard and profile visibility, and whether the Polymarket US app shows any leaderboard or public profile, could not be checked (fetch blocked, search budget exhausted).
- Whether Polymarket plans any privacy feature for wallets or positions was not researched.

## 2. Persistence of skill: PnL distribution, concentration, out-of-sample persistence, and who the "top wallets" are

### Takeaway
Profits are extremely concentrated and roughly 70% of accounts lose, while a small group (about 3% of accounts) shows statistically real skill. Forward-in-time persistence of wallets picked from leaderboards is weak to absent in the tests available, and many top wallets are liquidity providers or bots whose fills are not directional signals.

### Cited Findings

**Distribution and concentration**
- Akey, Grégoire, Harvie and Martineau (CEPR DP21615 / SSRN 6443103): Nov 2022–Mar 2026, over 2.4 million users, $67 billion of volume. The top 1% of users with positive PnL capture 76.5% of profits, rising to about 80% around the 2024 election. Successful traders provide liquidity with limit orders; unsuccessful traders take liquidity with market orders. [A][snip] — [CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/dp21615); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6443103); [OSC-hosted PDF](https://www.osc.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/research-grant-competition-2026-who-wins-and-who-loses-in-prediction-markets.pdf)
- Gómez-Cram, Guo, Jensen and Kung (LBS / Yale, SSRN 6617059; sample 2023–2025): 1.72 million accounts, 210,322 markets, about $13.76 billion of volume. Shares by class: skilled 3.16% (54,325 accounts; press reports 3.14%), lucky 28.93%, unlucky 61.66%, anti-skilled 6.15%; 1,660 accounts identified as market makers. [A][full for the reproduction's table of paper values, snip for the paper] — [reproduction](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6617059); [The Block, 26 Apr 2026](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2026-04-26-skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study-398902)
- Bloomberg analysis (28–29 Apr 2026): more than 100,000 accounts lost at least $1,000 between Jan 2025 and Apr 2026, nearly double the number that gained as much. About 800 bot-like accounts cleared more than $100,000 each, roughly $131 million in total. Suspected bots are about 5% of wallets and 75% of volume. Roughly 70% of traders lost money on a realised basis. [B][snip] — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/prediction-market-users-suffer-broad-losses-as-bots-reap-gains); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/crypto/articles/100-000-polymarket-accounts-booked-062234759.html); [Newser](https://www.newser.com/story/388532/polymarkets-biggest-winners-arent-human.html)
- Same coverage: the bottom 95% of users place 56% of their trades at extreme prices, against 28% for the top 0.1% of earners. [B][snip] — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/prediction-market-retail-traders-losses-bloomberg/)
- Solidus Labs (29 Apr 2026; politics markets, Dec 2025–Feb 2026, about $16 million of profits): 0.55% of profitable maker wallets captured half of maker gains and 0.26% of winning taker wallets a similar share. About 15% of volume in some markets looked like self-trading. Solidus sells the surveillance product behind the report and has a Kalshi deployment deal. [C][snip] — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260429164898/en/Polymarket-Under-the-Polygraph-Solidus-Report-Uncovers-Systemic-Risks-in-Onchain-Prediction-Markets); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/a-tiny-group-is-winning-on-polymarket-as-under-1-of-wallets-take-half-the-profits)
- A secondary blog cites an April 2026 on-chain analysis of 2.5 million wallets with about 15.9% profitable; method unknown. [D][snip] — [Tradox](https://tradoxvps.com/5-best-polymarket-strategies-for-beginner-traders-in-2026/)

**Persistence**
- Gómez-Cram et al. split events into training and test halves: 44% (press) or 46% (reproduction's citation of the paper) of accounts classed as skilled in training stay skilled in test, against about 10% for mutual funds in a parallel test; about 70% stay profitable; the train/test correlation of skill p-values is 0.35. [A][snip + full] — [news.bitcoin.com](https://news.bitcoin.com/polymarket-study-finds-3-14-drive-accuracy/); [reproduction](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders)
- An independent reproduction on public data (1,708,410 accounts, $12.6 billion) matches the classification (3.23% skilled) and the p-value correlation (0.34) but finds weaker persistence: 34% skilled-to-skilled and 66% still profitable. It splits on "a random half of the events", not on time. [C][full] — [reproduction README](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders); [REPORT.md](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders/blob/main/outputs/REPORT.md)
- The authors caution that it is uncertain whether the skilled traders keep participating as platforms grow and fees rise. [A][snip] — [gncrypto summary](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/study-3-14-percent-polymarket-accounts-drive-market-accuracy/)
- Akey et al.: monthly performance is only modestly (one version says weakly) persistent, and the authors say this may reflect selection, because many traders leave, rather than durable skill. They also find insider trading unlikely to explain the largest winners. [A][snip] — [OSC-hosted PDF](https://www.osc.ca/sites/default/files/2026-10/research-grant-competition-2026-who-wins-and-who-loses-in-prediction-markets.pdf); [Hacker News thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48221877)
- Jia and Sun (arXiv 2610.34754, submitted 26 Oct 2026, not yet peer reviewed) conclude that "historical profitability does not necessarily translate into persistent trading advantages or profitable copy trading". [A][snip, abstract only] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34754)
- Walk-forward test on sports (vendor, on-chain trades Aug 2025–Oct 2026): the month-to-month rank correlation of wallet results was between −0.03 and +0.15 in MLB, −0.09 and +0.03 in NFL, and −0.19 and +0.13 in college football. [C][full] — [OrcaLayer backtest](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-copy-trading-whales-backtest)
- A bot-directory study (2026) found a wallet list built one month earlier returned anywhere from +15.8% to −5.3% depending on the day it was measured. [C][snip] — [Polbots](https://polbots.com/guides/does-polymarket-copy-trading-work)

**Survivorship, inflated win rates and non-directional "top wallets"**
- PANews (4 Jan 2026) analysed about 27,000 trades of December's top-10 profit wallets. Displayed win rates exclude unresolved losing positions (僵尸单): SeriouslySirius falls from 73.7% to 53.3% and DrPufferfish from 83.5% to 50.9%; the best true win rate in the sample was about 57.6% (simonbanza). Profits came from position sizing and payoff ratio, and hedging used combinations of sports market types. [C][snip] — [BlockBeats repost](https://www.theblockbeats.info/news/60763); [Tencent News repost](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260104A05YI000)
- Polbots checked 407 wallets from Polymarket's top-100 leaderboards and judged only 36 copyable at all; it describes the monthly leaderboard as "mostly machines" whose liquidity-taking side would cost a copier the spread those wallets earn. [C][snip] — [Polbots](https://polbots.com/guides/does-polymarket-copy-trading-work)
- A Biteye article warns that leaderboard profits may be survivorship, that some tools' PnL figures can be off by several multiples, and that copying one leg of an arbitrage bot carries asymmetric risk. The exact page could not be identified from snippets. [D][snip] — one of [Odaily 5207604](https://www.odaily.news/zh-CN/post/5207604), [Odaily 5210040](https://www.odaily.news/zh-CN/post/5210040) or [MEXC repost](https://www.mexc.com/news/988541)
- BlockBeats argues the few stable accounts specialise in a niche where they have an information edge, for example temperature markets traded off NOAA forecasts. [D][snip] — one of [cnyes 6372686](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6372686) or [cnyes 6298156](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6298156)
- An individual's screen of the top-30 wallets by 30-day leaderboard PnL (against 30 random wallets) left only two wallets that passed skill, concentration, consistency and copyability filters. [C][full] — [BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader)
- Vendor research is unstable: Convexly withdrew its audit of the top 100 wallets in August 2026; its 10,000-wallet study is titled "Calibration Barely Predicts Profit". [D][snip] — [Convexly](https://www.convexly.app/research/polymarket-10k-wallet-study)

### Inferences
- Summing the Gómez-Cram classes gives about 32% of accounts with positive PnL (skilled plus lucky) and about 68% negative, which agrees with Bloomberg's roughly 70% losers. The 15.9% figure probably uses a different definition.
- The headline 44–46% persistence is not a tradable statistic. It comes from a random split of events, uses a classification that needs the full sample, and replicated at 34%. The forward-in-time tests (monthly rank correlation near zero in sports) are the relevant ones for a copier.
- Persistence at the level of "this account is skilled" coexists with non-copyability, because the skilled group earns largely through limit orders and timing (Akey et al.; OrcaLayer), which a follower cannot reproduce.
- Leaderboards ranked by PnL or win rate are biased three ways at once: survivorship, open losing positions excluded from win rates, and market makers, arbitrage bots and possible incentive farmers ranked alongside directional traders.

### Gaps
- Full-text numbers from Akey et al. (share of profitable users, persistence coefficients) and from Jia and Sun (persistence tables) were not retrieved.
- No source quantified how many leaderboard wallets are market makers, arbitrage bots or airdrop / liquidity-reward farmers beyond the Polbots 36-of-407 count and Bloomberg's bot estimates.
- No forward-in-time persistence test was found for politics or long-dated markets.
- Lead not re-verified this session: a Columbia study (Nov 2025) on Polymarket wash trading is known from earlier reporting; no 2026 update was retrieved.

## 3. Copy-trading performance under realistic latency, slippage and adverse selection

### Takeaway
The 2026 evidence does not support copying leaderboard or whale wallets as a reliably positive-expectancy strategy after fees: the average copied whale loses about 4–5% per trade in sports walk-forward tests, last month's top wallets revert to noise, and a public four-month paper log ended down 10%. The main cause is selection failure rather than latency, except for in-play informed wallets, whose real edge disappears within seconds.

### Cited Findings

**Academic**
- Jia and Sun (arXiv 2610.34754, Oct 2026) tested copying "smart money" wallets with order-book-aware paper trading and live on-chain execution and report that past profitability does not necessarily yield profitable copy trading. Their numerical results were not retrievable. [A][snip, abstract only] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34754)
- "The Anatomy of Polymarket" (arXiv 2603.03136) finds that large October 2024 whale bets on Trump were absorbed through volume rather than extreme price dislocation. It does not study copiers. [A][snip] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03136v1)

**Vendor walk-forward backtest (OrcaLayer, Issue 2, Oct 2026; on-chain trades Aug 2025–Oct 2026)** [C][full] — [repository](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-copy-trading-whales-backtest)
- Rule: copy a wallet's first buy of at least $20 at a price of 0.30–0.85, buying $10 at the wallet's price plus 1 cent, paying the sports taker fee `0.05 × p × (1 − p)`, holding to resolution. Wallets are picked on two months and tested on the next.
- MLB (799,542 copied buys): the top 10 go from +$2.72 to +$3.78 per $10 while picked to between −$2.23 and +$0.16 the next month.
- NFL (196,351): picked wallets between −$1.63 and +$1.46 per $10, no month with a t-statistic of 2; the average whale between −$0.07 and −$0.53.
- College football (123,561): average whale between −$0.28 and −$0.64; the November top 10 −$1.07 (t −2.8).
- League of Legends (891,617): pools of 54–89 wallets chosen by a strict profile screen; all 12 results negative across 6 pick dates and 2 rules, including the "consensus" rule that copies only when a second pool wallet buys the same outcome.
- Re-run through their public API on 800 MLB games (May–Sep 2026): top 10 fall from about +$3 per $10 to −$0.17, −$1.29 and +$1.06; the average whale loses −$0.55 (t −3.3), −$0.39 (t −2.3) and −$0.38 (t −1.8); 161,094 copies total −$83,012.60.
- Disclosed limits: five sports only; politics and long-dated markets not tested; the +1 cent copy price is optimistic for in-play markets; one disclosed look-ahead probably flatters the pools.
- Warning in the same document: "Smart Money" labels computed today on a wallet's full history already know the test months, so backtests that select by such labels look better than live.

**Copying apparently informed in-play wallets (OrcaLayer, Issue 1, football, Sep–Oct 2026)** [C][full] — [file 3](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/03-informed-traders-copy.md)
- 92 wallets selected in-sample for being positioned on the scoring side before goals earned +$2.33 per $10 at their own prices out of sample (t 6.8, n 10,198, +$23,752). A copy 1–5 seconds later earned −$0.10 per $10 (t −0.5, n 6,210).
- One of the best such wallets made 92% of its buys as maker fills. In a quarter of cases the ask was 9 cents higher in the same millisecond and 14 cents higher one second later.
- Look-ahead trap: selecting only buys that were followed by a goal showed +$4.23 per $10 (t 2.1, n 95); testing all 13,794 signals from the same 94 leaders gave −$0.41 per $10 (t −2.8, n 6,243).
- The authors state this covers second-scale in-play football only and does not show that following informed money is useless where information takes hours or days to be priced.

**Public paper-trading log (individual project, two screened leaders, $100 flat stake, $10,000 paper bankroll)** [C][full] — [README](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader); [state file](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader/blob/main/data/copy_state.json)
- README snapshot (12 Aug 2026): −$1,937 over 191 trades before an in-play filter (3–22 Jul), then +$2,108 over 81 trades (t 1.53). The author notes the trades the filter skipped would also have made +$1,485, so the turnaround reflects the leader's regime more than the filter.
- My computation from the state file last updated 3 Nov 2026: 612 closed copies, total PnL −$1,034.36 (−10.3% of bankroll), win rate 45.6%, mean −$1.69 per trade (t −0.36), maximum drawdown −$2,477.61.
- Same file: median entry price equalled the leader's volume-weighted price (mean gap +0.07 cents). Filling every copy at the leader's price would still have lost −$958.39, so entry slippage explains about 7% of the loss.
- Same file: 599 of 612 copies followed one leader; tennis was positive (ATP +$843, WTA +$1,443) and MLB, World Cup football, CS2 and LoL were negative; 1,211 entries were skipped as in-play.
- Stated limits: no queue position, market impact or adverse selection, so results are "a lower bound on copying costs"; no fee model.

- A 2026 Medium piece models a 0.6-second lag as about 0.98% slippage on top of about 5.34% in fees, and says its own figures are unverified and that no vendor publishes audited slippage data. [D][snip] — [Medium](https://medium.com/@shailamie/copy-trading-on-polymarket-is-a-spread-business-choose-your-bot-accordingly-2026-fa59cce51857)

**Fees a copier pays (Oct 2026)**
- Only takers pay: fee = shares × rate × p × (1 − p), with rates of 0.07 for crypto, 0.05 sports, 0.05 economics, 0.04 politics, 0.04 finance and 0 geopolitics. Makers pay nothing and receive 15–25% of taker fees as rebates. [C][full, citing Polymarket fee docs] — [OrcaLayer mechanics notes](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/05-polymarket-mechanics-facts.md)

### Inferences
- At p = 0.50 the taker fee is 1.0 cent per share in politics (2.0% of stake), 1.25 cents in sports (2.5%) and 1.75 cents in crypto (3.5%). Adding 1 cent of slippage (2% at 50 cents) gives a round-trip-free entry cost of about 4–5% for a hold-to-resolution copier in sports, which is about the size of the average-whale loss OrcaLayer measures. So the average large first buy has roughly zero edge before costs: size is not information.
- Three independent designs point the same way: selection by past results does not carry forward. In the paper log, slippage was negligible and the screened leaders still lost for the copier.
- Latency matters most where the edge is real (in-play, news-driven), and least where no edge exists, so a low-latency bot does not rescue a bad wallet list.
- The balance of evidence is negative for sports and for naive leaderboard copying, and untested rather than negative for slow markets (politics, long-dated events) and for narrow specialists (weather-type niches). No rigorous positive result was found for those either.
- OrcaLayer's screen drops the less profitable wallet from each pair that buys the same outcomes a median of 60 seconds or less apart on 10 or more markets. That such pairs exist is consistent with copy bots or one operator running several wallets, with the follower earning less.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study was found on whales exploiting followers (decoy trades, splitting across wallets, hedging elsewhere, selling into copier demand), or on widely followed wallets being front-run or counter-traded. - No measurement of the price impact of whale trades and its decay over minutes was found for 2026 markets.
- Jia and Sun's live-execution returns, slippage and sample size are the single most important missing numbers.
- No audited live (real-money) copy-trading track record from any bot vendor was found.

### Inferences
- Insider wallets are mostly one-off accounts (about 1% overlap with the persistently skilled group), so there is no stable list to follow. A follower must detect a new wallet in real time and then trade after its price impact.
- The statistical screens are not ex-ante signals. Mitts and Ofir use profitability as an input, and the 97.2% Orca win rate describes wallets classified after the fact. Neither says what a real-time alert earns, and no false-positive rate was found for any detector.
- The asymmetry is unfavourable: a large bet from a fresh wallet at long odds that is uninformed loses the follower's stake, while one that is informed has usually moved the price before a follower can act.
- Exposure for a follower is unsettled. No source addressed liability for copying publicly visible trades. The cases so far charge people who held and misused confidential information. Polymarket's rules do bar trading on illegally sourced information, platforms now refer accounts to prosecutors, and tools marketed as "insider trackers" make intent easy to allege. Deliberately tailing suspected insiders should not be presented as a recommended practice.

## 6. What an LLM can realistically add

### Takeaway
LLMs have documented uses around wallet following as cheap text classifiers, explainers and query interfaces, for example labelling about 219,000 market questions as insider-prone for roughly $20–45. Nothing found shows that LLM wallet profiling turns copy trading into a profitable strategy, and the binding constraints are data and selection, not language understanding.

### Cited Findings
- Market-context classification at scale: Gómez-Cram et al. label each market question as insider-prone (yes, unclear, no) with Claude Haiku at temperature 0. The reproduction sizes the job at about 219,000 unique questions, about 8,800 calls of 25 questions, at an estimated $20–45 on Haiku 4.5, with a label mix of about 10.6% yes, 0.4% unclear and 89% no (sports and crypto almost entirely no). [A][full via reproduction] — [insider classification notes](https://github.com/JasonJSant/polymarket-skilled-traders/blob/main/docs/insider_classification.md)
- Trade explanation: Jia and Sun describe a multi-agent framework that generates "interpretable and verifiable" explanations of individual smart-money trades, alongside behavioural clustering of wallet strategies by statistical methods. [A][snip, abstract only] — [arXiv 2610.34754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34754)
- Wallet Q&A through MCP: OrcaLayer offers a free MCP server so Claude can answer prompts such as listing the top 20 sports wallets with resolved-market counts and profit factor and flagging thin records. 0xInsider also lists an MCP server. [C][full] — [OrcaLayer backtest README](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-copy-trading-whales-backtest); [directory](https://github.com/sssobolevski/polymarket-bots)
- Products marketing AI wallet analysis (claims only): PolyTrak ("AI-powered trader analysis and copy-trading optimizer"), PolyWin ("AI trader discovery"), Virae AI (whale leaderboard with one-click copy), Rainmaker (AI-agent terminal across Polymarket, Kalshi and Novig), Alphascope and Polytragent (AI research). - Polymarket publishes an official open-source "Polymarket Agents" framework for LLM trading agents with retrieval over news and betting data; it is a forecasting and execution toolkit, not a wallet-following tool. [A][full] — [Polymarket/agents](https://github.com/Polymarket/agents)

Here's Guide How to Print Money", and a blog claiming whales are right 73% of the time with no source. [D][snip, titles only] — [X article](https://x.com/LunarResearcher/article/2038622884642398503); [DEV post](https://dev.to/storm_son_b44db572b250b68/polymarket-july-2026-ai-model-wars-heating-up-smart-money-positioning-330e)

[C/A][full / snip] — [pselamy tracker](https://github.com/pselamy/polymarket-insider-tracker); [arXiv 2605.00493](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00493v2)

### Inferences
- Realistic LLM roles, in order of evidence: (1) labelling markets by type, domain and susceptibility to private information; (2) summarising a wallet's history into a strategy description (maker versus taker, hedged pairs, domain specialisation) from structured statistics computed by code; (3) drafting a possible reason for a trade from market rules and news, for a human to check; (4) triaging alerts by context, for example discounting a "fresh wallet" alert in a market with no plausible private information.
- An LLM cannot observe intent, off-venue hedges, other wallets of the same operator, or resting orders. Any "reason for the trade" is a hypothesis that cannot be verified from on-chain data, and a fluent wrong rationale is a specific risk.
- The LLM adds nothing where the data is absent (Kalshi, Polymarket US), and it does not fix the core failure, which is that past results do not select future winners.
- The most defensible use is negative screening: explaining why a leaderboard wallet is not a directional signal (market maker, arbitrage leg, lucky concentration) so a user does not copy it.

### Gaps
- No product was found that demonstrably uses an LLM to explain individual trades in production, and no evaluation of the accuracy of LLM-generated trade rationales or wallet classifications was found.
- Jia and Sun's agent design and its verification method were not accessible.
- No paper was found using LLMs for anomaly detection on prediction-market order flow.

## 7. Practical constraints: capacity, slippage in thin markets, and the exit problem

### Takeaway
Even where a leader has an edge, a follower meets fees and taker delays on entry, cannot replicate maker fills, and learns of exits and hedges late or not at all. In the one public log available, entry slippage at $100 stakes was negligible, while results depended heavily on which positions were exited with the leader and which were held to resolution.

### Cited Findings
- Entry mechanics: taker fees of up to 1.75 cents per share at mid prices, a 1-second non-cancellable taker hold in football and 250 ms in some crypto and finance markets, no delay for makers (Oct 2026). [C][full] — [OrcaLayer mechanics notes](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/05-polymarket-mechanics-facts.md)
- Maker-fill leaders: a top informed wallet filled 92% as maker; when its fill was the last trade at the old price the ask was already 9 cents higher in the same millisecond. [C][full] — [OrcaLayer file 3](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-arbitrage-market-making-copy-trading-backtests/blob/main/03-informed-traders-copy.md)
- Depth versus leader size (my computation from the public log, Jul–Nov 2026, mostly tennis and other sports): the leader's median cost per copied position was $2,450 (p90 $8,988), and the recorded ask depth at the copier's entry had a median of 14,040 and a 10th percentile of 903 (units as recorded by the bot). [C][full] — [state file](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader/blob/main/data/copy_state.json)
- Skips in the same log: 1,211 in-play, 21 no book or extreme price, 14 "chase" (ask beyond the leader's price plus the limit), 2 thin book. [C][full] — [state file](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader/blob/main/data/copy_state.json)
- Exit handling in the same log: 91 positions closed when the leader cut below 50% of peak shares made +$1,696 in total (+$18.64 each), while 521 held to resolution lost −$2,730 (−$5.24 each). Median holding time was 4.6 hours. [C][full] — [state file](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader/blob/main/data/copy_state.json)
- The author lists exit latency as a limitation: exits wait until bid depth can absorb the position and resolution checks run once per cycle, so a position can be held past the price the leader got. [C][full] — [BallesJr README](https://github.com/BallesJr/polymarket-copy-trader)
- Chinese-language guides note that proportional copying can fall below the $1 minimum order for small accounts, and that a smart-money order can consume most of the visible book so followers cannot fill at the same price. The exact page could not be identified from snippets. [D][snip] — one of [Odaily 5207604](https://www.odaily.news/zh-CN/post/5207604) or [Odaily 5210040](https://www.odaily.news/zh-CN/post/5210040)
- Stress on entry price: OrcaLayer's MLB test adds a +3 cent variant to the +1 cent base and warns that +1 cent is optimistic in play. [C][full] — [OrcaLayer backtest](https://github.com/orcalayer/polymarket-copy-trading-whales-backtest)
- Hedged and multi-leg leaders: PANews finds top sports whales combine market types (totals, moneyline) rather than simple Yes/No pairs, so copying one leg misreads the position. [C][snip] — [BlockBeats repost](https://www.theblockbeats.info/news/60763)

### Inferences
- Capacity is small. A copier staking $100 behind a $2,450 leader position is invisible; a few dozen such copiers, or one large one, would equal the leader's own size and move thin books against themselves. No source measured this, so the capacity of any single followed wallet is unknown and probably in the low thousands of dollars per trade outside headline markets.
- The exit problem is structural. The follower sees an exit only after the leader's sell has printed, cannot see a hedge placed in another wallet or venue, and cannot tell a stop-out from profit-taking. The log's split between leader-exit and held-to-resolution outcomes shows how much of the result rides on exit rules the follower has to guess.
- A follower holding to resolution takes on resolution and dispute risk and capital lock-up that a leader who trades out does not.
- Combining sections 2, 3 and 7: for a small account the binding constraints are selection and fees; for a large account they are depth and signalling. Neither end has a documented positive-expectancy configuration as of November 2026.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of copier capacity, follower-induced price impact, or how many bots follow the most-watched wallets was found.
- No data on slippage for copy orders in politics or long-dated markets, or for stakes above $100, was found.
- No evidence on how quickly leaders' exits are observed and matched in practice beyond the single log above.
