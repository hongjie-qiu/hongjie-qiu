## 1. Within-market rebalancing (YES + NO ≠ $1; multi-outcome / negative-risk sums ≠ 1; split / merge / convert)

### Takeaway
This was the largest documented arbitrage pool historically (about $40M realized on Polymarket between April 2024 and April 2025 across within-market and cross-market types; the within-market share is believed to dominate, but the breakdown could not be re-verified this session), but by 2026 the measured gaps are small and last seconds: one 2026 order-book study found only 7 executable single-market episodes in 173 NBA games with a 3.6-second median life, and NegRisk violations last roughly 8–16 seconds at the median. The 2026 roll-out of taker fees (up to about 1.8 cents per share at 50 cents in crypto) removes most taker-side versions of the trade; detection is pure arithmetic, so an LLM adds nothing beyond helping write code.

### Cited Findings

**Mechanics (official documentation)**
- [A] Split and merge are the standard Conditional Token Framework operations: split converts pUSD collateral into one YES plus one NO token; merge converts a YES+NO pair back into pUSD. (Docs as retrieved in Nov 2026 denominate collateral in "pUSD".) — [Polymarket docs: CTF overview](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/ctf/overview)
- [A] In a negative-risk ("neg risk") event, one NO share in any market can be converted into one YES share in every other market of the event; the conversion is a single atomic call through the Neg Risk Adapter. The docs' example: one NO on "Other" in a three-outcome election converts into one YES each on the two named candidates. — [Polymarket docs: Negative risk](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/negative-risk); [Polymarket docs: Neg risk (advanced)](https://docs.polymarket.com/advanced/neg-risk)
- [A] "Augmented" neg risk handles outcomes added after market creation and changes how the "Other" outcome is defined, which matters for any sum-to-one arithmetic. — [Polymarket docs: Neg risk (advanced)](https://docs.polymarket.com/advanced/neg-risk)
- [A] The NegRiskAdapter audit confirms the design: the adapter converts certain NO positions into YES positions in other questions of the same market, minting the YES positions on the CTF contract. — [ChainSecurity audit of NegRiskAdapter](https://reports.chainsecurity.com/Polymarket/ChainSecurity_Polymarket_NegRiskAdapter_Audit.pdf)
- [C/D] A third-party model of the convert operation: converting NO positions on k outcomes (amount a each) returns YES on the outcomes not included plus (k − 1) × a in collateral. Unofficial; check against the adapter code before relying on it. — [Autonomous Finance: Polymarket NegRisk foundations](https://autonomous-finance-landing-page.vercel.app/research/en-US/polymarket-negrisk-foundations)
- [D] Practitioner framing of "convert arbitrage": if the sum of all YES prices in a neg-risk set is below $1, buying every YES locks in a profit at resolution. The official docs do not make an arbitrage claim. — [startpolymarket.com: converting negative risk](https://startpolymarket.com/learn/converting-negative-risk/)

**Measured size and frequency**
- [A] Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer and Suarez-Tangil (IMDEA Networks; AFT 2025; arXiv 2508.03474, posted 5 Aug 2025) studied Polymarket from **April 2024 to April 2025** using on-chain order-book history and non-atomic arbitrage. They define Market Rebalancing Arbitrage (within one market or condition) and Combinatorial Arbitrage (across markets) and estimate about **$40 million of realized profit** across both. **Possibly superseded:** the sample ends before the 2026 fee regime. — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474); [AFT 2025 proceedings entry](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.AFT.2025.27)
- [C] Per the alphaXiv summary of the same paper, **7,051 of 17,218 conditions (41%)** showed at least one arbitrage opportunity, with a median profit of about $0.60 per dollar invested. Secondary summary; verify in the PDF. — [alphaXiv overview of 2508.03474](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2508.03474v1)
- [C/D] A Substack note says the paper analysed about 86 million bets and that roughly 14 of the 20 most profitable wallets were bots. Blog-level, unverified. — [Substack note (navnoorbawa)](https://substack.com/@navnoorbawa/note/c-271675481)
- [A] "Arbitrage Analysis in Polymarket NBA Markets" (Cheng, Yang, Zou; UCLA; arXiv 2605.00864, April 2026) reconstructs market states from **over 75 million order-book snapshots across 173 games**. Single-market arbitrage is rare: **only 7 executable in-game episodes, median duration 3.6 seconds**. The authors note that snapshot data may overstate true lifespans. — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1); [RePEc entry](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2605.00864.html)
- [A] "Executable Arbitrage and Market Efficiency in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2608.00666, Aug 2026) separates payoff-space no-arbitrage from protocol-executable no-arbitrage. In Polymarket NegRisk markets the adapter operationalises only the NO-to-YES direction before settlement, and positive payoff-bound violations in the order book are heavily concentrated on the unsupported YES side. — [arXiv 2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666)
- [C] A Substack recap of that paper reports **2,098 YES-side violations with a median duration of 16.15 seconds versus 36 NO-side violations with a median of 7.99 seconds**. A separate literature-review site attributes "$1.12 million in total arbitrage profits" to the paper, and a snippet of the paper gives a market-state benchmark of 5,185 USDC for the older FPMM sample. All secondary; the sample period was not retrievable. — [Tiger Polymarket Prediction Substack](https://tigerpolymarketprediction.substack.com/p/weekly-algorithm-executable-vs-payoff); [Moonlight literature review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/executable-arbitrage-and-market-efficiency-in-prediction-markets)
- [A] Capital lock-up interacts with these mechanics: Gebele and Matthes (TUM; arXiv 2605.31431, 29 May 2026) find that negRisk conversion compresses settlement discounts by recycling part of a position into synthetic collateral. — [arXiv 2605.31431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31431)
- [D] Practitioner descriptions of "both-sides" bots (the "gabagool" style) in 15-minute crypto markets: typical combined-price gaps of $0.03–$0.04 per share pair on BTC 15-minute markets; one self-reporting author cites $0.01–$0.02 profit per token pair on roughly 190 trades a day; reverse-engineering posts say the well-known wallet does not buy both sides simultaneously but legs in (buy-only, almost no sells). Main stated risks are failing to fill the second leg before resolution and execution failures. No audited P&L found. — [dev.to: reverse-engineering "gabigol"](https://dev.to/fatherson/reverse-engineering-gabigol-high-frequency-crypto-updown-farmer-on-polymarket-d0a); [dev.to: 176 trades, what my bot actually made](https://dev.to/manja316/176-trades-on-polymarket-what-my-bot-actually-made-its-not-what-you-think-1nab); [dev.to: Dutch-book bot for 15-minute markets](https://dev.to/xniiinx/just-built-a-dutch-book-arbitrage-bot-for-polymarkets-15-minute-crypto-markets-i7b)
- [D] Disconfirming view: a low-rigor explainer argues a same-market YES+NO sum below $1 "will never appear on your screen" and that such trades are volatility bets rather than arbitrage. This conflicts with the order-book studies above (which do find gaps, only very short-lived ones). — [KuCoin explainer](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/explaining-polymarket-why-yes-no-must-equal-1)

**2026 fee regime (changes the economics of every taker-side rebalancing trade)**
- [B] Polymarket added taker-only fees to 15-minute crypto up/down markets in early January 2026, with the stated purpose of funding maker liquidity rebates; fees peak near 50% odds and fall toward zero at the extremes. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-quietly-adds-taker-fees-15-minute-crypto-markets)

- [D, unconfirmed against the official fee page] Reported fee coefficients after 30 March 2026: crypto feeRate 0.072, i.e. $1.80 peak fee per 100 shares at 50 cents; economics / culture / weather / other feeRate 0.05, i.e. $1.25 per 100 shares at 50 cents. — [dev.to (polytrage)](https://dev.to/polytrage/polymarket-is-introducing-fees-heres-what-actually-changes-4ai0)
- [B] Pine Analytics (on-chain data) lists crypto as the highest-fee category with a peak effective fee of 1.80%, and estimates about $1.2M a day in gross fees on about $160M a day of taker volume after the roll-out. — [Pine Analytics: Polymarket Fee Rollout](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)
- [A] Official docs: effective 20 November 2026 (23:59 UTC) tiered maker rebates replace the daily market-wide rebate pools; combos (parlays) keep their own fee coefficient and earn no maker rebates; geopolitics remains fee-free; liquidity rewards are unchanged. — [Polymarket docs: Fee and rebate changes, November 2026](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/fee-and-rebate-changes-november-2026)

### Inferences
- **Fee arithmetic (my calculation from the cited coefficients, assuming fee per share = feeRate × p × (1 − p)).** Buying both sides of a binary crypto market as a taker at about 50 cents each costs about 3.6 cents in fees per $1 complete set; at feeRate 0.05 it costs about 2.5 cents. Practitioner-reported gaps are 1–4 cents. Taker-taker rebalancing in fee-bearing categories is therefore uneconomic unless the gap exceeds the fee, and multi-leg "buy every YES" trades pay the fee on every leg (ten legs at 10 cents each at feeRate 0.05 is about 4.5 cents per set). What survives is (a) fee-free categories (geopolitics), (b) maker-side legging, which is really inventory-bearing market making, and (c) convert/merge paths where only some legs are bought on the book. Caveat: these coefficients are the reported March–November 2026 values; a crypto fee cut was reported for 20 November 2026 whose size was not retrieved, so the crypto figure may now be lower.
- **Trend.** The 2024–25 sample showed opportunities in 41% of conditions; 2026 order-book studies show single-market gaps that are rare and last a few seconds. This is consistent with heavy bot competition plus fees, although no single study measures the decline on a consistent basis.
- **Who captures it.** Automated accounts. Opportunity lifetimes of 3.6–16 seconds and tiny displayed size put this beyond manual traders, and the NBA study concludes risk-free extraction is "confined to retail scale" by depth (see section 2 for size figures).
- **Why it is not riskless.** Execution is non-atomic on an off-chain order book (one leg fills, the other moves); the NegRisk adapter supports only one conversion direction pre-settlement, so some "payoff" arbitrages are not executable; augmented neg-risk events can gain outcomes after creation; capital is locked until resolution when the position cannot be merged or converted.
- **LLM role: essentially none.** Detection is summation over order books; the edge is latency, fee tier and inventory management. An LLM is at most a coding assistant.

### Gaps
- **Exact Saguillo et al. breakdown not verified in this session.** Unverified recollection of the paper (check against the PDF before use): single-condition arbitrage roughly $10.6M (about $5.9M buying YES+NO below $1 and about $4.7M selling above $1); multi-condition NegRisk rebalancing roughly $29M (buying all NO about $17.3M, buying all YES about $11.1M); combinatorial only about $0.1M; top arbitrageur about $2.0M over about 4,049 trades.
- No 2026 platform-wide replication of the realized-profit measurement under the post-fee regime was found; the 2026 studies are narrower (NBA only, NegRisk only).
- Sample periods for arXiv 2608.00666 and for the NBA study (which season) were not retrievable.
- No study of sum-to-one violations on Kalshi's mutually exclusive multi-outcome events was found.
- The official Polymarket fee page with current per-category coefficients could not be opened; the feeRate values come from a developer blog.

## 2. Combinatorial / logical arbitrage across semantically related markets, and LLM dependency detection

### Takeaway
This is the one structural strategy where language understanding is a genuine input: the 2025 IMDEA paper used embeddings plus an LLM to find dependent markets, and an October 2026 paper recovers 1.9 million logical relations across more than 255,000 contracts by "semantic analysis of resolution criteria" and reports that an idealised arbitrageur earns 3.5% a week after spreads and fees. The realized record is far more modest: the LLM step in the 2025 paper surfaced only 13 valid dependent pairs, and a 2026 NBA study finds a 101-basis-point median return capped at about 15 shares per opportunity.

### Cited Findings
- [A] Saguillo et al. (sample April 2024 – April 2025) note that naive cross-market checking needs O(2^(n+m)) comparisons, so they reduce the search with heuristics (timeliness, topical similarity, combinatorial relationships), then use text embeddings (Linq-Embed-Mistral) and LLMs to extract logical dependencies from market condition descriptions. — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)
- [C] Per the alphaXiv summary, the LLM step identified **13 semantically dependent pairs, all tied to the 2024 U.S. presidential election**, and the authors suggest better LLMs could capture dependencies across more than two markets. — [alphaXiv overview of 2508.03474](https://alphaxiv.org/overview/2508.03474v1)
- [C/D] A Chinese-language reprint surfaced in search (BlockBeats via 鉅亨網; the exact article among the three linked could not be confirmed) describes the integer-programming approach to related markets and says the research team found **1,576 candidate dependent market pairs** in the 2024 election set, far more than survived validation. — [cnyes/BlockBeats 6268971](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6268971); [cnyes/BlockBeats 6377561](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6377561); [cnyes/BlockBeats 6406812](https://news.cnyes.com/news/id/6406812)
- [C] The Flashbots Collective discussion of the paper describes its contribution as "a scalable methodology for detecting market dependencies using LLMs". — [Flashbots Collective thread](https://collective.flashbots.net/t/arbitrage-in-prediction-markets-strategies-impact-and-open-questions/5198)
- [A] **Follow-up, October 2026:** Hongjian Shi and Daojing Zhai, "Combinatorial Arbitrage in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2610.23517, q-fin.TR). They prove that prices outside the convex hull of admissible payoff vectors admit arbitrage, find optimal portfolios by integer programming, and use venue metadata plus semantic analysis of resolution criteria to recover **1.9 million logical relations covering more than 255,000 contracts**. — [arXiv 2610.23517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23517)
- [C] Per the RePEc abstract of the same paper: among settled portfolios, **an ideal arbitrageur that takes the full displayed depth of every opportunity and recycles capital at resolution earns a risk-free return of 3.5% a week after spreads and fees**; opportunities **persist longer and attract less observed participation where relation discovery and execution are more costly**. Sample period not retrieved. — [RePEc entry for 2610.23517](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2610.23517.html)
- [A] **Follow-up, April 2026 (sports):** in the UCLA NBA study, combinatorial Moneyline–Spread arbitrage is more common than single-market arbitrage: **290 active episodes, overwhelmingly in the final minutes of live play; median return 101 basis points; 76.9% of opportunities limited to an average executable size of 14.8 shares**; the theoretical "middle" jackpot was never realized. A single NBA game carries about 45 markets (about 14 moneyline / spread / totals and more than 30 player props). — [arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.00864v1)
- [A] PolySwarm (arXiv 2604.03888, April 2026) is a multi-agent LLM trading framework that, among other things, computes KL/JS divergence across related markets; it is a trading-system paper rather than a measurement of dependency-detection accuracy. — [arXiv 2604.03888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.03888)
- [A] The protocol-executability point from section 1 applies here too: efficiency depends on whether the protocol exposes payoff equivalences as executable primitives, not only on the payoff structure. — [arXiv 2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666)
- [B/C] Cointelegraph Magazine ran a feature titled "AI agents can reshape arbitrage in prediction markets" (content not retrieved beyond the title). — [Cointelegraph Magazine](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/ai-agents-can-reshape-arbitrage-prediction-markets)

### Inferences
- **The LLM is a discovery tool, not an execution tool.** In both papers the language model's job is to turn free-text resolution criteria into logical constraints (implication, mutual exclusion, exhaustiveness). Everything after that — constraint solving by integer programming, order-book replay, leg execution — is conventional engineering. Shi and Zhai's finding that gaps persist longer where relation discovery is costly is the most direct evidence in the literature that semantic discovery is a real barrier to entry, and therefore that a capable LLM is a genuine enabler for this strategy specifically.
- **Detection yield was low in 2025.** Going from 1,576 candidate pairs to 13 valid ones (figures from secondary sources) implies heavy false-positive filtering and manual validation; LLM output cannot be traded blind.
- **Scale is the binding constraint.** Realized combinatorial profit in the 2024–25 sample is believed to be a tiny share of the $40M total (per an unverified recollection of the paper's breakdown; see Gaps in section 1), and the 2026 NBA evidence shows about 1% edges on about 15 shares. The "3.5% a week" figure is an upper bound for an ideal trader taking all displayed depth; it says nothing about capacity in dollars.
- **Relation types.** The Moneyline–Spread relation is an implication (covering a negative spread implies winning). Date-ladder markets ("by March" implies "by June") and nomination-versus-general markets are the same class of subset relation; they should be the cleanest for an LLM to parse because the logic sits in the title, whereas relations hidden in resolution fine print are harder and riskier.
- **Why it is not riskless.** A mis-parsed rule turns an "arbitrage" into a directional bet; legs execute non-atomically across separate books; capital is locked until the last leg resolves; two logically linked markets can still be resolved inconsistently by the oracle.

### Gaps
- **Accuracy of LLM dependency detection is not quantified in anything retrievable this session.** Unverified recollection of Saguillo et al. (check the PDF): the model was DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B and a validation accuracy of about 81% was reported on a consistency check; the funnel ran from tens of thousands of candidate pairs to 1,576 flagged, a few hundred after manual review, and 13 valid.
- No 2026 paper reporting precision/recall of LLM-based relation extraction on prediction-market rules was found; Shi and Zhai's error rate for their 1.9M relations was not retrievable.
- No measurement specific to date-ladder or nomination-versus-general pairs (frequency, cents of edge, duration) was found.
- Sample period, dollar capacity and realized (as opposed to idealised) profits in Shi and Zhai were not retrievable.

## 3. Cross-platform arbitrage (Polymarket vs Kalshi vs other venues; sportsbooks; options-implied probabilities)

### Takeaway
Price gaps between venues are real and persistent — the best 2026 dataset finds 2–4% average execution-aware deviations between semantically equivalent markets — but they persist because closing them is expensive (segregated collateral, fees, capital locked to resolution) and because "equivalent" markets are frequently not equivalent in their resolution rules. Large gaps are rare in liquid markets (above 5 points in only 0.8% of minutes during the 2026 World Cup), and no audited record of sustained cross-platform arbitrage profit was found.

### Cited Findings

**Size and persistence of gaps**
- [A] Gebele and Matthes, "Semantic Non-Fungibility and Violations of the Law of One Price in Prediction Markets" (TUM; arXiv 2601.01706, 5 Jan 2026): first **human-validated** cross-platform dataset of aligned markets, **over 100,000 events across ten venues, 2018–2025**. About **6% of events are concurrently listed** on more than one platform, and semantically equivalent markets show **persistent execution-aware price deviations of 2–4% on average, even in highly liquid settings**. They argue the cause is the absence of a shared definition of event identity, which makes arbitrage capital-intensive or unenforceable. — [arXiv 2601.01706](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01706)
- [B] Dune's analysis of 2026 World Cup markets: Polymarket–Kalshi divergences **above 5 points occurred in only 0.8% of minutes**, always at moments of sudden price movement; Dune argues almost no retail trader can access both venues and that institutions able to trade both treat the gap as durable carry. — [Dune: Digital Asset Brief #4](https://dune.com/blog/digital-asset-brief-4-prediction-markets-world-cup-fifa)
- [A] A Boğaziçi University working paper reports an average cross-platform spread of 6.2 percentage points on the same contract (the snippet does not make clear which venues) and, using Hasbrouck information shares, attributes nearly 85% of price discovery jointly to Polymarket and Betfair in its clean panel. — [Boğaziçi working paper](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)
- [D] A hackathon dashboard matched only 18 markets and found Kalshi about 18% higher on average; too small to generalise. — [Devpost: PredFi](https://devpost.com/software/predfi)

**Lead-lag and price discovery**
- [A] Ng, Peng, Tao and Zhou, "Price Discovery and Trading in Modern Prediction Markets" (SSRN 5331995, revised April 2026), using common contracts on Polymarket, Kalshi, PredictIt and Robinhood before the 2024 U.S. election: **Polymarket leads Kalshi in price discovery, especially when liquidity and activity are high**, implying economically meaningful arbitrage opportunities; net order imbalance from large trades predicts subsequent returns. **Possibly superseded** (2024 data, before Kalshi's 2025–26 growth). — [SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5331995)
- [A] Tsang and Yang (arXiv, March 2026), on the 2024 presidential-election markets: Polymarket was thin in its early months, with elevated price impact, wide arbitrage deviations and few active participants; price discovery differed sharply across political shocks. — [arXiv 2603.03136](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.03136); [arXiv 2603.03152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03152) (attribution between the two IDs not confirmed)

**Versus options and sportsbooks**
- [A] A 2026 arXiv paper compares Polymarket Bitcoin contracts with option-implied probabilities: **mean gap 5.6 percentage points over 214 hourly observations**, largest when the option-implied probability is low and when time to expiry is long. — [arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19517)
- [D, conflicting] Sports pricing versus sportsbooks: one outlet's March–April 2026 sample of 64 NBA / NHL / MLB markets put Kalshi's sports vig at about 0.85% versus about 4.62% on same-time sportsbook lines; a Casino.org piece citing Bettormetrics concludes the opposite, that prediction markets are not a better deal than regulated sportsbooks. — [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/prediction-markets-vs-sportsbooks/); contradicted by [Casino.org](https://www.casino.org/news/prediction-markets-have-sports-pricing-problems/)
- [D] An affiliate guide describes cross-venue sports arbitrage as "real, narrow and execution-bound": small on liquid headline markets, larger on niche markets where one venue is thin. — [Tech Insider: prediction market arbitrage](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/prediction-market-arbitrage/)

**Frictions: fees, capital, transfers**
- [D] Kalshi's taker fee is described as 0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price); one guide estimates a break-even cross-platform spread of about 2.75 cents at a 50-cent midpoint. — [Kalshi-vs-Polymarket arbitrage guide](https://sailgp.com/prediction-markets/guide/kalshi-vs-polymarket/arbitrage); [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/prediction-markets-vs-sportsbooks-volume-fees-data-2026)
- [D] The two order books are separate systems with no shared feed, and capital cannot move between Kalshi and Polymarket inside the trade window, so a meaningful share of working capital sits idle on each side. — [dev.to (polytrage)](https://dev.to/polytrage/why-the-same-market-trades-at-different-prices-on-polymarket-and-kalshi-4l84)
- [A] Capital lock-up is priced: Gebele and Matthes model prediction-market prices as probabilities discounted for delayed settlement and locked capital, and build an "annualized settlement wedge". (Details in section 6.) — [arXiv 2605.31431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31431)
- [B/C] A CCN article dated 9 November 2026 is headlined "Polymarket US fees 36 percent higher [than] Kalshi" (content not retrieved). — [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/polymarket-us-fees-36-percent-higher-kalshi/)
- [D] A vendor claim that cross-platform monitoring "captures 1–3% risk-free returns on liquid markets" is unsourced. — [Substack (navnoorbawa)](https://navnoorbawa.substack.com/p/the-math-of-prediction-markets-binary)

**Differing resolution rules (the main reason this is not arbitrage)**
- [B] The settlement processes differ structurally: Kalshi itself determines the outcome after trading closes; Polymarket uses UMA's Optimistic Oracle, where disputed outcomes go to a token-holder vote. — [AOL: 4 biggest differences between Kalshi and Polymarket](https://www.aol.com/news/4-biggest-differences-between-kalshi-090901646.html); [DeFi Rate: how Kalshi and Polymarket settle](https://defirate.com/?p=5575)
- [A/C] Weather example from the 2601.01706 author: the two platforms' temperature markets reference different weather stations (Kalshi uses Central Park for New York), the stations frequently disagree, and the markets "can (and sometimes do) resolve differently". — [Jonas Gebele on X](https://x.com/j9ebele/status/2008938480617849193)
- [C] Government-shutdown contracts, 31 January 2026: the Polymarket and Kalshi contracts diverged because trigger conditions were defined differently (about 88% versus about 93%). A secondary comparison says Polymarket keyed on an OMB determination and Kalshi on a different official definition; not checked against the rulebooks. — [Odaily newsflash](https://www.odaily.news/en/newsflash/466653); [CryptoRank: shutdown flaw exposed](https://cryptorank.io/news/feed/447ea-prediction-markets-shutdown-flaw-exposed)
- [D, unconfirmed] A blog claims that during the 2024 shutdown debate Polymarket resolved "Yes" although no shutdown occurred while Kalshi resolved "No". Single self-published claim; no primary source found. — [Monad blog](https://blog.monad.xyz/blog/prediction-market-arbitrage)
- [D] Worked hypothetical: Kalshi's Fed contract covers a 25bp cut at a scheduled meeting while Polymarket's covers any cut, so an emergency 12.5bp cut would make an arbitrageur lose on both legs. — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/prediction-markets/arbitrage-prediction-markets)
- [C] Single-platform mis-resolutions are documented (for example a Polymarket Ukraine–U.S. rare-earths-deal market resolved "Yes" with no deal; a Kalshi Oscars-viewership market paid the wrong side). The exact page behind each claim was not confirmed; they surfaced alongside the following sources. — [Steptoe: the risks of ambiguity in prediction markets](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/its-not-on-the-house-the-risks-of-ambiguity-in-prediction-markets.html); [Substack: Prediction markets can't agree on the truth](https://michaellwy.substack.com/p/prediction-markets-cant-agree-on)
- [D] Traders themselves price this risk: a Manifold market asked whether Polymarket, Kalshi and Manifold would all resolve their shutdown markets the same way. — [Manifold](https://manifold.markets/bens/will-polymarket-kalshi-and-manifold)

**Tooling and LLM use**
- [D] Scanner products and open-source matchers exist (an Apify "Polymarket + Kalshi arbitrage finder", live "arbitrage finder" dashboards). No published accuracy figures for their market matching were found. — [Apify actor](https://apify.com/congism/polymarket-kalshi-arb-finder); [PredictionHunt](https://www.predictionhunt.com/smart-bets); [StartupHub arbitrage board](https://www.startuphub.ai/arbitrage)
- [D] A Chinese-language promotional page for a cross-platform bot claims 5–15% monthly returns and a Sharpe above 2.5; self-reported and unverified. — [Jason Chen GitHub page (zh)](https://jason-chen-2017.github.io/Jason-Chen-2017/reading/prediction_market_crypto_arbitrage_bot.html)

### Inferences
- **A 2–4% average gap is roughly the cost of closing it.** Kalshi's taker fee alone is about 1.75 cents at 50 cents, Polymarket now charges takers in most categories, and capital is tied up on both venues until resolution. The observed deviation is better read as a no-arbitrage band than as free money.
- **Trend through 2026.** Earlier evidence (2024 election) showed wide deviations in a thin market; the 2026 World Cup evidence shows gaps above 5 points in under 1% of minutes in liquid markets. Opportunities have migrated to niche and thin markets and to moments of sudden repricing, where execution risk is highest.
- **Who captures it.** Firms with accounts, collateral and automation on both venues. Dune's observation that almost no retail trader can access both venues reflects legal and geographic segmentation (background, not re-verified here and covered by another researcher: Kalshi is a U.S. CFTC-regulated exchange, Polymarket's international venue restricts U.S. persons, and Polymarket US is a separate CFTC-regulated venue that files its own rules). That segmentation is part of why gaps persist.
- **What an LLM contributes.** Two things: (1) candidate matching of markets across venues from titles and descriptions; (2) reading both rulebooks and flagging differences in resolution source, cut-off time, tie handling and edge cases. The fact that the reference academic dataset had to be human-validated, and that its authors coin "semantic non-fungibility", indicates automated matching is error-prone and that rule-difference detection is the high-value, language-heavy step. Execution, fee modelling and collateral management are not LLM tasks.
- **Mis-match is the dominant tail risk.** A false "equivalent" pair converts a hedged position into two directional bets that can both lose.

### Gaps
- **No verified, quantified case of a cross-platform arbitrageur losing money on a split resolution was found**, only hypotheticals, single-platform mis-resolutions and one unconfirmed 2024 shutdown claim.
- No published precision/recall for LLM-based cross-venue market matching or rule-difference detection.
- No 2026 lead-lag study using Kalshi's much larger 2026 volumes; the Ng et al. result rests on 2024 data.
- Gap magnitudes after both venues' 2026 fees, half-life of gaps, and realized profits of dual-venue firms were not found. Planned searches on trading-firm activity (Bloomberg / WSJ / FT) did not run because the search budget was exhausted.
- Which venues the 6.2-point Boğaziçi figure refers to; sample details of the options paper (2606.19517) beyond 214 hourly observations.

## 4. Latency arbitrage against reference prices (short-duration crypto up/down markets vs spot exchanges)

### Takeaway
Before January 2026 bots profited from Polymarket's fee-free 15-minute crypto markets lagging spot exchanges; Polymarket responded with taker-only fees explicitly aimed at this (January 2026), extended them (March 2026), cut maker fee pass-through, and by late 2026 measured Polymarket–Binance lags are 16 milliseconds at the median on venue clocks and about 350 milliseconds after large moves. This is now a co-location and maker-rebate business in which automated accounts are 55–62% of volume and most bots lose; LLMs are too slow to play any role in the loop.

### Cited Findings
- [B] In early January 2026 Polymarket added taker-only fees to 15-minute crypto up/down markets without a formal announcement; the fee revenue funds maker liquidity rebates, and coverage frames the change as neutralising bots that exploited delays between Polymarket prices and spot prices on major exchanges. — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/384461/polymarket-adds-taker-fees-to-15-minute-crypto-markets-to-fund-liquidity-rebates); [Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/cryptocurrency/polymarket-introduces-dynamic-fees-to-curb-latency-arbitrage-in-short-term-crypto-markets/); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/polymarket-quietly-adds-taker-fees-15-minute-crypto-markets)
- [B/C] The reported peak fee was about **1.56%** near 50% odds as fees were extended from 15-minute contracts to all crypto markets (6 March 2026); Odaily's framing is that around 50% probability a taker needs an edge above 1.56% just to break even. — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/on-polymarket-and-kalshi-five-minute-crypto-bets-now-dominate-prediction-flows/); [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5209447); [ChainCatcher (zh)](https://www.chaincatcher.com/zh-tw/article/2253749)
- [B] After the 30 March 2026 restructuring, crypto carried the highest peak effective fee of any category, 1.80%. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)
- [C] Maker pass-through was cut: the fee reimbursement to makers on 15-minute markets went from 100% to 20%. Official docs later described the maker-rebate share as 20% for crypto, 25% for most categories and 50% for finance, paid daily. — [KuCoin news flash](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-adjusts-trading-fee-reimbursement-from-100-to-20); [Polymarket docs: maker rebates](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/maker-rebates)
- [B/C] Polymarket also removed the 500 ms taker quote delay in crypto markets (date not retrieved). — [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5209447)
- [A/B] From 20 November 2026 maker rebates are tiered by 30-day maker weighted volume; CCN (18 Nov 2026) reports rates of 7% for $1M–$4M, 10% for $4M–$10M and 15% at $10M or more, with small makers outside the tiers, alongside a cut to crypto fees (size of the cut not retrieved). The docs' own example: a Tier 4 maker whose fills generate $100 of taker fees earns $15. — [Polymarket docs: November 2026 changes](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/fee-and-rebate-changes-november-2026); [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/polymarket-cuts-crypto-fees-ends-small-trader-rebates/)
- [B] Dune: in 5-minute and 15-minute markets **automated addresses control 55–62% of volume**; 5-minute markets overtook 15-minute volume within two weeks of launch. — [Dune: Polymarket fast markets](https://dune.com/blog/polymarket-fast-markets)
- [B/C] Ultra-short-term crypto bets reach roughly $70 million a day across Polymarket and Kalshi, and 5- and 15-minute contracts are over half of crypto trading on both platforms. — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/on-polymarket-and-kalshi-five-minute-crypto-bets-now-dominate-prediction-flows/)
- [A] **Measured latency, 2026.** The OpenMarket dataset (Gregory Young; arXiv 2607.26245, 28 Jul 2026) pairs Polymarket BTC markets with Binance BTC-USDT at millisecond resolution: 727,098,247 rows, 54 Polymarket days between **12 Feb and 15 May 2026**, about 2.9 million lead-lag pairs. Findings: an apparent **16 ms median lag** on venue source clocks (stable at about 16–19 ms regardless of the size of the price difference, with heavy tails), and, in a collector-clock event study, Polymarket quotes responding to Binance moves of at least 5 basis points after a **median 347 ms**. — [alphaXiv entry for 2607.26245](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.26245); [RePEc entry](https://ideas.repec.org/p/arx/papers/2607.26245.html)
- [D, conflicting] Settlement uses Chainlink data streams; developer blogs disagree on feed latency (under 300 ms versus updates every 10–30 seconds or on 0.5% deviations). — [dev.to guide to 5-minute bots](https://dev.to/benjamin_cup/the-ultimate-guide-to-building-a-profitable-5-minute-polymarket-crypto-trading-bot-march-2026-4ohg)
- [D] A Chinese-language practitioner source surfaced in search states that the arbitrage window in BTC 5-minute markets is about 2.7 seconds and that competing requires co-located servers and a six-figure budget (exact page among the two linked not confirmed). — [QuickNode guide (zh)](https://www.quicknode.com/zh/builders-guide/best/top-10-polymarket-trading-bots); [Yellow (zh-tw)](https://yellow.com/zh-tw/news/%E8%8F%81%E8%8B%B1%E7%A8%8B%E5%BC%8F%E9%AB%98%E6%89%8B%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95%E5%9C%A8-polymarket-%E9%9D%A0%E6%A9%9F%E5%99%A8%E4%BA%BA%E6%AF%8F%E6%9C%88%E8%B3%BA%E9%80%B2-20-%E8%90%AC%E7%BE%8E%E5%85%83%EF%BC%8C-%E5%8D%BB%E5%BE%9E%E4%B8%8D%E7%9C%9F%E6%AD%A3%E3%80%8C%E9%A0%90%E6%B8%AC%E3%80%8D%E7%B5%90%E6%9E%9C)

PYMNTS adds that 5% of wallets account for 75% of volume. Bot classification is inferred from Dune trade records. (All-strategy figures, not latency-specific.) — [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains); [BeInCrypto summary](https://beincrypto.com/prediction-market-retail-traders-losses-bloomberg/); [PYMNTS](https://pymnts.com/markets/2026/most-polymarket-users-lose-money-as-top-accounts-win-big)
- [D] Self-reported bot results are unreliable and often promotional: developer guides promise 1–4% per cycle or win rates above 65%; a sponsored page claims a 98% win rate; a Telegram post claims $15K to $529K in 15 days. None is verified. One developer argues post-fee winners are "the best liquidity providers", not the fastest takers; another argues well-built bots become more dominant. — [dev.to (emilanielsen)](https://dev.to/emilanielsen/polymarkets-new-fee-structure-why-many-trading-bots-may-no-longer-be-profitable-2j3j); [BeInCrypto: arbitrage bots dominate](https://beincrypto.com/arbitrage-bots-polymarket-humans/)
- [B/C] The short contracts have also drawn manipulation concerns (moving the thin reference print near expiry). — [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-bitcoin-contracts-price-manipulation/)

### Inferences
- **The taker version of the trade is largely closed.** With a median reaction lag of 16–350 ms and a taker fee peaking at a reported 1.56–1.80% near 50 cents (about 1.8 cents per share under the reported March 2026 crypto coefficient, before the November 2026 cut), a taker must be both faster than the fastest market makers and right by more than the fee. The remaining edge is on the maker side (quote from the spot feed, earn spread plus rebate, cancel fast), which the November 2026 tiering further concentrates in makers above $1M–$10M of monthly volume.
- **Capital and infrastructure.** Co-location, direct exchange feeds and high maker volume are prerequisites; the tiered rebate schedule means small accounts receive no rebate at all.
- **LLM role: irrelevant.** Decision latency budgets are milliseconds; LLM inference takes hundreds of milliseconds to seconds. The signal is a pricing model on a numeric feed. Research prototypes that put LLM agents on "latency arbitrage" (PolySwarm) do not change this.
- **Not riskless.** Reference-feed divergence at expiry, adverse selection from faster participants, fee and rule changes at short notice (fee or rebate changes in January, March and November 2026, plus removal of the taker delay), and reference-price manipulation.

### Gaps
- **No independent before/after measurement of bot profitability around the January and March 2026 fee changes was found**; the widely circulated pre-fee anecdotes (for example a bot said to have turned a few hundred dollars into several hundred thousand in a month) could not be verified this session and should be treated as unverified.
- The Dune fast-markets post and the Pine Analytics post could not be opened in full, so volume and P&L time series are missing.
- Details of the November 2026 crypto fee cut (new coefficient) were not retrieved.
- Kalshi's short-duration crypto contracts: no latency study found.

## 5. Market making and liquidity-reward farming

### Takeaway
Liquidity provision is where the platforms now direct the money (maker rebates funded by taker fees, plus separate liquidity-reward programmes such as more than $5M for sports in April 2026), and the aggregate evidence says makers as a class earn what takers lose (about ±2.7% at 50 cents on Kalshi). But the gains are concentrated in large automated makers, a majority of bots lose money, and retail "reward farming" estimates are routinely overstated because fills are adversely selected.

### Cited Findings
- [A] Polymarket's liquidity-rewards formula boosts two-sided depth (single-sided orders still score) and rewards tightness to the mid, adjusted for size; the April 2026 sports programme distributed **over $5M** in incentives across sports and esports markets for that month. — [Polymarket docs: liquidity rewards](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/liquidity-rewards)
- [A] Maker rebates are funded by taker fees, paid daily, with category shares of 20% (crypto), 25% (most categories) and 50% (finance) before 20 November 2026; the percentage is at Polymarket's sole discretion. From 20 November 2026 rebates are tiered by 30-day maker volume (reported 7% / 10% / 15% at $1M / $4M / $10M), and liquidity rewards are unchanged. — [Polymarket docs: maker rebates](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/maker-rebates); [Polymarket docs: November 2026 changes](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/fee-and-rebate-changes-november-2026); [CCN](https://www.ccn.com/news/crypto/polymarket-cuts-crypto-fees-ends-small-trader-rebates/)
- [B] The rebate pool is material: about $1.2M a day of gross taker fees on about $160M a day of taker volume after 30 March 2026. — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)
- [A] Polymarket US's liquidity-incentive filing with the CFTC rewards resting liquidity and itself notes that prices move rapidly and **adverse-selection risk is highest during the mid-event period**. — [CFTC rule filing (Polymarket US)](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules11032642173.pdf)
- [C] Becker's analysis of **72.1 million Kalshi trades** (via a reprint): takers show negative excess returns at 80 of 99 price levels and makers positive returns at the same 80; at 50 cents takers earn −2.65% and makers +2.66%. He calls the premium takers pay for low-probability YES contracts an "optimism tax". — [KuCoin reprint of Becker's analysis](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/analysis-reveals-systematic-wealth-transfer-in-prediction-markets)
- [A] Bürgi, Deng and Whelan, "Makers and Takers: The Economics of the Kalshi Prediction Market" (CEPR DP 20631; CESifo WP 12122; over 300,000 contracts): makers are relatively well-informed traders who post offers and takers accept them; the favourite-longshot bias exists for both but is stronger for takers; Kalshi's pre-2025 fee design charged takers but not makers, worsening takers' returns. — [UCD working paper](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); [GWU working paper 2026-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf); [CESifo WP 12122](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp12122.pdf)
- [D, with stated caveat] A practitioner microstructure note using about **568 million on-chain Polymarket fills through March 2026** finds every maker-volume decile earns a positive volume-weighted markout at every horizon studied. Caveat stated by the author: markouts are computed against subsequent trades rather than quoted mids, which conflates spread capture with adverse selection. — [Polymarket market-making: a microstructure note](https://avdheshcharjan.github.io/notes/polymarket-mm.html)
- [B] Counter-evidence on distribution: Bloomberg found 823 bot accounts earned $131M but a majority of bots lost money. — [Bloomberg](https://bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-28/most-prediction-market-traders-are-losing-money-while-bots-rack-up-gains)
- [D] Community return claims vary by an order of magnitude: one guide says two-sided making returned $200–$800 a day on $50K but warns that one bad week of adverse selection around major news can wipe out a month; another puts realistic retail earnings at $5–$30 a day on $5K–$20K before losses; a third argues reward calculators overstate by 3–5× because being filled is usually adverse. — [polymarkets.co.il guide](https://polymarkets.co.il/en/guide/liquidity-rewards/); [dev.to rewards calculator](https://dev.to/fatherson/polymarket-liquidity-rewards-build-a-real-calculator-min-size-max-spread-break-even-risk--117o); [Medium rewards calculator](https://medium.com/@ezzekielnjuguna/polymarket-liquidity-rewards-calculator-estimate-maker-rewards-min-size-max-spread-and-risk-7ddc4319590f)
- [C/D] Chinese-language analysis (ChainCatcher / Odaily reprints): Polymarket introduced fees together with two market-maker reward programmes in early 2026; a practitioner describes inventory risk (库存风险) as the main exposure and spread plus platform subsidy as the income; adverse selection (不利选择) can lose money faster than directional trading. — [ChainCatcher (zh)](https://www.chaincatcher.com/zh-tw/article/2253749); [ChainCatcher (zh), second piece](https://www.chaincatcher.com/zh-tw/article/2234513); [KuCoin (zh-hant) on LP incentive traps](https://www.kucoin.com/zh-hant/news/flash/polymarket-lp-incentive-mechanism-four-key-insights-and-cost-traps)
- [A] Academic infrastructure exists for deeper work: a June 2026 paper documents a Polymarket-v1 on-chain database and cites a liquidity-provision study of about a million trades from February–April 2026; IMDEA has a paper on Polymarket initial liquidity; a 2026 paper studies what happens when institutional liquidity enters prediction markets. Results were not retrievable. — [arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04217); [IMDEA: Polymarket initial liquidity](https://dspace.networks.imdea.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12761/2065/Polymarket_Initial_Liquidity-3.pdf?sequence=1); [arXiv 2604.10005](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10005v2)

### Inferences
- **Makers win in aggregate; the average entrant does not.** Kalshi trade data and the Polymarket markout note both show positive maker edge on average, while Bloomberg shows most bots losing. The reconciliation is concentration: a few hundred well-capitalised automated makers capture the spread, rebates and rewards.
- **2026 trend.** Platform policy moved value from takers to makers (fees funding rebates), then concentrated rebates in the largest makers (November 2026 tiers). Reward farming with passive wide quotes is being squeezed from both sides: scoring favours tight quotes, and tight quotes are the ones that get picked off.
- **Risks.** Adverse selection around scheduled and unscheduled news, inventory accumulation in one-sided flow, binary jump-to-resolution risk (a quote at 60 cents can be worth zero seconds later), and programme terms that change at the platform's discretion.
- **LLM role: peripheral but real.** Quoting, hedging and cancel logic are numeric. A plausible LLM use is as a risk switch: classifying incoming headlines or rule clarifications to widen or pull quotes, and reading market rules to avoid quoting markets with ambiguous resolution. No measured evidence of the value of this was found.

### Gaps
- **No audited maker P&L for Polymarket or Kalshi was found**, and no study of reward-farming returns net of adverse selection.
- Kalshi's own market-maker and liquidity-incentive programme terms and the identity and results of institutional makers were not retrieved (search budget exhausted).
- A known open-source Polymarket market-making bot whose author reportedly warns it is no longer profitable could not be checked (GitHub access was restricted in this session); treat as unverified.
- Rebate rate for makers between $200K and $1M of monthly volume under the November 2026 tiers was not retrieved.

## 6. Systematic bias harvesting (favourite-longshot bias, structural NO, high-probability "bonds", time decay)

### Takeaway
The favourite-longshot bias is well documented on Kalshi (cheap contracts lose heavily; takers lose about 2.7% even at 50 cents) and present but category-dependent on Polymarket (sports is an exception and results flip with the weighting method). The popular retail expressions of it are weak: "always bet NO" confuses a 73–74% base rate with an edge, and near-certain "bonds" earn low single-digit returns that a 2026 paper shows are largely compensation for locked capital and settlement risk rather than mispricing.

### Cited Findings

**Favourite-longshot bias (FLB)**
- [A] Kalshi (Bürgi, Deng, Whelan; over 300,000 contracts): prices show a systematic FLB; low-price contracts win less often than needed to break even. As summarised, the average pre-fee return is about **−20%** and about **−22% after fees**; the bias holds for makers and takers but is stronger for takers; the authors report it appears to be shrinking over time. — [UCD working paper WP2025_19](https://www.ucd.ie/economics/t4media/WP2025_19.pdf); [GWU working paper 2026-001](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)
- [C] Kalshi (Becker; 72.1M trades): takers have negative excess returns at 80 of 99 price levels; the mispricing is most compressed at 50 cents (takers −2.65%, makers +2.66%). — [KuCoin reprint](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/analysis-reveals-systematic-wealth-transfer-in-prediction-markets)
- [A] Polymarket ("The Favorite-Longshot Bias in Prediction Markets: Evidence from Polymarket", arXiv 2609.12878, Sept 2026): longshot losses and favourite gains appear in crypto and politics; **sports is the main exception, where longshots earn positive returns**; averaging by parent event rather than by dollar, longshots earn about **+4.09%**, so the headline result depends on weighting. — [arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12878)
- [A] Polymarket weather markets ("Time-Varying Favorite-Longshot Bias in Polymarket Weather Markets", arXiv 2610.13593, Oct 2026): the favourite rule shows a 50.71% post-fee return with a wide confidence interval; the longshot rule is about break-even (−0.23% after fees, interval includes zero). The authors caution that these returns **exclude order-book depth, spreads and slippage and are not evidence of executable profit**. — [arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13593)
- [A] Cross-category calibration (arXiv 2602.19520, Feb 2026): the pattern differs by domain; sports shows no such gap, while weather and entertainment show the opposite pattern (prices too extreme, −0.09 each). A Princeton thesis on Polymarket calibration finds the bias present across domains and worse at longer horizons. — [arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19520); [Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/d5708372-5ea3-4252-b669-7f9cee07387c)

**"Nothing ever happens" (structural NO)**
- [B] Engineer Sterling Crispin's open-source bot buys NO on every non-sports Polymarket market and holds to resolution (it defaults to paper trading). Decrypt's framing is that the bot "has a point"; a widely shared figure is that 73.4% of Polymarket markets resolve NO. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/364381/polymarket-bot-bets-no-has-a-point)
- [B] Disconfirming: Protos reports the bot's creator "keeps losing money". — [Protos](https://protos.com/this-bot-only-bets-no-on-polymarket-and-its-creator-keeps-losing-money/)
- [D] A Substack analysis of about 161,638 Polymarket and Kalshi contracts puts the contract-level NO rate near 74% but shows this is an artefact of multi-outcome events (one outcome must resolve YES at the event level) and concludes that betting NO on the base rate "isn't a strategy, it's a meme". On thin longshots the bid/ask can be 2–4 cents wide, a large share of a 3–5 cent price. — [Delta Confidence Substack](https://deltaconfidence.substack.com/p/nothing-ever-happens)

**High-probability "bonds" and time decay**
- [B/C] Polymarket's own blog describes "bonds" as high-probability bets that rarely flip and notes bettors use them, for example around Fed decisions. — [AOL reprint of Polymarket blog](https://www.aol.com/articles/polymarket-shares-bettors-navigating-fed-185445519.html)
- [D] Tail-risk warning from an options-trading outlet: markets pricing 95–99% are "almost never wrong", but the tail shows up over enough trades. — [tastylive](https://www.tastylive.com/news-insights/fomc-polymarket-kalshi-when-rate-cut-priced-before-call)
- [D] Chinese-media listicle of "almost risk-free" bets cites an annualised return of about 5.5% on a high-probability NO, described as beating U.S. Treasuries, and frames these as low-risk arbitrage. — [Odaily: Ten almost risk-free absurd bets on Polymarket](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5208560)
- [A] **Key 2026 paper.** Gebele and Matthes, "When Certainty Is Not Worth It: Capital Lock-Up and Settlement Discounting in Prediction Markets" (arXiv 2605.31431, 29 May 2026): prices in collateralised, oracle-settled markets are discounted probabilities, reduced by a factor D(τ) ≤ 1 for delayed settlement, locked capital, outside opportunities, liquidity and residual platform risk. They construct an "annualized settlement wedge" (a required-return measure for locked capital). Their illustration: $1,000 of NO at the observed price earns roughly **4.2%, but only after locking capital for most of a year**. Adjusting prices for these discount curves removes about **48–88% of the near-certainty horizon gradient**; negRisk conversion compresses the discount, and yield-bearing collateral flattens the term structure. — [arXiv 2605.31431](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31431)
- [C/D] Fee note: Polymarket's effective taker fee is highest near 50% and approaches zero as the price nears 0 or 1, and Kalshi's fee formula is likewise proportional to price × (1 − price), so fees are small in absolute terms on near-certain contracts. — [ChainCatcher (zh)](https://www.chaincatcher.com/zh-tw/article/2253749); [DeucesCracked (Kalshi formula)](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/prediction-markets-vs-sportsbooks-volume-fees-data-2026)

### Inferences
- **The FLB is harvested by makers, not by a simple taker rule.** On Kalshi the edge shows up as maker excess return against taker flow; a taker who "sells longshots" by buying favourites still pays spread and the taker's side of the bias. The reliably profitable form is resting offers that sell cheap YES contracts to optimistic takers, which is market making (section 5).
- **Category selection matters and is unstable.** Polymarket sports longshots earned positive returns and weather shows the reverse pattern; a blanket FLB rule would have lost in those categories.
- **"Bonds" arithmetic.** At a 95-cent entry one loss erases about 19 wins; at 99 cents, about 99. Fees are small but not negligible against so thin a gain: by the cited formulas a taker at 95 cents pays roughly 0.2–0.3 cents per share, about 5–7% of the 5-cent upside (my arithmetic; Kalshi's rounding can make this worse on small orders). The measured 2–5% annualised pick-up is in the same range as the settlement discount documented by Gebele and Matthes, so most of the apparent yield is compensation for lock-up and oracle or platform risk, not free alpha. The residual tail is concentrated in ambiguous-resolution and disputed-oracle events (section 3).
- **LLM role: tail-risk triage.** The valuable language task is reading resolution rules, clarifications and dispute history to exclude markets where "near-certain" hides definitional risk, and to estimate time to settlement. Selecting contracts by price and computing returns is trivial code. (Buying near-certain outcomes after the event but before formal resolution is a news play covered by another researcher.)

### Gaps
- **No rigorous 2026 backtest of the "bonds" strategy with win rate, loss severity and returns net of spread was found**; the tail-risk description is qualitative.
- No independent net-of-cost return series for the always-NO bot; evidence is two conflicting news stories.
- Price-bucket tables for the Polymarket FLB paper and the meaning and interval of the 50.71% weather figure were not retrievable.
- Whether the Kalshi FLB continued to shrink through 2026 after fee and volume changes is unknown; the Kalshi papers use data through 2025.
- Polymarket "holding rewards" on long-dated markets (relevant to lock-up cost) could not be checked.

## 7. Other strategies discussed in 2026 (weather with forecast models, mention markets, airdrop / points farming)

### Takeaway
Weather and mention markets are the two niches most often cited as still having edge in 2026, precisely because they are thin: weather edge comes from numerical-model update timing and resolution-rule quirks, mention-market edge from reading rules and transcripts. Evidence for both is anecdotal. Airdrop farming distorted volume (about a quarter of Polymarket volume flagged as likely wash trading in a 2025 study) and is an incentive overlay rather than an arbitrage.

### Cited Findings

**Weather markets**
- [D] As of April 2026 Polymarket ran over 570 active weather markets; the most traded are daily high temperatures in major cities, with single markets clearing $300,000–$400,000 in 24-hour volume. The claimed edge is lag between model updates (GFS and ECMWF refresh about every six hours) and market prices, plus a resolution quirk: markets settle on Weather Underground data with temperatures truncated to whole degrees rather than rounded. — [dev.to: how Polymarket weather markets work](https://dev.to/cryptodeploy/how-polymarket-weather-markets-actually-work-50nb)
- [D] Named-trader anecdotes: "gopfan2" with a claimed $1.48M total profit, a bot at about $88,000, bots "quietly making $24,000", and a viral "weather bot built with Claude, $6K a month" blueprint. All unaudited; at least one is a paid-course pitch. — [dev.to: top weather traders](https://dev.to/fatherson/top-7-most-profitable-weather-traders-on-polymarket-2f9b); [dev.to: the $88K weather bot](https://dev.to/fatherson/the-88k-weather-prediction-bot-on-polymarket-lessons-from-real-data-3d47); [YouMind: Claude weather-bot blueprint](https://youmind.com/de-DE/landing/x-viral-articles/polymarket-weather-trading-bot-blueprint)
- [B] Mainstream coverage confirms the category boomed in spring 2026. — [Bloomberg, 10 Apr 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-10/what-are-weather-prediction-markets-and-do-they-work); [Insurance Journal, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/04/15/866011.htm)
- [A] Academic check: in Polymarket weather markets the longshot rule is about break-even after fees and the favourite-rule return has a wide interval, with no allowance for depth, spread or slippage. — [arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13593)
- [A/C] Cross-venue weather contracts reference different stations and can resolve differently. — [Jonas Gebele on X](https://x.com/j9ebele/status/2008938480617849193)

**Mention markets**
- [D] Mention contracts ask whether a word or phrase will be said during a defined event (NFL broadcasts, earnings calls, political speeches, FOMC press conferences, public figures' social media); they trade on both Kalshi and Polymarket. Wording and resolution rules determine what counts; in one disputed Sanders market Kalshi's rules prioritised video from a designated list of outlets, falling back to transcripts. — [Bonus.com guide](https://www.bonus.com/prediction-markets/trading-guide/mention-markets/); [DeFi Rate: mention markets](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/mention-markets/); [OddsShopper](https://www.oddsshopper.com/articles/prediction-markets/kalshi-politics-markets-for-sports-bettors)
- [D] The stated edge is parsing speech patterns, reading prepared remarks released ahead of time and understanding event context; one opportunity survey calls it "very human-judgment dependent" and notes that thin markets (weather, economics, mentions) are where edges exist but large capital cannot be deployed. — [Prediction market opportunity map, 18 Mar 2026](https://blakethom8.github.io/mobile-reports/2026-03-18_prediction-market-opportunity-map.html)
- [B] NPR covered the mention-market and Super Bowl "alpha" culture in January–February 2026. — [NPR, 17 Feb 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/17/nx-s1-5716224/kalshi-polymarket-super-bowl-alpha); [NPR glossary, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/17/nx-s1-5672615/kalshi-polymarket-prediction-market-boom-traders-slang-glossary)

**Airdrop / points farming**
- [B] A Columbia study (SSRN, Nov 2025, not peer-reviewed) estimated about **25% of Polymarket volume may be wash trading**, peaking near 60% in December 2024, falling to about 5% in May 2025 and rising to about 20% in early October 2025; by category 45% of sports volume, 17% of election, 12% of politics and 3% of crypto volume was flagged. The authors link it to airdrop expectations but say the motive is not confirmed. **Possibly superseded** for 2026. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research/)
- [B] Farmers became "more sophisticated" ahead of the expected token: traders interviewed target volume, profitability, liquidity provision and number of markets traded as likely allocation criteria rather than obvious wash trades. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/344895/polymarket-airdrop-farmers-become-more-sophisticated-token-looms)
- [A] Polymarket now lists "combos" (parlays) with their own fee coefficient and no maker rebates, a new instrument whose pricing relative to its legs is a potential structural-consistency check. — [Polymarket docs: November 2026 changes](https://docs.polymarket.com/programs/fee-and-rebate-changes-november-2026)

### Inferences
- **Weather is a numerical-model trade.** The inputs are ensemble forecasts and station data; an LLM does not forecast temperature. Its realistic roles are building and maintaining the pipeline and reading resolution rules (station, truncation, time zone). The "built with Claude" stories are about code generation, not about the model supplying the edge.
- **Mention markets are the most naturally LLM-shaped niche**: the work is text (rules, past transcripts, prepared remarks) and base rates of phrase usage. But capacity is small, and resolution disputes about what counts as a "mention" are the main loss mode.
- **Farming changes the economics of other strategies.** If hedged or low-edge volume is rewarded with tokens, rebates or points, traders will accept negative trading P&L, which compresses spreads and visible arbitrage for everyone else. With taker fees now charged on most categories, wash-style farming has a direct cost it did not have in 2025.
- **Competition.** These niches have low capacity (hundreds of thousands of dollars of daily volume per market at best), so they suit small accounts and are unattractive to large firms, which is why they are the ones retail guides promote.

### Gaps
- **No verified P&L for any weather or mention-market trader**; no academic study of mention markets was found.
- Whether Polymarket's token and airdrop had launched by November 2026, and on what criteria, was not established. Points programmes on newer venues (Opinion, Limitless, Predict.fun) and hedged cross-venue farming returned no results before the search budget ran out.
- No 2026 update of the wash-trading share under the fee regime.
- No measurement of combo/parlay pricing versus legs.

## 8. Cross-strategy quantification, competition trend, and what the LLM actually does

### Takeaway
Across the taxonomy, measured edges in 2026 are a few cents or less and last seconds wherever detection is mechanical, and they survive mainly where discovery requires reading text or where capital and legal access are scarce. The LLM is a genuine enabler in exactly two places — extracting logical relations between markets and comparing resolution rules across markets and venues — a useful risk filter in two more (bonds, market making), and irrelevant to the speed-bound strategies that hold most of the documented profit.

### Cited Findings
- [A] Largest verified realized-arbitrage figure: about $40M on Polymarket, April 2024 – April 2025, overwhelmingly within-market. — [arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)

**What the LLM does versus what is engineering and speed**
- **LLM as genuine enabler (semantic work that cannot be done by arithmetic):** (1) extracting implication, exclusion and exhaustiveness relations from titles and resolution criteria; (2) matching markets across venues and, more importantly, diffing their resolution rules; (3) triaging near-certain or quoted markets for ambiguity and dispute risk; (4) reading transcripts and rules in mention-style markets.
- **Where LLM output must not be trusted blind:** the 2025 funnel from 1,576 candidate pairs to 13 valid ones, and the need for human validation in the cross-platform dataset, both indicate high false-positive rates. A false positive here does not produce a missed trade; it produces an unhedged position.
- **Pure engineering and speed (LLM irrelevant in the loop):** sum-to-one scanning, NegRisk conversion paths, latency trading against spot, quoting and inventory control, fee-tier optimisation, cross-venue collateral management.
- **Overall pattern.** Profit documented at scale (about $40M of arbitrage in 2024–25, believed to be mostly within-market; $131M to 823 bots in 2025–26 across all strategies) sits in the speed-and-capital strategies. The strategies where an LLM helps are the ones with the smallest measured capacity. An LLM-centred approach is therefore best understood as a way to find a small number of semantically complex, slow-moving mispricings, not as a way to compete for the mechanical ones.

### Gaps
- **No source quantifies the incremental profit attributable to LLM-based detection in live trading**; the case for the LLM rests on two research pipelines (2025 and October 2026) and on inference.
- Consistent year-on-year measurement of opportunity counts and durations does not exist; the "decay" narrative is assembled from studies with different scopes.
- Typical capital requirements and realized returns for combinatorial and cross-platform strategies in 2026 are not documented in any verified source found.
- Several planned checks did not run because the session's web-search budget was exhausted and page fetching was blocked: trading-firm activity in prediction markets (Bloomberg / WSJ / FT), Chinese deep dives from PANews / Foresight News / BlockBeats on tail-end and market-making strategies, exploit incidents affecting market-making bots, LLM market-matching evaluations, and the full text of every arXiv paper cited above. All arXiv-sourced numbers should be re-checked against the PDFs before publication.
