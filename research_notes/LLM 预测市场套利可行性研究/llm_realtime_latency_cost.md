# Speed and cost of LLM real-time decision pipelines, and direct evidence on whether LLM agents are fast enough to trade news on prediction markets (state of the world: 26 November 2026)

Scope: speed and cost only. This file closes section 3 of `event_driven_fast_reaction.md`, which could not verify any latency benchmark. Facts already established there (first buy about 7 s and price above 90% at about 31 s after a press release; quote-driven price discovery; 150 ms taker hold on Polymarket crypto markets and about 1-3 s on live sports; a millisecond political-post feed at up to $100,000 a month; $0.05-$0.60 per researched decision; Lopez-Lira and Tang's 34 bps a day) are used as inputs and were not re-researched.

## 1. Current inference latency of production LLMs (time to first token, output speed) and the practical floor for a short classification call with structured output

### Takeaway
The best measured floor found is 0.2-0.4 s median (0.25-0.95 s at the 95th percentile) for a typed classification call of about 1,000-1,600 input tokens sent through a public router in October 2026; general small chat models on mainstream APIs sit around 0.4-1.2 s for a short structured answer once thinking is switched off (my estimate from tracker figures, not a measurement), and reasoning-mode calls take from about 2 s at the lowest effort to more than a minute at the highest before the first answer token. Independent tracker figures are measured on a 10,000-token prompt and include thinking time, so they overstate the delay of a short headline call and must not be read as the floor.

### Inferences
- **[CALC] Practical floor, short classification with structured output, network included.** (a) Typed decision endpoint: 184-408 ms median and 245-946 ms at p95 for the thirteen fastest hosted models in the October 2026 measurements above, with ten of the thirteen under 550 ms at p95. (b) Small general chat model with thinking off: time to first token of roughly 0.35-0.75 s on the tracker (10,000-token prompt where stated), plus 20-60 output tokens at 130-240 tokens/s (0.1-0.5 s), giving about 0.4-1.2 s; the fact that `gpt-6-luna-decisions` answered in 203 ms median on about 1,100 tokens suggests the lower half of that range is reachable on short prompts. (c) Specialised hardware: at 470-1,850 tokens/s a 60-token answer takes 0.03-0.13 s, so total time is almost entirely time to first token; the only sub-200 ms figures are unverified, so a defensible range is 0.1-0.4 s. (d) Reasoning mode: about 1.6-2.8 s to the first answer token at the lowest effort setting of a small model (no low-effort figure was found for frontier models), 7-30 s at high settings and 68-110 s at maximum; a frontier model at 80-97 tokens/s adds 0.6-0.75 s for 60 output tokens. (e) Multi-step research loops: minutes.
- Default-on thinking is the main reason tracker figures for late-2026 "small" models look slow. A pipeline must set the lowest effort or disable thinking explicitly, otherwise a Haiku-class call can take tens of seconds.
- The structured-output schema must be compiled before the event. A cold schema costs seconds to a minute on the first call and the compiled grammar expires after about a day without use, so an event-driven bot needs a scheduled warm-up call.
- Network placement adds a term nobody published: LLM endpoints and the trading venue are not in the same place, so either the model call or the order leg crosses a long-haul link (one practitioner measured about 88 ms from US-East to the Polymarket order API; see question 6).

### Gaps
- No primary-source p50/p95 for short prompts (a few hundred tokens) on mainstream small chat models was found. Artificial Analysis publishes only the 10,000-token workload, and its live pages could not be opened; every tracker number above comes from search summaries that disagree across snapshots.
- No measured steady-state overhead of schema-constrained output (per request, after warm-up) from any provider.
- No independent time to first token for small non-reasoning models on Groq, Cerebras or SambaNova in late 2026; the registry snapshot in question 2 suggests Groq's hosted catalogue has shrunk to gpt-oss and Qwen models, so older Llama 3.1 8B figures may be superseded.
- No latency data for xAI's current small or fast tier; sources conflict on whether a fast variant of Grok 4.7 is served on the public API.
- Time-to-first-token figures for Claude Opus 5.5, Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra were not extracted.
- Regional variation, rate-limit queueing under news bursts and tail latency beyond p95 are unmeasured.

## 3. Local or self-hosted small models for sub-100 ms text classification

### Takeaway
Sub-100 ms classification is supported by published timings only for encoder-class models on a data-centre GPU (about 7-36 ms per item) or for very small encoders on CPU; I found no verified 2026 practitioner report of a sub-100 ms news classifier in a prediction-market bot, and the one measured local run in a trading study took about 0.5-0.9 s per item on a laptop-class GPU.

### Cited Findings
- **[V-paper] (search summary)** An October 2026 paper on compact encoders for closed-set decisions reports DeBERTa-v3-large zero-shot classification (one inference pass per option) at a median of 36.1 ms on an A100 GPU and 728 ms on a 16-thread CPU, about 54 times the CPU median of its smallest model. — [arXiv 2610.30970](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30970)
- **[V-paper] (search summary)** A 2026 study of transformer inference optimisation reports BERT-base at batch size 1 and sequence length 128: 45.2 ms on CPU (PyTorch, FP32), 8.4 ms on GPU (PyTorch, FP32) and 6.9 ms on GPU with ONNX Runtime. — [arXiv 2603.28708](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.28708)
- **[V-paper] (search summary)** A 2026 comparison finds fine-tuned BERT-family encoders reach competitive or better classification performance "at one to two orders of magnitude lower cost and latency" than zero-shot or few-shot LLM prompting. — [arXiv 2602.06370](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06370)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** In the trading study above, the local decision model (Laya, built on ModernBERT-large, run sequentially on Apple MPS on the same news states that billed about 1,000-1,600 tokens on hosted models) took 536 / 758 ms (p50 / p95) in the main run and about 900 / 1,190 ms when the GPU was shared with training. — [NewsAction NA-12 REPORT](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-12/REPORT.md); [NA-06 REPORT](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-06/REPORT.md)
- **[U] (search summary)** Model cards: FinSense, a ModernBERT-base (149 million parameters) sentiment classifier for headlines and wires, "runs happily on CPU" with no latency figure; Laya-Finance, a fine-tuned ModernBERT-large, is limited to English passages of a few hundred words. — [FinSense](https://huggingface.co/AnkitAI/FinSense-ModernBERT-Financial-News-Sentiment); [Laya-Finance](https://huggingface.co/hravi/laya-finance)
- **[U] (search summary)** A hosting company's benchmark of 34 CPU-only small-language-model configurations names LFM2.5-350M (4-bit) as the latency leader for routing, classification and extraction; the summary gave no millisecond figure. A third-party site claims 50-200 ms for Gemma 3 270M on mobile and a 25 ms routing decision without methodology; informal tests found Gemma 3 270M and Qwen3 0.6B unreliable on structured multi-step tasks. — [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/blog/slm-cpu-benchmark/); [LocalAIMaster](https://localaimaster.com/models/gemma-3-270m)
- **[V-paper] (search summary)** Framework overhead dominates at batch size 1 and small inputs, so latency stops falling with input size. — [The Framework Tax, arXiv 2302.06117](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06117)

### Inferences
- **[CALC]** The smallest encoder in the October 2026 paper runs at roughly 13-14 ms on CPU (728 ms divided by 54). A headline-length input on a fine-tuned encoder therefore fits in 10-50 ms on one CPU box and under 10 ms on a GPU, with no network leg.
- Local inference removes the 0.2-0.4 s API round trip but is not automatically fast: a large encoder with a long input on consumer hardware was slower (0.5-0.9 s) than the hosted decision models (0.2-0.3 s).
- A local encoder answers only fixed questions it was trained for (relevant or not, direction, entity). Mapping a headline to one of thousands of changing markets still needs retrieval or a generative model, so the realistic design is a local filter in front of a hosted model, not a replacement.

### Gaps
- No practitioner write-up in 2026 with measured p50/p95 for a self-hosted news classifier used in prediction-market trading was found; the two searches on this returned academic timings and marketing.
- No verified latency for small generative models (Gemma 3 270M, Qwen3 0.6B, LFM2.5-350M) under llama.cpp or vLLM on a classification prompt.
- No ModernBERT-specific batch-size-1 latency figure was found.

## 4. Direct studies of LLM agents trading on news, including arXiv 2611.03985 and arXiv 2605.19337, and seconds-level response of Polymarket or Kalshi to scheduled macro releases

### Takeaway
Neither named paper is a prediction-market study: arXiv 2611.03985 is a pre-registered test on S&P 500 stocks showing that fast language models (0.2-2.3 s per reading) saw news that was already priced by the time a trade was possible, with no strategy surviving costs, and arXiv 2605.19337 is an audit of LLM trading-agent papers. No study measuring LLM-agent reaction time against prediction-market price moves, and no seconds-level study of Polymarket or Kalshi around FOMC, CPI or jobs releases, was found.

### Cited Findings

"Thinking Fast, Trading Late: System One Decision Models vs the Market" (arXiv 2611.03985)
- **[V-paper] (read in full: replication README, stage reports and the 40-page PDF)** Authors: Amir Dehkordi, Alireza Kargarzadeh, Nariman Khaledian, Navid Parvini, Sid Ghatak, Arman Khaledian; dated November 2026 (arXiv submission 2 November 2026 per search summary). Asset class: S&P 500 stocks with point-in-time membership, July 2024 to July 2026. It is not about prediction markets. — [NewsAction README](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction); [arXiv 2611.03985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03985)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** Design: for each company and session the news state is built as of 09:15 ET with company identity masked; models give a pre-open reading at 09:15 and a post-reaction reading at 10:00 and after the close. The analysis plan was frozen by hash on 28 October 2026 and the January-July 2026 test period unblinded once. Instruments: a fine-tuned open-weights decision model (Laya-Fin, on ModernBERT-large), three commercial decision models chosen by tournament (d1-ultra, span-02, drex-v2.0), and baselines including a generative LLM with an April 2024 knowledge cutoff. — [NewsAction README](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** Result 1: the commercial models' readings correlate with the overnight move from previous close to open (daily information coefficient 0.074-0.077) but not with the move after the open (about zero, -0.001 to 0.002). "The news is priced by the time a trader can act on it at the open." Timing matters: d1-ultra's overnight coefficient is 0.115 when the latest article arrives between 08:00 and 09:15, 0.061 for 04:00-08:00 and not significant before 04:00. — [paper PDF](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-16/paper.pdf)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** Result 2: the fine-tuned model's coefficient fell from 0.055 and 0.065 (t = 3.6) on two validation quarters to 0.020 (t = 1.7) on the blinded test; d1-ultra's fell from 0.097 to 0.039; the generative LLM baseline fell from 0.052 to 0.001. — [paper PDF](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-16/paper.pdf)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** Profitability: costs were charged at 5, 10 and 20 basis points per side. "No pre-registered strategy is worth trading after costs." Net of 10 basis points every main book loses; the best gross Sharpe ratio (1.88) breaks even at 8.3 basis points per side; one-day books turn over about 4.4 times capital a day; the largest deflated-Sharpe probability across 545 logged trials is 0.024. In-sample conditions with Sharpe ratios up to 6.8 failed on the test. — [paper PDF](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-16/paper.pdf)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** Speed and cost of the models used: 288 ms median for d1-ultra, 680 ms for drex-v2.0, 2,262 ms for span-02, at $0.028-$0.064 per 1,000 decisions. — [NewsAction NA-12 REPORT](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-12/REPORT.md)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** The models could not tell over-reaction from under-reaction, and commercial models identified the masked company in 62-74% of cases against a 28% baseline ("masking hides names, not companies"), so backtests of language models on historical news mix reading ability with memory. Same-day news explained only 0.25-0.82% of a stock's own daily variance out of sample. A forward test on news published from 22 October 2026 was pre-registered on 29 October 2026 and has not been run. — [NewsAction README](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction)
- **[V-paper] (read in full)** The paper restates that Lopez-Lira and Tang's tradable open-to-close drift "turns unprofitable at 20 basis points per round trip". — [paper PDF](https://github.com/AmirDehkordi/NewsAction/blob/main/artifacts/NA-16/paper.pdf)

It is an audit-oriented evidence map of 77 studies of LLM trading agents: only 2 of 19 studies in its primary subset report a time-consistent data split and 1 of 19 an explicit transaction-cost model. ### Inferences
- The premise that these two arXiv papers measured LLM agents against prediction-market prices is wrong and should be corrected in the report. A practitioner blog cited in the earlier round attributed a "30-second to 5-minute" unadjusted window to arXiv 2605.19337; nothing visible in that paper supports it, so the claim should be treated as unsourced.
- The survey (PDF dated 10 October 2026) said no trading evaluation of typed decision models had been published; the NewsAction paper, dated three weeks later, is one, for equities, and its answer is negative.
- The equity result still transfers as a warning. Reading speed was not the problem there (0.2-0.7 s per reading for two of the three hosted models); the tradable moment came after the market had already moved, and a validated signal shrank by two thirds out of sample. Prediction markets trade continuously, so there is no opening auction to wait for, but the seconds-level evidence from the earlier round shows the same ordering: other participants act first.
- Published live LLM agents on prediction markets work at a cadence of one decision per 15-45 minutes and lost money. That cadence is 100 to 400 times slower than the 7-second first trade documented earlier; they are not news-reaction systems at all.

### Gaps
- No study, positive or negative, that timestamps an LLM agent's order against the price path of a prediction market after a news item.
- No seconds-level or minute-level event study of Polymarket or Kalshi around the 2:00 p.m. FOMC statement or the 8:30 a.m. CPI and jobs releases; two searches found only daily or intraday-forecast work. This would need Kalshi trade data or Polymarket on-chain fills.

## 7. End-to-end latency and cost budget by pipeline stage

### Takeaway
On a wire-grade feed a rule-based pipeline can act in tens to a few hundred milliseconds and a small fast model in roughly 0.25-1.4 s, both inside the 7-second first-trade interval, while high-effort reasoning calls (7-110 s) and research loops (minutes) arrive after most of the move; on self-serve public feeds the 4-7 s feed tail puts every design at or behind the first trade. Money goes to the feed (thousands to $100,000 a month) rather than the model (dollars a day). These totals are sums of separately sourced stages, not a measured pipeline.

### Inferences
- **[CALC] End-to-end totals** (sum of stages 1-5; venue delay added where it applies):

| Feed | Rule-based | Small fast model | Frontier, single call | Frontier, research loop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional wire | about 0.05-0.25 s | about 0.25-1.4 s | about 2-110 s | 4-45 minutes |
| Retail low-latency news API (0.1-0.5 s) | about 0.15-0.7 s | about 0.3-1.9 s | about 2-110 s | 4-45 minutes |
| X self-serve stream (tail 4-7 s) | up to about 4-7 s | up to about 4.2-8.4 s | about 6-118 s | 4-45 minutes |
| Public broadcast with transcription | about 19-63 s | about 19-64 s | about 21-173 s | 4-45 minutes |

- **[CALC] Position against the 7 s and 31 s marks.** A small fast model on a wire-grade or sub-second feed acts before the first documented buy, but so does any rule-based competitor, earlier by roughly 0.2-1.2 s. On X's self-serve stream the small-model pipeline is on time only when the stream delivers well inside its 99th-percentile figure. A low-effort reasoning call (about 2-3 s on a small model; no figure was found for frontier models) fits before the 31 s mark on a fast feed; high-effort calls (7-110 s) mostly do not. Research loops miss the event entirely and belong to trades with horizons of hours or days.
- **[CALC] Monthly cost shape** for a monitor reading 50,000 items a day (1.5 million a month): model calls about $45-$270 a month on a decision model or a Haiku-class model, or about $10,800 a month on Opus 5.5 before thinking tokens; the same 1.5 million items read through X's self-serve API cost $7,500 a month (the cap is 3 million posts, $15,000); enterprise wires are unpublished and a specialist feed runs up to $100,000 a month. The feed costs roughly 30 to 170 times the small-model bill on X self-serve and several hundred to about 2,000 times on a $100,000 feed.
- The value of the model is therefore confined to cases where a rule cannot be written in advance (unfamiliar wording, mapping to the right market, reading resolution criteria), and only if the answer is still worth acting on 0.2-1.2 s after a rule-based system would have acted. Whether that residual exists in prediction markets is unmeasured (question 4).
- A workable fast design implied by the numbers: persistent connections, a pre-compiled schema refreshed daily, thinking disabled, a typed-decision or small non-reasoning model, a local encoder as a first filter, and the order process located near the venue. None of this removes the feed disadvantage.

### Gaps
- No published end-to-end benchmark (item arrival to order acknowledgement) for any LLM pipeline on a prediction market; the totals above are sums of separately sourced stages, not a measurement, and stage delays measured at different places and times may not add linearly.
- Rule-based parsing and lookup times are assumptions; no benchmark was found.
- The median (not tail) delay of X's streams, the true order-acknowledgement latency at Polymarket and Kalshi, and any venue delay on non-crypto, non-sports markets as of late November 2026 are unconfirmed.
- No evidence on queueing and rate limits when many bots call the same model endpoint during a major news burst.
