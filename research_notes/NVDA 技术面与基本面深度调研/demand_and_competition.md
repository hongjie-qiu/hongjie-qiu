## 1. Demand: customer capital spending, Nvidia's order visibility and signs of digestion

### Takeaway
Reported demand is still accelerating: FQ3 FY2027 Data Center revenue was $107.6B (+110% YoY), FQ4 revenue is guided to $130B ±3% with no China data-center compute, the FY2028 growth outlook was raised to about 75% from 70%, and every large cloud says 2027 capex will exceed 2026 (roughly $720–740B for the big four in 2026 on company guidance; 2027 consensus above $1T). The bearish evidence concerns the edges and the quality of that demand rather than the core: Microsoft is pulling back from neocloud deals and cancelled a roughly $3B Nscale project (Nov 2026), Nebius cut 2026 revenue guidance on site delays, CoreWeave says 2027 capex would be flat on committed backlog alone, power and construction delays are pushing deliveries into 2027, and a rising share of demand rests on a few AI labs (about 15% of Nvidia's Data Center revenue) and debt-funded buyers.

## 3. Supply chain

### Takeaway
The binding constraints for 2027 are high-bandwidth memory and power, not Nvidia's own packaging allocation: all three HBM makers say 2027 output is largely sold, HBM4 contract prices are forecast to roughly double, and turbines, transformers and grid connections carry multi-year lead times. ### Inferences
- The bottleneck has moved from CoWoS to HBM4 and to site power. CoWoS estimates for end-2027 (170k–250k wafers per month) are roughly 1.6–2.4x the end-2026 estimate of 105k (computed), while HBM capacity relief is dated 2028–2029.
- Scarcity cuts both ways for Nvidia: it caps Rubin volume and raises unit cost (HBM4 +121%, CoWoS +20% as reported), but Nvidia's allocation share and its reported price advantage of about 11–20% on HBM4 versus Broadcom and AMD (computed from $32 / $36 / $40) mean rivals are constrained at least as much.
- Power, not chips, is now the stated cause of the cancellations and guide cuts found in section 1. This creates a risk of GPUs shipped ahead of usable sites; no direct evidence of idle Nvidia inventory at customers was found.
- Larger multi-year supply commitments (whatever the exact figure) shift risk onto Nvidia's balance sheet if 2028 demand disappoints.
- US manufacturing remains partial: wafers and system assembly are onshore, but advanced packaging for those wafers was still in Taiwan in the latest report found.

## 4. Competition and market share

### Takeaway
Nvidia still takes roughly three-quarters or more of AI-accelerator revenue in 2026 (estimates range from about 75% to 86% depending on scope), and rivals' schedules keep slipping (AMD's full-rack ramp to Q2 2027, Intel's inference GPU possibly to 2027). But the alternatives are now large and funded: Broadcom guides AI revenue to double to $115B in FY2027 and $230B in FY2028, Google's external TPU sales are estimated at $62.5–99B for 2027, AMD has 6 GW agreements with both OpenAI and Meta plus Anthropic, JPMorgan expects custom chips to out-ship GPUs in units in 2027, and in China Nvidia's unit share fell to 34% in H1 2026 with Huawei at 28%.
