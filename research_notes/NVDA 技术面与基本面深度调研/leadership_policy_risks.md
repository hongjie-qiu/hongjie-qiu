## 1. Leadership and governance: Jensen Huang's resignation and the succession (highest priority)

### Takeaway
The resignation is real and confirmed by primary documents: Nvidia's Form 8-K and press release "NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition" say the board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as President, CEO **and director** on November 11, 2026, effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress as **interim** CEO (keeping the CFO job), Harvey C. Jones as the first-ever board chair, and a search for a permanent CEO under way. The company gave no reason; every major outlet ties the exit to Huang's admitted extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a former executive of Singapore-based Nvidia customer Megaspeed whom US intelligence reportedly suspects of working for China — a matter now under investigation by the board (Wachtell Lipton), the FBI/DOJ, the SEC (reported) and Congress (Huang testifies Dec 1). No reputable source contradicts the resignation; sources differ only on the date (Nov 11 vs. Nov 12), on "resigned" vs. "ousted", on how many relationships were involved, and on whether the espionage link is proven.

## 2. Export controls and China

### Takeaway
Export-control risk rose sharply in the week after the resignation: on Nov 15–16, 2026 BIS issued an interim final rule requiring licences for advanced AI chips shipped to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil and for chips going to data centers that supply significant computing power to China-headquartered firms (the "remote access" loophole), and on Nov 17 Nvidia said it has stopped shipping licensed Hopper-generation chips to China and excluded China from guidance. Direct China data-center revenue was already small (about $2.4B, roughly 2% of Q3 FY2027 revenue), so the larger exposure is now indirect demand routed through Southeast Asia — Jefferies' estimate, as relayed by one source, is $44–62B of next-year data-center revenue — plus the possibility that Congress writes limits into law.

### Inferences
- The credit curve now ranks the chain clearly: Nvidia about 139 bp, Oracle about 298 bp, CoreWeave above 1,100 bp. Stress is concentrated in leveraged intermediaries that buy Nvidia systems with borrowed money against a handful of AI-lab contracts; it has not reached the hyperscalers, which are Nvidia's largest customers. The key transmission channel to Nvidia is a slowdown in neocloud and OpenAI-related orders, plus mark-downs on Nvidia's $99B equity book and eventual calls on its guarantees.
- Nvidia's financial commitments (about $99B invested, $25B committed, about $108.5B guaranteed ≈ $230B+, my sum; IFR's $530B figure evidently uses a wider definition) are of the same order as two quarters of revenue. They are manageable against cash generation but make Nvidia's results more correlated with its customers' solvency than a pure supplier's would be. The Q3 10-Q, when filed, is the place to check for changes.
- Alphabet's pattern (–$9.1B quarterly FCF, zero buybacks, $59B of equity and $80.8B of debt in nine months) shows that even the strongest buyer is funding 2026 capex externally. 2027 capex guidance from the hyperscalers (late January – early February 2027) is therefore the single most important demand-side datapoint for Nvidia, and it now depends on bond-market access as well as on business cases.
- CoreWeave's decision not to issue parent-level unsecured debt through 2027 implies slower self-funded growth at a leading specialist-cloud customer unless asset-backed (GPU-collateralised) financing stays open — which in turn depends on the depreciation debate being resolved in favour of longer lives.
- The leadership change interacts with financing: several counterparties relied on Huang personally in structuring guarantees and investments (per commentary), and an interim CEO who is also the CFO may be more conservative about new backstops — good for risk, potentially negative for near-term demand that depended on vendor support.

## 6. Power, energy and data-center permitting constraints

### Takeaway
Power and local permitting have become a binding constraint on deploying Nvidia systems in the US: trackers count 75 projects (about $130B) blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 alone, press investigations find roughly half of 2026's planned US projects delayed or cancelled, grid-connection waits run four to seven years in the main hubs, and moratoria are spreading (state-level in New York, a reported pause in Texas, a federal bill). Most numbers come from trackers and secondary sites and should be treated as indicative.

### Cited Findings
*Latest developments found: FERC show-cause orders, Jun 18, 2026; FT project count, Oct 18, 2026; Goldman comment on Malaysian capacity, Nov 18, 2026. Tracker and moratorium items are undated or from secondary pages.*
- **Blocked and delayed projects:** at least 75 US data-center projects worth about $130B were blocked or delayed in Q1 2026, a record quarter (tracker figure). — [Good Jobs First](https://goodjobsfirst.org/data-center-moratorium-bills-are-spreading-in-2026/) [T] (attribution approximate); [Network World](https://www.networkworld.com/article/4200222/data-center-energy-constraints-and-moratoriums-are-mounting-expect-to-see-stalled-ai-projects.html) [T]

- **Stranded power abroad:** after the new US export rule, Malaysian data centers built for Chinese AI-training customers "could be left with idle power" (Goldman Sachs via CNBC). — [CNBC, Nov 18, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-us-ai-chip-export-controls-data-centers-.html) [M]

### Inferences
- With Nvidia still supply-constrained, power and permitting delays have so far shifted where and when systems are installed rather than reduced shipments; the risk is that chips are delivered and financed faster than sites are energised, which lengthens customers' cash-conversion cycles and feeds the credit stress in section 5.
- Delays are not all the same thing: some reflect local opposition and grid queues (a timing issue), others — SoftBank's turbine cancellation, the paused Malaysian loan — reflect capital discipline or policy shocks (a demand issue). The second kind is the more worrying for Nvidia.
- State moratoria and the federal bill are a 2027 political risk: the spy scandal plus rising power bills give data-center critics more leverage in the new Congress.
