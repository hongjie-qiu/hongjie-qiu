## 7. The "peak earnings" debate

### Takeaway
The market is pricing Nvidia like a cyclical at its peak: three data providers put its forward P/E at the lowest since 2011–2013, and the whole chip sector's forward multiple has fallen from about 33x to about 14–15x in a year even though analysts expect semiconductor earnings to rise about 65% in 2027. The dot-com comparison most often made (Cisco) does not fit on valuation — Cisco peaked at about 131x forward earnings in March 2000, Nvidia is at about 10x — so the live argument is the memory-stock pattern (lowest P/E at the top of the cycle; Micron is at 6.5–8.3x forward today); bulls answer that guidance was just raised and estimates are still rising, bears that a low multiple on record margins is exactly what a peak looks like.

### Inferences
- **What the price implies (derived).** At the S&P 500's forward multiple of about 18x, $150.96 capitalises sustainable EPS of about **$8.4** (150.96 / 18) — almost exactly trailing non-GAAP EPS ($8.36), 47% below FY2028 consensus ($15.91) and 61% below FY2029 consensus ($21.34). Put differently, the market price is consistent with earnings never rising above today's trailing level, or rising as guided and then roughly halving. Sell-side consensus shows neither: its lowest FY2029 EPS estimate ($15.14) is still 81% above trailing EPS.
- **The gap between market and analysts is therefore unusually wide.** Consensus models deceleration with no down year through FY2030; the multiple (5.8–7.1x FY2029–FY2030 EPS) says investors assign little credibility to those years. The out-year estimates are also thinly covered (four to eight analysts), which weakens them as evidence.
- **Cisco is the wrong template for valuation, a relevant one for mechanism.** Cisco at its peak was about 13x more expensive on forward earnings than Nvidia is now (131x versus ≈ 10x). What the two share is dependence on customers whose spending relies on external financing. The memory-cycle template (single-digit P/E at peak earnings, as Micron shows today) fits the multiple better; whether it fits the business depends on whether Nvidia's 71–72% gross margin and 14.7%-a-quarter implied growth path (section 3) hold.
- **Three things in the 17 Nov report bear directly on the debate (derived, sections 2–3):** (1) week-adjusted sequential growth guided for Q4 is only about 4%; (2) the FY2028 guide nevertheless requires roughly 15% sequential growth each quarter; (3) free cash flow (≈ $145 bn trailing) is running at about 65% of GAAP net income. Bulls can cite (2) as management confidence; bears can cite (1) and (3) as early signs of a plateau and of working-capital or prepayment strain.
- **Two different discounts are mixed in the current multiple.** Before the Huang news the stock was already at about 17x forward earnings at its record high (cycle scepticism); the further fall to about 10x came in the week of his exit (governance discount). Analysts who cut targets cite the second; the peak-earnings argument is about the first. A resolution of the CEO question would address only part of the gap.
